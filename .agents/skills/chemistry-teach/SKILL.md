# Teach chemistry through conserved quantities and evidence

Read [homework-teach](../homework-teach/SKILL.md), its [lesson workflow](../homework-teach/references/lesson-workflow.md), and [science guidance](../homework-teach/references/science.md). They own privacy, routing, lesson structure, review, and delivery. This skill supplies chemistry decisions; it does not authorize experiments, lab conclusions, integration setup, or message sending. Route the new lesson as **Chemistry**; preserve legacy Science entries and existing URLs.

## Choose a coherent reasoning path

Inspect every supplied page visually, including notation, charge marks, arrows, units, and any handwritten work. Treat handwriting as uncertain learner reasoning, never as an answer key. Privately map the underlying demands to prerequisites, an original worked case, a transfer check, and any linked continuation. Do not publish that map or a source-question concordance.

Choose the smallest path that prepares the learner to act. Formula reading, electron accounting, spatial charge effects, and particle interactions are distinct tasks; a lesson about one does not silently certify the others. A lesson series may divide them, but the linked set must address the required reasoning. Say what information the learner still needs, such as a course convention, supplied geometry, or observed data.

Review separation at the level of the requested response. A generic definition, rank order, elemental list, or standard diagram can complete assigned work just as directly as a numerical answer. If so, teach its prerequisites or an independently different case and leave the final naming or inference to the learner. Change the reasoning context or representation as well as numbers: a token-counting model can introduce notation, but it must eventually connect to chemistry. A different atom symbol alone does not make a worked structure original.

## Build from a visible model to a method

Select the relevant branch rather than forcing every chemistry lesson through bonding:

- **Composition and quantities:** state what one counted unit is. Separate amount from composition, subscripts from coefficients, atoms from ions and molecules, and a molecular formula from an extended-solid ratio. Carry units through conversions; check dimensional consistency and sensible scale. For equation work, audit each element and total charge on both sides without balancing the assigned equation.
- **Electron structure and bonding:** distinguish a whole-diagram electron budget from a local shell check. When independent structure drawing is required, teach valence lookup, charge adjustment, a plausible skeleton, electron placement, shell audit, and revision on a genuinely new complete structure. An arithmetic ledger alone does not teach drawing. Explain why a shared pair counts once in the total and around both bonded atoms locally. State the range of any octet rule; unresolved formal charges, resonance, radicals, or expanded shells need an appropriate continuation rather than a forced simple answer.
- **Polarity and shape:** distinguish pair count, uneven sharing in one bond, and the spatial combination of effects in a whole particle. Electronegativity differences use one scale and are unitless; introductory cutoffs are approximate conventions. Obtain geometry from evidence or a stated shape model, not the placement of symbols on the page. If using arrows, verify directions, magnitudes, cancellation, and whether the illustration represents real chemistry or only vector addition.
- **Interactions, energy, and properties:** identify which particles interact and what changes. Separate internal connections from attractions between particles, physical change from chemical change, and trend from exact prediction. State comparison conditions such as pressure, temperature, size, and shape. Avoid universal force rankings and category-to-property shortcuts. Use synthetic data only when clearly labeled; never turn the learner's measurements into a lab conclusion.
- **Solutions, rates, and equilibrium:** identify species, amounts, concentration units, and the system boundary. Separate how fast change occurs from its favored extent; distinguish a model prediction from measured evidence. Define fixed conditions and audit conservation before interpreting a graph or changing an input.

Introduce unfamiliar language beside the visual, then formalize it. Prefer a small early diagnostic with a recovery explanation over assuming that a learner remembers a periodic-table convention. Place a new check immediately after each substantial concept; end with mixed decisions and an explanation task. Offer a useful pause point before a new model.

## Make the representation auditable

A diagram needs enough information to check the intended claim: clear atom identities or explicitly fictional labels, all relevant electrons or charges, visible units and axes, and a nearby text equivalent. Mark schematic sizes, supplied geometry, and simulated values. Do not let animation hide required electrons or manufacture missing evidence. Check initial, intermediate, final, paused, reset, reduced-motion, and no-script states. A fixed successful example should remain readable without running motion.

Practice must diagnose a plausible error and permit retry. Keep controls local to the page; prevent native form submission when scripts fail or are unavailable. Verify this by submitting or pressing Enter with JavaScript disabled and checking that no response enters a URL or request. Test that visible and accessible diagram descriptions match the current state, and that static/print teaching survives collapsed disclosures.

## Verify the full learning route

Open primary educational sources to verify the chemistry. Inspect learner-facing destinations for direct overlap with supplied work, including their worked examples and vocabulary lists. A citation is not permission to route the learner to the assigned response. Prefer a focused foundational source when a broad chapter would expose it; keep author verification citations separate from the learner's next action. Recheck locally authored reference pages as part of the same lesson set.

Use [the repository rubric](../../../docs/REVIEW-RUBRIC.md) for requested independent review. Provide a fixed candidate and all loaded dependencies, the authorized private source, and the declared lesson/series scope, without an author's score. Assess both each page and the linked coverage. Apply findings to the reusable chemistry instructions when they expose a recurring generation failure, then test that changed guidance against the next candidate. Preserve real scores, evidence limitations, exact hashes, and pending delivery; a target is not a reason to inflate an assessment.
