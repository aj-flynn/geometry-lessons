# English: make the reasoning visible

Use the [shared homework workflow](../homework-teach/SKILL.md) for private intake, routing, freshman-level teaching, accessibility, semantic review, and delivery. This skill adds English decisions; it does not authorize access, publication, or answering an assigned text. Use the library subject `English`.

## Find the thinking the task requires

Privately inspect the whole input, including prompts, tables, handwritten responses, and continuation pages. Determine the required operations, such as paraphrasing, explaining an image's effect, selecting evidence, inferring a trait, comparing interpretations, analyzing structure, or revising a sentence. Do not treat shared vocabulary as coverage. Teach a coherent subset only when its scope and a continuation for the remaining demands are clear.

Existing student responses are not a diagnostic score. In a learner conversation, ask which step feels unclear and give one fresh prerequisite check. When no reply is available, include an optional beginner stop using an unrelated example. For reading, distinguishing something directly stated from an inference is often a useful starting point; for writing, use a small check of the prerequisite actually needed.

## Keep original practice independent

Draft outside the public checkout. Use wholly original passages with different situations, imagery, character relationships, and interpretive claims. Preserve the reasoning operation, not the source scene's emotional conflict or distinctive language. A synonym substitution, updated setting, or renamed character is not sufficient separation. Compare every new passage, model response, hint, feedback message, glossary entry, and text alternative against all supplied questions privately.

Do not translate, classify, interpret, continue, rewrite, or evaluate the assigned passage for the learner. If a definition would itself supply an assigned response, teach a different prerequisite or use a different case. Do not confirm a proposed answer. Model a checking routine on new material and leave the final inference to the learner. Label invented historical-style sentences as invented; never attribute them to an author.

## Teach language accurately

Separate these operations when they matter:

- **Observation:** what the text explicitly says or shows.
- **Paraphrase:** the intended meaning restated in new words; preserve who does what, negation, uncertainty, conditions, and questions. A figurative phrase's plain meaning differs from its word-for-word literal sense.
- **Interpretation:** a claim about significance, attitude, character, or an idea, supported by precise evidence and a reason connecting the two.

Keep claims proportional to evidence. Distinguish character, speaker, narrator, and author; speech can be mistaken, playful, boastful, or deceptive. An action in one moment can support a trait without proving an unchanging personality. Consider an alternative reading when it tests the claim, and show what evidence would strengthen or weaken each reading. Multiple supported interpretations are possible, but unsupported claims are not equally defensible.

For literary devices, explain the wording and effect together. A label alone is incomplete. Test a tempting non-example: `like` or `as` does not always signal a comparison, an image can be literal, and devices can overlap. Explain unfamiliar words inline as well as in the linked glossary; gloss the words used in feedback too.

For unfamiliar historical language, read sentences across line breaks, locate the subject and verb, preserve negatives and conditions, and check uncertain meanings in reliable edition notes or historical dictionaries. Modern-looking words may have changed meanings. Use verified primary educational references, such as Folger for Shakespeare's language, and sources suited to the actual concept. Do not assume every English task concerns Shakespeare.

For writing and grammar, model a revision on an original sentence and explain its effect on meaning, clarity, or audience. Distinguish a grammatical requirement from a stylistic choice. Offer a new sentence or paragraph for transfer; do not produce an assignment-ready response or apply the revision to private homework.

## Build thinking before revealing an explanation

Sequence a small model, a fresh guided attempt, and an independent transfer. An annotated passage can show exact words, their plain meaning, and the supported inference. If selecting quotations is part of the assignment, ask the learner to choose a short sufficient passage from several original lines and explain why it supports the claim. When comparing characters, require a distinct evidence-to-claim link for each. Keep the source wording visible when a learner changes a reading lens so the evidence remains inspectable.

Make the learner do something before displaying the interpretation: predict an effect, choose the strongest evidence, compare two plausible readings, revise an overclaim, or identify what additional context would distinguish readings. Put the prompt outside any closed reveal and keep the explanatory result closed initially. A manipulative should change one meaningful feature or question at a time, offer repeat/reset, preserve the text, and invite an explanation of the result. Do not count tab switching through already-visible answers as substantive practice.

After each major concept, use a fresh check that changes context, representation, or direction. Include plausible misconception feedback and a retry. Balance answer length and specificity so the correct option does not advertise itself. End with a short mixed task where the learner chooses the needed operation, then generates an explanation. Provide comparison guidance after the attempt; do not automatically grade open interpretations as mastery.

Build competing options from actual details in the new passage: an attractive but weaker inference might ignore a qualification, confuse intention with outcome, or read a motive into an action. Feedback should explain both the detail that makes the option tempting and the limit it overlooks. Do not rely mainly on absurd or unrelated distractors. Include one final diagnosis whose prompt and placeholder do not announce the reading operation, name the device, or preselect evidence. Ask what needs checking first and why; keep method-specific hints and comparisons closed until after an attempt. A task labeled “choose a method” is still cued if its wording gives the method away.

Keep the reading path short enough to follow: small stopping points, optional reference material, and a clear return to independent work. Ask for later recall using a new passage before reopening the explanation. Keep all learner responses in page memory or the authorized private chat, never storage or public records.

Author practice forms disabled and enable them only after the script has bound submission prevention, or use another verified no-submit design. Without JavaScript, named inputs must not send responses through a default GET URL or browser history. Keep questions and comparison guidance readable without JavaScript and in print. If printing opens closed explanations, restore their previous state afterward, including when print events repeat.

## Check the actual lesson

Apply the [semantic review](../homework-teach/references/lesson-workflow.md) and [independent rubric](../../../docs/REVIEW-RUBRIC.md). Verify quotation accuracy within the original practice, paraphrase fidelity, grammar, evidence-to-claim links, alternative readings, and consistency between annotation, controls, feedback, and static/print versions. Verify that enabled text-entry boundaries and other essential control indicators contrast at least 3:1 with their adjacent colors; measure the rendered colors, including white fields on pale panels, rather than judging text contrast alone. Check the actual browser at desktop, tablet, phone, and enlarged text; exercise keyboard controls, reset, wrong-answer retry, no-JavaScript fallback, and the Lesson library return link. Record bounded limits honestly.

For requested iterative reviews, revise the reusable guidance first, then regenerate or re-exercise the affected teaching sequence from those instructions and the same authorized private input. Preserve correct existing content and URLs. Freeze the lesson and all loaded assets, give the independent reviewer authorized private input and raw requirements without the author's verdict, and report category evidence and hashes. Do not inflate a score or change the rubric to meet a requested threshold. The shared workflow owns manifest attestations, selected publication, and live verification.
