# Science lesson guidance

Use this branch for scientific concepts. Apply the shared privacy, no-answer, freshman-language, glossary, and publication requirements from `homework-teach`.

## Explain the relationship accurately

Identify the concept rather than the assigned answer. Distinguish an observation (what is measured), a model (a simplified representation), and an explanation (why a relationship is expected). Verify the relevant claims against a trustworthy primary educational source: an appropriate scientific institution, university, established textbook publisher, or original research when needed. Open the source and link the specific relevant page. State uncertainty or conditions instead of filling gaps from a blurry diagram or incomplete prompt.

Check symbols, axes, direction of arrows, units, conversions, signs, and mathematical relationships. Keep meanings precise: quantities that sound similar may describe different things. Explain unfamiliar terminology at first use and in the glossary. Give a concrete picture before adding an equation, and identify what every symbol means.

Do not complete the student's lab report, analyze their actual measurements into the assigned result, invent measurements, or label a copied data set as a new example. Use independently chosen synthetic data when practice needs data, clearly labeled as simulated. Leave the assignment's conclusion, classification, balancing, prediction, or explanation for the learner. If stating a single fact would answer the question directly, work on a prerequisite or a different case.

## Design the graphic and interaction

Choose a visual that explains the concept: a labeled process diagram, a graph with axes and units, a particle model, a comparison, or an interactive representation. Use code-native SVG/HTML/canvas with a text equivalent. Describe which features are symbolic, simplified, or not to scale. Do not imply that an illustration is measured evidence.

Let the learner predict what will change, adjust one input, observe the consequence, then explain it. Label controls with the quantity and units. Hold other quantities fixed only when that is a valid stated model assumption. Check the model's equations, expected trends, valid range, boundary cases, numerical precision, and endpoints against the source. The animation must not silently contradict the accompanying text or feedback. Provide pause/reset, reduced motion, keyboard access, and a static explanation of the key states.

For practice, ask about a new situation or simulated data. Feedback should point to the relationship and invite another attempt. It must not accept or grade the student's actual homework or produce its requested final explanation. Do not introduce instructions for conducting new physical experiments as part of this conceptual-lesson workflow; that is a separate request.
