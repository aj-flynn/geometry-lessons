# Spanish

Apply [homework-teach](../homework-teach/SKILL.md) to the verified Spanish route. Map what the learner must understand or express and the language decisions they must make. Read any image, surrounding dialogue, audio, or printed cue that affects those decisions. Distinguish essential prerequisites from material that happens to appear on the same page.

- Separate meaning from form. Establish what a phrase communicates in context before checking its spelling or grammar. With images or descriptions, distinguish a visible or stated clue from an inference. Allow multiple descriptions when evidence supports them. Avoid inferring identity or personality from appearance alone.
- Teach the smallest relevant contrast by changing one cue at a time. Show what each word depends on. For agreement, trace each description to the noun or subject it describes and contrast shared gender forms with changing endings. For verb forms, connect person and number to the subject, including an understood subject when the ending or context identifies it. For tense, mood, pronouns, or word order, show how the alternatives change meaning in a short situation.
- Scaffold from comprehension to use. Gloss unfamiliar words next to the example and keep Spanish phrases intact with correct accents, punctuation, and language tags. Use natural Spanish with an audience or purpose. Check unfamiliar forms, usage, and meaningful regional variation in an authoritative dictionary or grammar. Label regional or register limits where they affect the learner's choice.
- For reading or listening, model finding a clue in an original short text or clip and offer an equivalent transcript for original audio. For speaking or writing, let the learner rehearse or draft a short message for an unrelated fictional situation before comparing possible versions. Feedback should identify whether the choice changes meaning, breaks a required form, or is a valid variant.
- Keep assigned words, images, sentences, and personal prompts out of learner examples and answer keys. A direct translation, a rearranged sentence with the same cues, or a word bank that gives away the assigned choices can supply the work. Change the context, input type or direction of reasoning, and language where practical. Finish with a fresh transfer and a checklist the learner can apply independently to their own work.
