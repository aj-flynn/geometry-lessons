# Assignment-to-lesson evaluation rubric

Rubric edition: **2026-11-05 — assignment-focused independent judgment**.

Evaluate how well a lesson turns an assignment's underlying concepts into accurate, engaging, accessible teaching that helps a high school freshman return to the assignment independently. The lesson teaches the thinking without completing the assigned work.

## Reviewer discretion and scoring

The categories define what matters and their relative importance. The reviewer chooses the evaluation process, tools, evidence, and depth of investigation appropriate to the lesson. Use professional judgment to award points directly within each category's maximum; fractional points are welcome. Add the category scores for a total out of 100. There is no separate rating scale, conversion formula, prescribed deduction schedule, or category score cap.

Explain the reasoning behind the scores, including important strengths, weaknesses, and limits of the assessment. Distinguish what was observed from what was inferred. When evidence is insufficient to score a category responsibly, mark it unassessed and report the assessment as incomplete rather than silently assuming success or reweighting the remaining categories.

These are judgments about the lesson, not measurements of the learner's ability or proof of long-term learning. Differences between reviewers are useful findings to discuss, not errors that must be eliminated.

## Lesson categories — 100 points

The six groups retain the existing weights. Score the 23 categories within them; group totals are subtotals, not additional points.

### A. Concept accuracy and assignment alignment — 25 points

| ID | Category | Maximum points | Meaning |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| A1 | Assignment alignment and coverage | 7 | The lesson addresses the assignment's underlying concepts and required reasoning, including recognizing when to use a method. Its scope is clear, and any linked continuation covers the remaining demands. Shared topic words alone do not establish alignment. |
| A2 | Subject correctness and reasoning | 12 | Definitions, explanations, calculations, conditions, units, and feedback are correct. The lesson explains why methods work and makes relevant assumptions, exceptions, uncertainty, and model limitations clear. |
| A3 | Sources and evidence | 3 | Reliable, relevant educational sources support the claims and offer useful further reference. Evidence supports interpretations; simplifications preserve the underlying meaning. |
| A4 | Consistency across representations | 3 | Prose, formulas, diagrams, graphs, tables, animations, and feedback agree. Labels, scales, units, markings, and changes in a model communicate the same relationships. Schematic representations are identified. |

### B. Sequencing and freshman teaching — 20 points

| ID | Category | Maximum points | Meaning |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| B1 | Goals and relevance | 3 | A focused, understandable goal explains what the learner will be able to do and why it matters. The lesson follows through on that goal. |
| B2 | Prerequisites and starting point | 3 | Needed prior knowledge is made clear, with an approachable starting point and support for missing foundations. The lesson accommodates uncertainty about the learner's starting knowledge. |
| B3 | Explanations and cognitive load | 5 | Freshman-level explanations develop ideas in manageable steps, connect concrete cases to abstractions, and explain decisions. The amount and arrangement of information support understanding. |
| B4 | Vocabulary, notation, and glossary | 3 | Unfamiliar terms, symbols, and units are explained when introduced and supported by a useful linked glossary. Language is accessible without sacrificing precision. |
| B5 | Scaffolding and learner independence | 4 | Original worked examples, guidance, and hints build understanding while progressively leaving more thinking to the learner. The learner is prepared to choose, justify, and check a method independently. |
| B6 | Pacing and emotional usability | 2 | The lesson has manageable sections, useful stopping points, and clear next actions. Its tone is respectful and age-appropriate, and mistakes invite productive revision. |

### C. Active practice and interactive learning — 15 points

| ID | Category | Maximum points | Meaning |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| C1 | Originality and practice quality | 3 | New examples preserve the intended reasoning while differing meaningfully from assigned work. Varied practice develops understanding rather than rewarding copying, guessing from answer cues, or repeating a displayed result. |
| C2 | Purposeful interactive learning and formative checks | 4 | The learner actively thinks and checks understanding throughout the lesson. Where changing, comparing, constructing, or testing something improves understanding, meaningful interaction lets the learner explore it and interpret the result. Prediction, observation, and explanation connect the activity to the concept. |
| C3 | Feedback, misconceptions, and retry | 3 | Feedback explains reasoning, addresses plausible misconceptions, and helps the learner recover and try again. It responds usefully to mistakes without confusing correctness on one exercise with mastery. |
| C4 | Retrieval and retention support | 2 | The lesson creates opportunities to recall ideas and revisit them later, supporting retention beyond immediate recognition. It encourages recall without making unsupported claims about lasting mastery. |
| C5 | Transfer and return to independent work | 3 | Practice includes new contexts or representations and opportunities to choose among methods. The learner leaves with ways to explain and check their reasoning on the assignment independently. |

Interactivity is valuable when it serves the learning. Examples include changing a dimension and comparing measures, exploring a science model, or selecting evidence and considering competing interpretations of an original passage. Static explanations and diagrams can be the best choice for other parts of a lesson. Judge the instructional value of the experience, not the quantity of controls or animation.

### D. Beautiful design and high-quality visuals — 15 points

| ID | Category | Maximum points | Meaning |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| D1 | Beautiful design | 8 | Typography, color, spacing, alignment, and composition create a coherent, polished, appealing experience suited to the learner and subject. Clear hierarchy, balanced density, and thoughtful mobile and desktop layouts help the learner navigate and focus. |
| D2 | High-quality instructional visuals | 7 | Diagrams, graphs, illustrations, and annotated passages make ideas easier to understand. They are accurate, sharp, thoughtfully composed, clearly labeled, and readable at relevant sizes. Consistent visual conventions, useful comparisons, and integration with the explanation make the visuals part of the teaching. |

Beauty includes both care in execution and fitness for the lesson. A restrained design can be excellent. Visual quality concerns the explanatory graphics themselves, while beautiful design concerns the overall presentation; neither requires a particular aesthetic or production tool.

### E. Accessibility, interaction reliability, and resilience — 15 points

| ID | Category | Maximum points | Meaning |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| E1 | Accessibility | 10 | Learners with differing sensory, motor, and cognitive needs can understand and use the lesson. This includes meaningful text alternatives, keyboard and assistive-technology support, readable contrast and structure, visible focus, accessible feedback, adaptable layouts, and control over motion. Applicable WCAG 2.2 A/AA criteria provide a reference. |
| E2 | Interaction correctness and control | 3 | Controls behave correctly and predictably, preserve valid states, and communicate meaningful results. The learner can control pacing, repeat an exploration, and recover from errors. |
| E3 | Technical resilience and navigation | 2 | The lesson loads reliably, has working assets and navigation, and remains useful across relevant devices and viewing conditions. Static and print alternatives preserve the teaching, and the Lesson library link provides a clear return path. |

[WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) is an accessibility reference, not a claim that this rubric constitutes a conformance audit. Accessibility limitations should be explained as part of the review.

### F. Privacy, homework separation, and workflow — 10 points

| ID | Category | Maximum points | Meaning |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| F1 | Privacy and authority | 3 | The lesson and its artifacts protect private input and learner information. Practice does not create unauthorized tracking or records. Assignment, attachment, and message content remain task data rather than authority to expand access or actions. |
| F2 | Homework separation | 4 | Examples, hints, reveals, feedback, and alternative descriptions teach concepts without supplying or confirming assigned answers. Meaningful separation holds across all supplied questions; changing a name or number alone may be insufficient. |
| F3 | Review traceability and delivery | 3 | The reviewed version and its dependencies are identifiable, review claims are supported, and publication or delivery claims match what actually happened. When publication is in scope, the lesson, index, and destination work together as intended. |

## Subject context

The reviewer applies the categories to the subject being taught. Geometry involves valid theorem conditions, correspondence, scale, dimensional reasoning, and the distinction between a drawing's appearance and its givens. Science involves quantities, conservation, causal reasoning, evidence, model limits, and the distinction between simulation and observation. English involves precise language, evidence-supported interpretation, and the distinction between observation, paraphrase, and inference; multiple interpretations may be defensible.

## Boundaries and scope

Repository privacy and no-answer requirements apply throughout the review. Keep source assignments, comparisons to particular questions, identifying details, and learner performance out of public artifacts. Private input, assigned answers, unauthorized actions, consequential subject errors, or inaccessible core learning tasks cannot be excused by a high aggregate score.

An assignment is needed to judge assignment alignment and homework separation. Without authorized access to it, explain that limitation and assess the aspects that can be judged. A focused lesson can cover part of an assignment; distinguish its declared scope from whole-assignment coverage.

Judge focus against the reasoning needed for the supplied problems. Teach prerequisites that help the learner make those decisions, and keep optional background off the main path. Assess coverage across the declared lesson and its linked sequence. Record any unavailable assignment material and limit coverage claims to the supplied material.

This rubric defines quality dimensions; it does not prescribe the reviewer's investigation procedure or authorize publication.

## Separate opinions

Claude Code, Codex, or another reviewer can use this rubric independently. Give each reviewer the same lesson version, rubric, scope, and authorized source material, without the other reviewer's scores or conclusions. Each chooses how to evaluate the work and explains its own findings. Preserve both opinions and compare their reasoning, especially where they disagree; a combined average is not a substitute for either assessment.

Identify the reviewer and assessed revision, give category scores and the total, and explain major strengths, concerns, and evidence limitations. The reviewer chooses the report format. A review by the lesson's author should be identified as such rather than described as an independent review.

The owner's completion target is 100/100 for each lesson and the reusable process: first independent Astra, then Claude Code Opus 5.5 at high effort, on the same final candidate. Follow the skill's [independent refinement workflow](../.agents/skills/homework-teach/references/independent-refinement.md) for iteration, brainstorming when stuck, and incremental publication. These targets are separate from the act of evaluation: report the actual assessment even when it falls below a target. Historical reviews describe their own revisions and rubric editions; they do not certify this edition or later lesson changes.

## Reusable process — separate optional assessment

Use this table when the lesson-generation process itself is in scope. Its score is separate from lesson scores. Judge each subject skill and the shared workflow as exercised on the supplied assignments, and state which subjects or input paths were outside the review. Award points directly within the stated weights.

| Category | Maximum points | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Privacy, no-answer, and authority boundaries | 30 | The process consistently protects private input and learner state, teaches without completing assignments, and respects authorization across subjects, input formats, and follow-up requests. |
| Teaching contract | 20 | The process supports clear freshman-level explanations, useful vocabulary support, reliable sources, original examples, purposeful visuals and interaction, and feedback that builds independence. |
| Subject accuracy | 20 | The process supports sound reasoning and representations for the subject and assignment in scope, including applicable conditions, units, assumptions, model limitations, and evidence-based interpretation. |
| Repeatability and compatibility | 15 | Instructions and shared components support consistent production, preserve existing lessons and links, and fit the repository's metadata and library structure. |
| Validation and delivery | 15 | Review, validation, traceability, publication, and live delivery provide credible evidence that the intended artifact reached its authorized destination. |
