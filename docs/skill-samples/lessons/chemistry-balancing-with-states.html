A subscript is a small number inside a formula. It tells you how many atoms of the preceding element are in one counted unit. For example, one molecule of CS2 contains one carbon atom and two sulfur atoms. A coefficient is the number in front of a formula. It multiplies the count of every element in that formula.

Match one atom count at a time

Hydrogen bromide formation

H2 + Br2 → HBr

In this example, all three substances are gases under the stated conditions.

The arrow separates the reactants (starting substances) from the products (substances formed). Atoms are rearranged, so each element must have the same number of atoms before and after the reaction.

  1. With an understood 1 in every coefficient position, the left side has 2 H atoms and 2 Br atoms. The right side has 1 of each.
  2. Use 2 HBr to supply 2 H atoms on the right. It also supplies 2 Br atoms, so both counts match. Keep HBr intact because the formula identifies the substance.
  3. Write H2 + Br2 → 2 HBr. The simplest whole-number ratio is 1 to 1 to 2. There is no whole number greater than 1 that divides all three coefficients.
  4. A state symbol goes directly after its formula. Use the stated conditions for each substance. Here, all three are gases, so write H2(g) + Br2(g) → 2 HBr(g). The counts stay the same.
  5. If a second description specifies liquid bromine and the other two substances as gases, write H2(g) + Br2(l) → 2 HBr(g). Only the label on bromine changes.

Finish the worked equation

  1. Return to the trial with 1 Fe2O3. Fe matches at 2 atoms and S matches at 4. On the right, add oxygen from both formulas: (1 × 3) + (4 × 2) = 11 O atoms.
  2. O2 is the only term you can change here without disturbing Fe or S. It delivers O atoms in pairs. Eleven is odd, so double all of the trial coefficients to keep Fe and S matched and make the right-side O total even.
  3. Now the right side has (2 × 3) + (8 × 2) = 22 O atoms. Use 11 O2 on the left, since 11 × 2 = 22.
  4. Check 4 FeS2 + 11 O2 → 2 Fe2O3 + 8 SO2. Each side has 4 Fe, 8 S and 22 O atoms. All given formulas are neutral, so both sides have zero total charge. The coefficients share no whole-number divisor greater than 1.

Glossary

Subscript
A small number in a formula. For one element symbol, no subscript means one atom per counted unit.
Coefficient
The multiplier before a formula. A blank in a completed equation means 1.
Reactants
The starting substances, written to the left of the reaction arrow.
Products
The substances formed, written to the right of the arrow.
Simplest whole-number ratio
A set of whole-number coefficients with no shared divisor greater than 1.
State symbol
A parenthetical label for the stated conditions. (s) is solid, (l) is liquid, (g) is gas, and (aq) means dissolved in water.
Formula unit
One count of the ratio given by the formula of an ionic compound.

Fresh retry · Iron oxide

Fe2O3 + 3 CO → 2 Fe + 3 CO2

With 1 Fe2O3, use 2 Fe. Use equal coefficients for CO and CO2 to match C. Each CO becomes CO2 by picking up one extra O. Three CO take the three O atoms from Fe2O3. Left O is 3 + (3 × 1) = 6, and right O is 3 × 2 = 6. Each side has Fe 2, C 3 and O 6.
