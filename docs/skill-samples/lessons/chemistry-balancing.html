01 / Count one unit

What does the number multiply?

In 2 CO2, the front number, or coefficient, counts two molecules. A molecule is a group of bonded atoms. The small number, or subscript, gives two O atoms per molecule. Each molecule also contains one C atom. Two molecules contain 2 C and 4 O atoms.

In an ionic crystal, positive and negative ions (charged particles) repeat through a solid. A formula unit gives their simplest ratio. For one Al2(SO4)3 formula unit, the outer 3 repeats the whole sulfate group. The counts are 2 Al, 3 × 1 = 3 S and 3 × 4 = 12 O atoms. If you write a 2 in front, all three counts double.

Compare your count

4 units × 2 nitrate groups per unit × 3 O atoms per group = 24 O atoms. If you counted 8, you counted N. Try counting Co the same way.

Check the retry

Each unit represents one Co atom, so four units represent four Co atoms.

02 / Follow the atoms

Change the amount, keep the formula

A reaction arrow separates the reactants (starting substances) from the products (substances formed). Imagine a sealed chamber. Atoms that start in one grouping may end in another. Each C stays a C and each O stays an O.

Predict. Imagine two CO molecules and two O2 molecules on the left, with two CO2 molecules on the right. Carbon already agrees. Will you need one fewer or one more O2 molecule to account for the oxygen? Explain what happens to the oxygen row before you test it.

Audit a different chamber

A record lists 3 carbon atoms and 2 oxygen atoms before a reaction, then 2 carbon atoms and 3 oxygen atoms afterward. A technician says the record is fine because both totals are five. What would you ask them to investigate before accepting the record?

Compare the audit

The carbon count lost one and the oxygen count gained one. In a sealed chamber undergoing an ordinary chemical change, look for a missing group, a counting error or an incorrect label. Try again with before counts of 2 C and 4 O and after counts of 2 C and 5 O. Which label points to the problem this time?

Check the retry

O points to the mismatch. Both records account for two C atoms.

How did we choose two CO molecules? Start with one CO and one CO2. Exactly one O atom is missing from the left, but O2 supplies O atoms in pairs. Double the entire trial: two CO and two CO2 now leave room for two O atoms, which one O2 can supply. When two rows tie different batch sizes together, look for a total both sides can make.

03 / Link the rows

Let one count constrain another

Given. Under conditions that favor chlorate formation, chlorine and potassium hydroxide can give potassium chloride, potassium chlorate and water. Treat those as the given species.

Cl2(g) + KOH(aq) → KCl(aq) + KClO3(aq) + H2O(l)

Goal. Find the smallest positive whole-number coefficients. Use the formulas to account for atoms. The labels (g), (aq), (l) and (s) mean gas, dissolved in water, liquid and solid.

  1. Set a scale. Try one KClO3 unit. Let x be the number of KOH units. KOH supplies x H atoms, so water uses x ÷ 2 molecules.
  2. Reconcile oxygen. KOH supplies x O atoms. On the right, the chlorate term uses 3 O atoms and water uses x ÷ 2 O atoms. x = 3 + x ÷ 2 x ÷ 2 = 3 Subtract half of x from both sides. x = 6 Double both sides. That fixes 6 KOH units and 3 water molecules. The numerical totals here count atoms.
  3. Use the potassium and chlorine counts. Six K atoms arrive in the KOH. One goes to the chlorate term, leaving five for 5 KCl units. The two salts then contain 5 + 1 = 6 Cl atoms, supplied by 3 Cl2 molecules.
  4. Check the result. Both sides have 6 Cl, 6 K, 6 O and 6 H atoms. All formulas shown are neutral, so both sides have zero net charge. The coefficients have no common divisor above one.

3 Cl2(g) + 6 KOH(aq) → 5 KCl(aq) + KClO3(aq) + 3 H2O(l)

KOH contributes to three element counts. Changing its coefficient changes every row containing K, O or H. The shared variable carries that effect into the water count automatically.

When a whole group repeats

Consider Al2(SO4)3(aq) + CaCl2(aq) → AlCl3(aq) + CaSO4(s). The sulfate group appears intact in just one formula on each side. Start with one aluminum sulfate unit. Three sulfate groups call for 3 CaSO4 units. Supply their three Ca with 3 CaCl2. The two Al then call for 2 AlCl3. Check Cl last: 3 × 2 = 2 × 3. Both sides also contain 2 Al, 3 Ca, 3 S and 12 O atoms. All terms are neutral formulas. The coefficients 1, 3, 2 and 3 share no divisor above one.

If the same elements appear in changed groups or other formulas, write separate atom rows for those elements. A group count alone could miss some of those atoms.

05 / Take the method with you

Explain your first move

Pick one equation you just built. Say why you started where you did, which rows changed together, and how you knew you could stop. For your own work, sketch a before-and-after table and check it after every revision.

Later recall. Before reopening the lesson, write a formula with parentheses and put a coefficient in front. Point to what each number repeats. Then recall one sign that a group shortcut will miss atoms.

Glossary

Atom
A unit of a chemical element. C and O are element symbols.
Coefficient
A number in front of a formula that multiplies the whole formula. An unwritten coefficient is one.
Subscript
A small number after a symbol or closing parenthesis. It gives the count of that atom or group within one formula.
Molecule
A group of atoms bonded together as one particle.
Ion and formula unit
An ion has a positive or negative charge. In an ionic solid the formula unit expresses the simplest ratio of ions in the extended structure.
Reactants and products
The substances consumed and formed, shown before and after the arrow respectively.
State labels
(s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water. They do not multiply atom counts.
Net charge
The sum of the charges represented on one side. For equations written with individual ions, include each ion's coefficient in that sum.
