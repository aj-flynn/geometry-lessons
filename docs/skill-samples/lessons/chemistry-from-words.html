General chemistry · Reaction notation

From reaction words to equations

Turn a reaction description into a balanced equation with the same count of each element on both sides.

  1. Read the description
  2. Build the formulas
  3. Balance the counts
  4. Glossary

Before you start

In 2 NH3, the coefficient 2 counts whole molecules. The subscript 3 counts H atoms in one molecule. How many H atoms are in two ammonia molecules?

Compare and refresh

Each NH3 contains 1 N and 3 H. Two copies contain 2 × 3 = 6 H atoms and 2 × 1 = 2 N atoms. An unwritten subscript means 1.

1. Read the description

A lab report describes mixing aqueous (dissolved in water) lead(II) nitrate with aqueous potassium phosphate. It identifies the solid that forms as lead(II) phosphate. Potassium nitrate remains in solution.

Read for roles before writing symbols. The reactants are what reacts. The products are what forms. “Mixing” identifies the substances on the left. The solid that “forms” and the compound that “remains” go on the right. Use a plus sign between substances on the same side.

Left of → · ReactantsLead(II) nitrate + potassium phosphate
Right of → · ProductsLead(II) phosphate + potassium nitrate

Add a state symbol immediately after each formula. Solid is (s), liquid is (l), and gas or vapor is (g). Both “aqueous” and “remains in solution” here give (aq). Use the stated conditions. If your course marks heating with Δ, put it above the arrow.

Read a product-first sentence

Oxygen gas is released when liquid hydrogen peroxide breaks down. Liquid water also forms. Put each substance on its side and add state symbols before comparing.

Where does the oxygen gas belong?

Compare the roles and states

Hydrogen peroxide (l) → water (l) + oxygen (g). “Breaks down” identifies the starting material. “Is released” and “forms” identify the products.

Retry. Solid copper metal appears when solid zinc is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate. Zinc sulfate stays dissolved. Write a word equation with state symbols.

Compare the word equation

Zinc (s) + copper(II) sulfate (aq) → copper (s) + zinc sulfate (aq). The zinc and copper(II) sulfate are present at the start.

2. Build the formulas

If the description gives a formula, copy it. For a named ionic compound, look up its ions (charged atoms or groups). A polyatomic ion contains several atoms and carries one overall charge. Its internal subscripts stay fixed.

Find the lead(II) and nitrate rows. One Pb2+ takes two NO3− groups for a total charge of zero. Write the positive ion first. Parentheses make the outside 2 apply to the whole nitrate group. The neutral formula unit (smallest whole-number ion ratio) is Pb(NO3)2.

Use a different lookup row. Write potassium phosphate, then lead(II) phosphate and potassium nitrate. Keep the charges on your scratch work.

Compare the other three formulas
  • Three K+ for one PO43− gives K3PO4.
  • Three Pb2+ for two PO43− gives Pb3(PO4)2. Each sign contributes six charges in magnitude.
  • One K+ for one NO3− gives KNO3.

For a named element, check its form under the given conditions. Nitrogen, oxygen and hydrogen normally occur as diatomic gases (two atoms per molecule), even if the sentence just says “gas.” For example, nitrogen gas is N2(g). The subscript 2 is part of the formula. The (g) only tells you the state.

For a molecular equation, write each substance as a complete neutral formula, including dissolved salts. In solution, those salts can be present as separate ions.

Pb(NO3)2(aq) + K3PO4(aq) → Pb3(PO4)2(s) + KNO3(aq)

This skeleton equation has the right sides, formulas and states. Its atom counts still need balancing.

Retry. Build the neutral formula for aluminum sulfate. Why do you need parentheses?

Compare the sulfate formula

Two Al3+ give +6 and three SO42− give −6. The neutral formula is Al2(SO4)3. Three whole sulfate groups contain 3 S and 12 O.

3. Balance the counts

The words fix the substances, sides and states. Changing a subscript changes a substance. Change only coefficients so that every element has the same number of atoms on each side of the arrow.

Start with the solid product. For one Pb3(PO4)2(s), you need three Pb atoms and two phosphate groups. Each phosphate group appears intact in one formula per side, so you can track it as a unit here. When a group breaks apart, tally its elements separately. Start with 2 before potassium phosphate and predict what else will change when you increase the lead(II) nitrate coefficient from 1 to 3.

Compare the lead nitrate counts

Changing from Pb(NO3)2(aq) to 3 Pb(NO3)2(aq) takes the left from 1 to 3 Pb, 2 to 6 N, and 14 to 26 O. Each added unit brings 1 Pb, 2 N and 6 O. The unchanged 2 K3PO4(aq) supplies all 6 K and 2 P, plus 8 of the O atoms. The right still has 3 Pb, 1 K, 2 P, 1 N and 11 O.

Now the left has six nitrate groups and six K atoms. Put 6 in front of potassium nitrate to match both at once.

3 Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2 K3PO4(aq) → Pb3(PO4)2(s) + 6 KNO3(aq)

Both sides have 3 Pb, 6 K, 2 P, 6 N and 26 O atoms. All terms are neutral. The coefficients 3, 2, 1 and 6 have no common divisor greater than 1. Omit the 1 in front of the product.

When oxygen ends up in two products

In the first stage of making nitric acid, ammonia gas reacts with oxygen gas over a solid catalyst. Nitrogen monoxide gas and steam form. Use NH3 for ammonia and H2O for water. Elemental oxygen is O2. The mono- in nitrogen monoxide gives one O, so write NO. In molecular names, di- means two. Steam is water in the gas state.

NH3(g) + O2(g) → NO(g) + H2O(g)

Start with N and H, which each go into one product. Two NH3 give 2 N and 6 H, so use two NO and three H2O. Start the oxygen check with two O2.

Choose the next coefficient to test

Which coefficient can you raise by 1 while keeping N and H matched? Try O2, NO or H2O. Predict the new O totals before testing.

2 NH3(g) + 2 O2(g) → 2 NO(g) + 3 H2O(g)

Compare the trials and finish balancing
  • 3 O2(g) leaves N at 2 and H at 6 on both sides. O is now 6 on the left and 5 on the right.
  • 3 NO(g) raises right-side N to 3. 4 H2O(g) raises right-side H to 8. Either trial also raises right-side O to 6.

Keep the product coefficients at 2 and 3 so N and H still match. Add both oxygen contributions.

2 NO(g)2 × 1 = 2 O atoms
3 H2O(g)3 × 1 = 3 O atoms
Right-side total2 + 3 = 5 O atoms

The two whole-number trials for oxygen supply 4 or 6 O. The coefficient 5/2 supplies 5 O in the ratio. Multiply every coefficient by 2 to clear the fraction.

4 NH3(g) + 5 O2(g) → 4 NO(g) + 6 H2O(g)

Each side has 4 N, 12 H and 10 O. The four coefficients have no common divisor greater than 1.

Retry. Solid silver(I) oxide breaks down on heating, forming solid silver and diatomic oxygen gas. Write and balance the full equation.

Compare the silver oxide reaction

One Ag+ cannot offset O2− alone. Use Ag2O. Two units supply the two O atoms needed for one O2 molecule and also supply four Ag atoms. Both sides are neutral.

2 Ag2O(s) → 4 Ag(s) + O2(g)

Work on your own

Pause here. When you return, recall what a coefficient changes. Try A before opening its comparison.

For each report, write a balanced molecular equation with states. Justify one formula and one coefficient.

A · A hot exhaust stream

In an incinerator, hydrogen sulfide gas, H2S, burns in excess oxygen gas to form sulfur dioxide gas and steam.

Compare the gas equation

Oxygen gas is O2(g), sulfur dioxide is SO2(g), and steam is H2O(g). For one H2S, use one SO2 to match S and one H2O to match H. These products contain 2 + 1 = 3 O atoms. Doubling gives 6 O atoms, supplied by three O2.

2 H2S(g) + 3 O2(g) → 2 SO2(g) + 2 H2O(g)

Each side has 4 H, 2 S and 6 O. With 2 O2 for one H2S, O is 4 on the left and 3 on the right. Use 3/2 O2, then double all four coefficients.

B · A solid settles out

Solid strontium sulfate forms when solutions of strontium nitrate and potassium sulfate mix. Potassium nitrate stays dissolved.

Compare the solution equation

“Forms when” puts strontium sulfate on the right. Match charges to write the formulas. Sr2+ takes two nitrate groups and SO42− takes two K+. The products use 1:1 ion ratios. Two potassium nitrate units match two K and two nitrate groups.

Sr(NO3)2(aq) + K2SO4(aq) → SrSO4(s) + 2 KNO3(aq)

Each side has 1 Sr, 2 K, 2 N, 1 S and 10 O. Use whole neutral formulas for dissolved salts in this molecular equation.

C · A metal changes places

Exposed aluminum metal reacts with dissolved copper(II) nitrate to form solid copper and dissolved aluminum nitrate.

Compare the metal reaction

Use element symbols for the solid metals. Cu2+ takes two nitrate groups and Al3+ takes three. Match six nitrate groups with three copper(II) nitrate units and two aluminum nitrate units. Then match the metals.

2 Al(s) + 3 Cu(NO3)2(aq) → 3 Cu(s) + 2 Al(NO3)3(aq)

Each side has 2 Al, 3 Cu, 6 N and 18 O. Check neutral formulas and the simplest ratio.

D · A solid breaks down

On heating, solid ammonium dichromate forms solid chromium(III) oxide, nitrogen gas and water vapor.

Compare the heated solid equation

Use two ammonium ions per dichromate ion. Both groups break apart, so count their atoms separately. One Cr2O3 matches 2 Cr, one N2 matches 2 N, and four water molecules match 8 H. The products contain 3 + 4 = 7 O atoms.

(NH4)2Cr2O7(s) → Cr2O3(s) + N2(g) + 4 H2O(g)

Check 2 N, 8 H, 2 Cr and 7 O per side. Use Δ above the arrow if your course marks heat.

Back to your assignment

  1. Mark the verb that separates what reacts from what forms. Mark each state.
  2. Write the skeleton. Choose an elemental form, molecular formula or neutral ion ratio. Look up unfamiliar entries.
  3. After each coefficient change, recount every affected element. Check both sides, states, neutral formulas and the smallest whole-number ratio.

Later recall. Close the page and write the three steps from memory. Explain how you’d recover if an oxygen count is odd and the only place left to adjust it comes in pairs.

Glossary

Balanced equation
Equation with the same number of atoms of every element and the same total charge on both sides.
Coefficient
Number in front of a whole formula. An unwritten coefficient means 1.
Subscript
Small number below the line. It counts the atom or parenthesized group immediately before it.
Reactant
Substance that reacts, written to the left of the arrow.
Product
Substance that forms, written to the right of the arrow.
State symbol
Parenthetical label after the formula that indicates physical state or whether the substance is dissolved in water.
Aqueous
Dissolved in water, shown as (aq).
Ion
Atom or group of atoms with an electrical charge.
Polyatomic ion
Group of two or more bonded atoms with an overall charge.
Formula unit
Counting unit for an ionic compound based on its simplest whole-number ratio of ions.
Molecular equation
Equation that uses whole formulas for the substances, including dissolved compounds that separate into ions in water.
Skeleton equation
Arrangement of the correct formulas on their respective sides before balancing.
Diatomic
Containing two atoms per molecule. For an elemental molecule those atoms are of the same element.
