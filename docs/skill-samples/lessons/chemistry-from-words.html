General chemistry · Writing equations

From reaction words to balanced equations

Read what reacts and what forms. Write each substance and its state. Then find the smallest whole-number amounts that conserve every element.

Use the reactions as written models.

Before you start. How many sulfur and oxygen atoms are represented by 2 SO3?

An ion is an atom or group with a charge. In the ionic name iron(II) bromide, (II) gives the iron ion a +2 charge. How many Br− ions balance one Fe2+ ion?

Check the count and the charges

Two SO3 molecules contain two S and six O atoms. The front number multiplies the whole formula.

Two Br− ions give −2, so the charges cancel and the formula is FeBr2. The Roman numeral gives a charge. The subscript, the small 2 in the formula, gives a count.

Use the glossary as you work.

1. Translate before you balance

Heating solid silver(I) oxide yields solid silver and oxygen gas. “Yields” marks the arrow between reactants (starting substances) and products (substances formed). Heat can go above the arrow as a condition.

  1. Build the formulas. Ag+ and O2− need a 2:1 ratio for zero total charge, giving Ag2O. Elemental silver is Ag. Free oxygen gas is diatomic, meaning each O2 molecule contains two oxygen atoms.
  2. Label the states. A state symbol tells how the substance is present in this reaction. Here the solids get (s) and oxygen gas gets (g). Liquid (l) describes a substance in its liquid state. Aqueous (aq) means dissolved in water.
  3. Write a skeleton. A skeleton equation places the substances on their sides before the amounts are settled. In a molecular equation, keep each substance’s complete neutral formula, even for a salt dissolved in water.

Ag2O(s) → Ag(s) + O2(g)

Keep formulas and states fixed. Change only a coefficient, the number in front. A subscript is a small number inside a formula that counts atoms or groups. Changing it changes the substance.

Count Ag2O formula units (ion ratios in the solid), Ag atoms, and O2 molecules. The same ratios work in moles.

Predict. One O2 molecule needs two oxygen atoms. How many silver(I) oxide formula units supply those atoms? What will that choice do to the silver count?

Set the silver coefficient to 4. Both sides now have Ag 4 and O 2. The terms are neutral, and 2:4:1 is simplest.

2 Ag2O(s) → 4 Ag(s) + O2(g)

Try a molten salt

An electric current through molten lithium chloride produces liquid lithium and chlorine gas. Write the whole equation on paper. Use Li+ and Cl− as formula clues. What will the lithium chloride coefficient be?

Compare the full equation

2 LiCl(l) → 2 Li(l) + Cl2(g)

Both sides have Li 2 and Cl 2. If you used (aq) for molten LiCl, look for a solvent in the description. The salt itself is liquid. If chlorine still differs, keep Cl2 and adjust the front number on LiCl.

Retry with a different salt

In a written reaction model, molten barium chloride yields liquid barium and chlorine gas. Barium forms a 2+ ion. Which coefficient pattern changes?

Compare the new ratio

BaCl2(l) → Ba(l) + Cl2(g). One unit already supplies two Cl atoms and one Ba atom.

2. Count all sources of an element

Methanol vapor, CH3OH, burns in oxygen gas to form carbon dioxide gas and water vapor. This is combustion in oxygen. Both products are gases.

CH3OH(g) + O2(g) → CO2(g) + H2O(g)

Match C and H first. One CH3OH has one C and four H, so use one CO2 and two H2O. Then adjust O2. A term containing a free element changes only that element when you adjust its coefficient.

Predict. Which O2 coefficient fills the oxygen gap for one fuel molecule? Count the O already in the fuel. A temporary fraction is allowed.

Try a fuel with a different composition

Liquid ethylene glycol, C2H6O2, burns with oxygen gas. It forms gaseous carbon dioxide and water vapor. Write a balanced molecular equation and show one oxygen count for each side.

Compare your counts

One fuel unit needs two CO2 and three H2O. They contain seven O atoms. The fuel already supplies two, leaving five. Use 5/2 O2 as a temporary coefficient and then double the whole equation.

2 C2H6O2(l) + 5 O2(g) → 4 CO2(g) + 6 H2O(g)

Both sides have C 4, H 12 and O 14. If you got 10 O on the left, add the 4 O atoms in the fuel. If doubling broke your C or H counts, check that you doubled every coefficient.

Retry with a different fuel

Use ethanol vapor (C2H6O), oxygen gas, carbon dioxide gas and water vapor. Is doubling necessary?

Compare the ethanol case

One fuel molecule requires two CO2 and three H2O. Seven product O atoms minus one O atom in the fuel leaves six. Use three O2. All coefficients are already whole numbers.

3. Keep ionic formulas whole

Solutions of aluminum sulfate and barium chloride are mixed. Solid barium sulfate forms, while aluminum chloride remains in solution. The solid that forms from solution is a precipitate.

The periodic table (IUPAC PDF) gives Ba for barium. The ion reference gives SO42− for sulfate, a polyatomic ion (a bonded group with a charge).

Use Al3+, Ba2+, and Cl− with sulfate to make neutral formulas.

  • Two Al3+ balance three sulfate ions (+6 and −6). Write Al2(SO4)3. The outside 3 repeats the entire sulfate group, so one unit has 3 S and 12 O.
  • One Ba2+ needs two Cl−, giving BaCl2. One Ba2+ and one sulfate give BaSO4. One Al3+ needs three Cl−, giving AlCl3.
  • Keep whole formulas for the (aq) terms, even if the salts separate into ions in water.

Al2(SO4)3(aq) + BaCl2(aq) → BaSO4(s) + AlCl3(aq)

Predict. Each sulfate group stays intact. What coefficient on barium sulfate will match the left side? Follow that choice through Ba and Cl.

Match the sulfate groups first. Three groups require 3 BaSO4. Match its Ba with 3 BaCl2, then use the six Cl atoms and two Al atoms in 2 AlCl3.

Al2(SO4)3(aq) + 3 BaCl2(aq) → 3 BaSO4(s) + 2 AlCl3(aq)

Each side has Al 2, Ba 3, S 3, O 12 and Cl 6. All formulas have zero net charge. With a 1 in the ratio 1:3:3:2, no further reduction is possible.

Try another solution pair

Mix aqueous potassium chromate and silver(I) nitrate. Solid silver(I) chromate forms and potassium nitrate stays dissolved. Use K+, Ag+, CrO42− and NO3−. Write the molecular equation before checking the solid term.

How should the solid product appear?

Compare the full equation

K2CrO4(aq) + 2 AgNO3(aq) → Ag2CrO4(s) + 2 KNO3(aq)

Both sides have K 2, Cr 1, O 10, Ag 2 and N 2. If nitrate is short on one side, adjust the coefficient in front of the whole nitrate compound. If the product is dissolved in your equation, reread which substance forms a solid.

Retry the solid formula

Use Pb2+ and I−.

Compare the lead compound

PbI2(s). One lead ion and two iodide ions make a neutral formula unit. The solid state comes from the description.

4. Read the conditions as written

Hot solid iron and steam form solid magnetite (Fe3O4) and hydrogen gas. Steam is H2O(g). Use Fe(s) for the metal and H2(g) for free hydrogen. Keep the specified oxide formula.

Fe(s) + H2O(g) → Fe3O4(s) + H2(g)

Predict. One magnetite unit needs four oxygen atoms. Where will they come from? Count the hydrogen atoms that arrive with them.

One magnetite unit needs 3 Fe and 4 O atoms. Four waters supply the O and bring 8 H atoms, enough for 4 H2. Both sides have Fe 3, O 4 and H 8. The neutral terms are in the simplest ratio.

3 Fe(s) + 4 H2O(g) → Fe3O4(s) + 4 H2(g)

When hydrogen goes into two products

Solid calcium hydride (CaH2) and liquid water produce calcium hydroxide and hydrogen gas. In the case shown, the hydroxide forms a solid. Two OH− balance one Ca2+, so write Ca(OH)2(s).

CaH2(s) + H2O(l) → Ca(OH)2(s) + H2(g)

Predict. Two waters supply the two O atoms in one hydroxide unit. How many H atoms are left for the gas after making the solid?

Try a reaction that makes steam

Gaseous hydrogen sulfide (H2S) reacts with solid copper(II) oxide, producing solid copper(II) sulfide and water vapor. Write the equation. Use Cu2+, O2− and S2− for the solids.

Compare formulas and states

H2S(g) + CuO(s) → CuS(s) + H2O(g)

Both sides have H 2, S 1, Cu 1 and O 1, so all coefficients are 1. If you wrote H2 on the left, reread the full compound name. If you labeled water (l), look for the word that gives its state here.

Retry with a new set of state cues

A description says that aqueous hydrogen peroxide decomposes to liquid water and oxygen gas. Write the skeleton with state labels.

Compare the skeleton

H2O2(aq) → H2O(l) + O2(g). “Aqueous” describes a substance dissolved in water, while “liquid water” describes water itself. Balancing would be a separate step.

Write two equations from scratch

Write each balanced molecular equation with state symbols. Point to one formula or state clue. Explain your first balancing step.

A. Two solutions

A solution of ammonium phosphate is mixed with a solution of calcium bromide. Solid calcium phosphate separates, and ammonium bromide stays in solution.

Use Ca2+ and bromide (charge −1). Use the periodic table for symbols and the ion reference for the other charges.

Diagnose and compare A
  • Formula differs? Check the charges in calcium phosphate. Three calcium ions and two phosphate ions cancel.
  • Formula matches but counts differ? Start with phosphate, then calcium. Use parentheses for multiple copies of a polyatomic ion inside a formula.
  • Only a state differs? Compare the substance that separates with the one that stays dissolved.

2 (NH4)3PO4(aq) + 3 CaBr2(aq) → Ca3(PO4)2(s) + 6 NH4Br(aq)

Each side has N 6, H 24, P 2, O 8, Ca 3 and Br 6.

B. A metal and a solution

Solid aluminum reacts with aqueous potassium hydroxide and liquid water. The products are hydrogen gas and a dissolved salt made of K+ and [Al(OH)4]− ions.

Diagnose and compare B
  • Product formula differs? One K+ cancels the charge on one [Al(OH)4]− ion.
  • O matches but H differs? For one dissolved product unit, one KOH and three waters supply four O atoms. They also supply seven H atoms. Subtract the four H in the dissolved product before pairing what is left.
  • Counts match with a fraction? Three remaining H atoms require 3/2 H2. Double all coefficients to get whole numbers.

2 Al(s) + 2 KOH(aq) + 6 H2O(l) → 2 K[Al(OH)4](aq) + 3 H2(g)

Each side has Al 2, K 2, O 8 and H 14. Both equations have neutral terms and the smallest whole-number ratios.

Later recall. Before reopening the lesson, write down the three decisions you make before adjusting any coefficient. Explain how you would find an incorrect state label even in a balanced equation.

Glossary

Reactants and products
Starting substances are on the left. Substances formed are on the right.
Diatomic
Two atoms per molecule. The subscript 2 in O2 is part of the formula for free oxygen gas.
State symbol
(s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water.
Aqueous
Dissolved in water, indicated by (aq).
Skeleton equation
The formulas on their proper sides before balancing the equation.
Coefficient
Number before a formula that scales the amount of the entire substance. A coefficient of 1 is usually omitted.
Subscript
Number showing how many atoms or parenthesized groups occur in one formula unit or molecule. An omitted subscript means 1.
Formula unit
The simplest ratio of ions indicated by an ionic compound’s formula. It describes composition in an extended solid.
Combustion
A reaction in which a fuel burns. Complete combustion of the fuels in this lesson gives carbon dioxide and water.
Precipitate
A solid that forms from a solution.
Ion
An atom or bonded group with a net electric charge.
Polyatomic ion
A bonded group of atoms with a net electric charge.
Molecular equation
Represents substances with their complete chemical formulas, including dissolved ionic compounds.
