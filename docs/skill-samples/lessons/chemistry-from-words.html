From reaction words to equations

Write a molecular equation using one formula for each substance. Atoms rearrange, so each element must have the same count on both sides. Find the smallest whole-number ratio.

Choose a formula route.

  • Salt or named ions. An ion is a charged atom or group. For salts such as ammonium sulfide, find the ions and make their charges cancel.
  • Prefixes in a molecular name. Mono, di and tri count one, two and three.
  • Formula given or common name. Use the given formula or look up a name like water.

Choose routes for dinitrogen trioxide and ammonium sulfide. Write the first formula. Then count S and O in 2SO3. The coefficient, or front multiplier, is 2.

1. Set the formulas and states

When heated, solid silver(I) oxide breaks down into solid silver and oxygen gas. Put the reactant (starting substance) before the arrow and the products (substances formed) after it. Heat is a condition. A Δ above the arrow can mark it.

  1. Compound formula. The Roman numeral in silver(I) gives the metal ion's positive charge, +1. The upper-right superscript shows charge, as in Ag+ and O2−. Two silver ions and one oxide ion sum to zero charge, so the neutral formula unit (smallest whole-number ion ratio) is Ag2O.
    Element formulas. Write the element symbol for a metal, as in Ag. Oxygen, nitrogen and hydrogen gases are diatomic (two atoms per molecule). Hydrogen gas is H2(g).
  2. State symbols. Attach a state symbol from the description. Use (s) for solid, (l) for liquid, (g) for gas, and (aq) for a substance dissolved in water. This gives the skeleton equation before balancing.

    Ag2O(s) → Ag(s) + O2(g)

    Need a formula pause? Review ion formulas, then return to this skeleton.

  3. One oxygen molecule needs two O atoms. Two silver oxide units supply them and also give four Ag atoms. Set the coefficients to show relative particle counts or amounts in moles, the unit for amount of substance. A subscript counts within one formula and stays fixed.

    2Ag2O(s) → 4Ag(s) + O2(g)

  4. Each side has Ag = 4 and O = 2. The terms are all neutral overall and the ratio 2 : 4 : 1 has no common divisor above one.

Apply it to a liquid

Liquid hydrogen peroxide, H2O2, breaks down into liquid water and oxygen gas. Write the full equation. Explain what changing the oxygen subscript in peroxide would do.

Compare the peroxide equation

2H2O2(l) → 2H2O(l) + O2(g)

Changing H2O2 to H2O changes peroxide into the formula for water. Keep both formulas fixed. One peroxide leaves one O after forming water, so two peroxides supply a whole oxygen pair. Each side has H = 4 and O = 4.

Write the equations from scratch

For each description, write a balanced molecular equation with state symbols. Verify each element and the smallest whole-number ratio before opening the comparison.

A solid forms in solution

Aqueous silver(I) nitrate reacts with aqueous potassium phosphate to form solid silver(I) phosphate and aqueous potassium nitrate. Build the formulas yourself. Use the ion lookup if you need to check a charge.

Compare the phosphate equation

3AgNO3(aq) + K3PO4(aq) → Ag3PO4(s) + 3KNO3(aq)

Phosphate has a charge of −3, so each phosphate needs three singly positive ions in its formula unit. That fixes the inner 3. The outside 3 on potassium nitrate counts three units and leaves its formula fixed. Each side has Ag = 3, K = 3, P = 1, N = 3 and O = 13.

Hydrogen from a hydride

Solid calcium hydride, CaH2, reacts with enough liquid water for the calcium hydroxide it forms to stay dissolved. Hydrogen gas also forms.

Compare the hydride equation

CaH2(s) + 2H2O(l) → Ca(OH)2(aq) + 2H2(g)

Each calcium ion needs two hydroxide ions to give zero net charge. The two groups need two O atoms, supplied by two water molecules. Add H from both reactants, 2 + 4 = 6. The two hydroxide groups already count 2 H, so hydrogen gas must account for the other 4 H. The totals on each side are Ca = 1, O = 2 and H = 6.

Hot iron ore

At high temperature, solid iron(III) oxide reacts with hydrogen gas to form solid iron and water vapor. Write your equation before comparing. With one oxide unit, name the element that fixes the water coefficient.

Compare the iron equation

Fe2O3(s) + 3H2(g) → 2Fe(s) + 3H2O(g)

Two iron(III) ions balance three oxide ions. Iron metal is Fe, and hydrogen gas is diatomic. The oxide provides three O atoms, requiring three water molecules and then three hydrogen molecules. The matching totals are Fe = 2, O = 3, H = 6.

A different vapor in a burner

Acetaldehyde vapor, C2H4O, burns completely in oxygen gas, forming carbon dioxide gas and water vapor.

Hint for oxygen in the fuel

Once C and H match, subtract the fuel's O atoms from the product O total. Oxygen gas supplies the rest in pairs.

Compare the acetaldehyde equation

2C2H4O(g) + 5O2(g) → 4CO2(g) + 4H2O(g)

For one fuel molecule, the carbon and hydrogen counts call for two molecules of each product. The products need six O atoms. The fuel supplies one, so oxygen gas must supply five. Multiply the entire intermediate ratio 1 : 5/2 : 2 : 2 by two. The matching totals are C = 4, H = 8, O = 12.

Glossary

Molecular equation
An equation using full formulas, including neutral formulas for dissolved salts.
Reactant
A starting substance on the left of the arrow.
Product
A substance formed on the right of the arrow.
Formula unit
The simplest whole-number unit representing an ionic compound’s composition.
Diatomic
Having two atoms in a molecule.
State symbol
A phase or solution label after a formula. (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas and (aq) dissolved in water.
Skeleton equation
The reactant and product formulas before balancing.
Coefficient
The front multiplier for a whole formula. Omitted means one.
Subscript
A lowered numeral counting the preceding atom or group within one formula. Omitted means one.
Ion
An atom or group of atoms with a net electric charge.
Polyatomic ion
A charged group of bonded atoms.
Catalyst
A substance that speeds a reaction and is regenerated.
