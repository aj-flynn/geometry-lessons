01 · Write the skeleton

Turn words into chemical terms

Reactants go on the left of the arrow. Products go on the right. A skeleton equation records the formulas and states before you decide whether any coefficients need to change.

(s)
solid
(l)
liquid
(g)
gas or vapor
(aq)
dissolved in water

For an elemental solid such as copper metal, write Cu(s). For a diatomic gas, the formula includes two atoms, as in F2(g) for fluorine gas. The name identifies the formula and the description determines the state.

A molecular equation lists substances by their complete formulas. It can include ionic compounds. For a dissolved salt, keep the neutral salt formula with (aq) even though the ions separate in water. A formula unit counts one formula’s worth of ions. In an ionic solid it represents a ratio in the lattice, the repeating arrangement of ions.

Worked example · Start at one

Given. Solid ammonium chloride breaks apart on heating to form ammonia gas and hydrogen chloride gas. Show the forward change described.

Goal. Write a balanced molecular equation with states.

  1. Ammonium, NH4+, and chloride, Cl−, have equal and opposite charges. One of each gives the neutral formula NH4Cl. Use the known ammonia formula NH3. Hydrogen chloride is HCl, with one H and one Cl per molecule.
  2. Write the solid on the left and both gases on the right. Heat is a condition, which can go above the arrow.
  3. Count atoms with an implied coefficient of 1 for every term. The coefficient is the multiplier in front of the formula.

NH4Cl(s) → NH3(g) + HCl(g)

H43 + 1 = 4
Cl11

Every row already matches. The ratio is 1:1:1, so all the coefficients stay implicit. Hydrogen is split across two products. Add both contributions to get the right-hand total.

Quick check · Choose the state from the description

Which pair records bromine vapor and potassium bromide dissolved in water?

Compare your state labels

Use Br2(g) and KBr(aq). Vapor fixes the first state. The words “dissolved in water” fix the second.

02 · Balance the atoms

Count the whole side

A subscript tells you how many atoms of an element are in one molecule or formula unit. A coefficient multiplies the entire formula. For example, 3SO2 counts 3 S atoms and 6 O atoms. Changing SO2 to SO3 would name a different substance.

Worked example · Oxygen in two products

Given. In the roasting of solid zinc sulfide, diatomic oxygen gas reacts to form solid zinc oxide and sulfur dioxide gas. Diatomic means two atoms per molecule.

Goal. Balance the molecular equation using the smallest whole-number ratio.

ZnS(s) + O2(g) → ZnO(s) + SO2(g)

The zinc and sulfide ions have charges +2 and −2. Zinc and oxide ions have charges +2 and −2 as well. Each solid therefore has a 1:1 ion ratio. With one unit of ZnS, one ZnO accounts for its Zn and one SO2 accounts for its S.

  1. Count O on the product side. 1 + 2 = 3 O atoms.
  2. Match 3 O atoms with pairs. 3 ÷ 2 = 3/2 molecules of O2 per formula unit of ZnS as a temporary ratio.
  3. Multiply every coefficient by 2. 2 × (1, 3/2, 1, 1) = (2, 3, 2, 2).

2ZnS(s) + 3O2(g) → 2ZnO(s) + 2SO2(g)

Zn22
S22
O3 × 2 = 62 × 1 + 2 × 2 = 6

All rows match. There is no common factor greater than 1. The fraction was a ratio step. The final equation counts whole molecules and formula units.

Test a shortcut

Start with 1 ZnS and 3 O2 on the left, and 1 ZnO and 1 SO2 on the right. Predict which rows would match if you doubled both products and left the reactants alone.

Compare the three states in words
  1. Starting state. Zn and S both match at 1 each. Oxygen is 6 on the left and 3 on the right.
  2. Double both products. Oxygen now matches at 6. Zinc and sulfur each have 1 on the left and 2 on the right.
  3. Use 2 ZnS on the left to bring both Zn and S to 2. Oxygen stays at 6 on each side. All rows now match.
Quick check · Is doubling necessary?

Solid sulfur, written S(s) for this equation, reacts with O2(g) to give SO2(g). Begin with one unit of each written formula. What does the oxygen count tell you to do?

Compare your oxygen reasoning

One O2 already supplies two O atoms to make one SO2. One S supplies its S atom. There is no odd oxygen total to resolve.

03 · Balance intact groups

Keep a group together when it appears on both sides

A polyatomic ion is a group of atoms with a net charge. When the same group appears intact in formulas on both sides and its atoms are accounted for by those groups, you can use a row for the group. Check the individual atoms at the end.

Worked example · A solid forms from solutions

Given. Aqueous strontium nitrate and aqueous potassium sulfate form solid strontium sulfate and aqueous potassium nitrate. The new solid is a precipitate.

Goal. Use neutral formulas and retain all four substances in the balanced molecular equation.

  • Strontium is Sr2+ and nitrate is NO3−. (+2) + 2(−1) = 0, so the formula is Sr(NO3)2. The outside 2 counts two whole nitrate groups.
  • Potassium is K+ and sulfate is SO42−. 2(+1) + (−2) = 0, so the formula is K2SO4.
  • One strontium ion with one sulfate ion gives SrSO4. One potassium ion with one nitrate ion gives KNO3.

Sr(NO3)2(aq) + K2SO4(aq) → SrSO4(s) + KNO3(aq)

  1. One Sr on each side matches. One sulfate on each side matches.
  2. Two nitrates on the left require a coefficient of 2 for KNO3 on the right.
  3. That same coefficient supplies two K on the right, matching the two K on the left.

Sr(NO3)2(aq) + K2SO4(aq) → SrSO4(s) + 2KNO3(aq)

Consider solid ammonium dichromate breaking down to solid chromium(III) oxide, nitrogen gas, and water vapor. Can you use an ammonium-group row by itself to track N and H on both sides?

04 · Apply the method

Write and check a fresh equation

For each description, write a balanced molecular equation with states on paper. Choose an efficient first row and explain what made it useful. Then compare your work.

  1. Two gases from a solution

    When aqueous ammonium nitrite decomposes, it produces diatomic nitrogen gas and water vapor. The nitrite ion is NO2−.

    Compare your equation

    NH4NO2(aq) → N2(g) + 2H2O(g)

    One ammonium ion and one nitrite ion give a neutral formula. Starting with 1 NH4NO2, its four H atoms require 2 H2O. That uses both O atoms. Count one N in each ion for 2 N on the left, matching one N2 on the right. The description gives water as a vapor.

  2. Gas-phase oxidation

    In a step of nitric acid manufacture, ammonia gas reacts with diatomic oxygen gas to yield nitrogen monoxide gas and water vapor.

    Compare your equation

    4NH3(g) + 5O2(g) → 4NO(g) + 6H2O(g)

    Start with 2 NH3 so the 6 H atoms make a whole number of water molecules. That sets 2 NO and 3 H2O. The O total is 2 + 3 = 5, so the ratio requires 5/2 O2. Double all four coefficients. Check N = 4, H = 12, and O = 10 on each side.

  3. Two solutions produce a solid

    Aqueous barium chloride and aqueous potassium phosphate form solid barium phosphate and aqueous potassium chloride. Barium ions carry charge +2, and phosphate ions are PO43−.

    Compare your equation

    3BaCl2(aq) + 2K3PO4(aq) → Ba3(PO4)2(s) + 6KCl(aq)

    For solid barium phosphate, 3(+2) + 2(−3) = 0. One formula unit of this solid requires three Ba and two phosphate groups. The corresponding reactant coefficients supply six Cl and six K, which fit six KCl. Check Ba = 3, P = 2, O = 8, K = 6, and Cl = 6 on each side.

  4. Iron from an oxide

    Consider the overall reduction of solid iron(III) oxide by carbon monoxide gas to produce solid iron and carbon dioxide gas. Iron(III) means Fe3+. The oxide ion is O2−.

    Compare your equation

    Fe2O3(s) + 3CO(g) → 2Fe(s) + 3CO2(g)

    Use 2 Fe on the right to match the neutral Fe2O3 on the left. Carbon must have the same coefficient in CO and CO2. With that coefficient called x, the oxygen row reads 3 + x = 2x, so x = 3. Check Fe = 2, C = 3, and O = 6 on each side. The overall equation combines the changes into one accounting step.

Read one description and explain why each term has its formula, side and state. Predict which coefficient will be easiest to set first. Add contributions from every term that contains an element, check every row and divide the coefficients by any common factor.

Reference

Glossary

Reactant and product
A starting substance and a substance formed, respectively.
State label
Parentheses indicating solid (s), liquid (l), gas (g) or dissolved in water (aq) under the described conditions.
Skeleton equation
The formulas of the substances on their respective sides before you finish balancing.
Molecular equation
An equation written using complete formulas for the substances, including neutral formulas for dissolved ionic compounds.
Molecule
A discrete group of bonded atoms. A diatomic molecule contains two atoms.
Ion and polyatomic ion
An ion has a net electric charge. A polyatomic ion is an ion with more than one atom.
Formula unit
One formula’s worth of a compound. For an ionic solid, it expresses the relative numbers of the ions.
Subscript and coefficient
A subscript inside a formula counts atoms or groups per unit. A coefficient in front counts units of the entire formula. An omitted number means 1.
Precipitate
A solid that forms from a solution.
Balanced equation
An equation with equal totals for each element and equal total charge on both sides. By convention, write the smallest whole-number coefficient ratio for these reactions.
