Chemistry · Compound labels

Names and formulas

Decide what a formula counts. Then use either charge balance or atom counts to translate between a compound’s name and formula.

Warm up. In O2 and O2−, which small number counts atoms? Which shows charge?

Compare the two symbols

The low 2 is a subscript that counts two oxygen atoms in O2. The raised 2− is a charge that applies to one oxygen ion, a particle with an electrical charge. When a count is one, the subscript is left blank.

Decide what you are counting

Ions in a solid

An ionic compound contains positive and negative ions. Its formula unit gives the smallest whole-number ratio of those ions. Their total charge must be zero.

Atoms in a molecule

A molecular compound contains molecules, separate groups of bonded atoms. A molecular formula gives the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule.

For the simple compounds here, look for a metal with a nonmetal or a named group ion as an ionic clue. Two nonmetals with a count word such as tetra- point to the molecular method. A name ending in -ide can occur in either method. Use the compound type given by your course if the clues are ambiguous.

01 · Formula → name

Find the charge on one metal ion

Given Cu3N, with a nitride ion N3−. Copper can form ions of different charges. Goal Name the compound and identify which copper ion the label describes.

  1. One nitride ion gives a negative total of 1 × (−3) = −3.
  2. To make the compound neutral, the three copper ions must give a positive total of +3.
  3. Divide the positive total by the number of copper ions. (+3) ÷ 3 = +1 per ion, so the ion is Cu+.
  4. Write copper(I) nitride. The Roman numeral (I) reports the charge on each copper ion. Nitride is the name of the negative ion.

Charge arithmetic here uses units of the elementary charge, the size of a proton’s charge. The low 3 on Cu counts the copper ions in the ratio. The (I) in the name describes each one.

Finish a label

The formula card reads Sn3P4. Write the compound’s full name and explain how the two subscripts enter your calculation. Use the lookup if you need a symbol or charge.

Compare the tin label

Four P3− contribute 4 × (−3) = −12. The positive total must be +12. Divide by the three tin ions to get (+12) ÷ 3 = +4 per ion. The label is tin(IV) phosphide.

Retry with a charge claim

A classmate says that three ions with a combined charge of +12 each carry +12. Test that claim by multiplying the claimed charge by three. What charge per ion gives the required total?

Compare your retry

Three at +12 would give +36. Three at +4 give +12.

02 · Ions → formula and name

Balance whole ions

Given Zn2+ and PO43−. Find their names in the ion list. PO43− is a polyatomic ion, a bonded group of atoms that carries one charge for the whole group. Goal Find the smallest neutral ratio and name the compound.

Predict, then test a ratio

One of each gives (+2) + (−3) = −1. How many of each ion would you use to reach zero? Try doubling your proposed counts. Will neutrality alone tell you when to stop?

Zinc ions
Zn2+
Phosphate ions
PO43−

Starting trial: 1 × (+2) + 1 × (−3) = −1. Change the counts to test your prediction.

Compare three trial ratios
  • 1 zinc to 1 phosphate gives +2 − 3 = −1.
  • 3 zinc to 2 phosphate gives +6 − 6 = 0.
  • 6 zinc to 4 phosphate gives +12 − 12 = 0. Divide both counts by two to return to the simplest ratio.

Put the ratio on the label

  1. Match positive and negative totals at their first shared multiple. A zinc ion adds +2 and a phosphate ion adds −3. The smallest matching magnitudes are 6 and 6.
  2. Three zinc ions contribute 3 × (+2) = +6. Two phosphate ions contribute 2 × (−3) = −6.
  3. Write Zn3(PO4)2. The outer 2 repeats the whole phosphate group. Each group keeps its one P and four O atoms.
  4. Write zinc phosphate. Say the positive ion first and keep the group’s name. In this naming system zinc has a fixed 2+ charge, so the label needs no numeral. If a metal can have different charges and the ion is given, turn that given charge into a Roman numeral. A supplied 3+ goes on the name as (III).

Check a new stockroom label

A supplier lists Ti3+ and SO42−. Write the formula and full name. Use the lookup to name both SO42− and SO32−. What distinguishes the two ions even though their charges match?

Compare the formula and name

Two Ti3+ and three SO42− give +6 and −6. Write Ti2(SO4)3, titanium(III) sulfate. The Roman numeral comes from the given titanium charge. Sulfate has four oxygen atoms per ion, while sulfite has three. Both carry charge 2−.

Retry with a doubled batch

If you count four titanium ions and six sulfate ions, is the batch neutral? What ratio goes on the label?

Compare your retry

Yes, +12 and −12 cancel. Reduce 4:6 to 2:3 for the formula unit.

03 · Formula ↔ name

Keep the molecule’s actual counts

In a binary molecular compound, exactly two elements appear. A prefix, a word part at the start of an element name, tells how many atoms of that element are in the molecule.

Read the formula

Given P4S10. Goal Write its molecular name.

  1. Keep 4 phosphorus atoms. Use tetra- with the first element name.
  2. Keep 10 sulfur atoms. Use deca- with the second element’s -ide form, sulfide.
  3. Write tetraphosphorus decasulfide. Reducing 4:10 to 2:5 would halve the count of each atom in the molecule.

Read the name

Given sulfur hexafluoride. Goal Write its molecular formula.

  1. The first element has no prefix, so count one sulfur atom, S.
  2. Hexa- gives six fluorine atoms, F6. Keep the order in the name.
  3. Write SF6. The count of one S is understood.

When the second element has one atom, keep mono-. In Cl2O the single oxygen gives monoxide (mono + oxide with one o dropped), so the name is dichlorine monoxide. Follow your course’s spelling for other prefix–oxide combinations.

Translate in both directions

Write the names of BrF5 and S2O. Explain what happens when a single atom appears in the first position or in the second. Then write the formula for disulfur decafluoride.

Compare the molecular labels

BrF5 is bromine pentafluoride. One Br takes no prefix in the first position and five F take penta-. S2O is disulfur monoxide. Two S take di-, and one O in the second position takes mono-, giving monoxide. Disulfur decafluoride is S2F10. Keep both counts even though they share a factor of two.

Retry with a one-atom ending

In OF2, which element takes a prefix? If you saw monofluoride at the end of a molecular name, how many fluorine atoms would that give?

Compare your retry

Only fluorine takes a prefix in oxygen difluoride. A second-position monofluoride would give one F atom.

04 · Choose your method

Finish two labels

A label printer has Cr2(SO4)3 on one card and dichlorine heptoxide on another. Write the missing name and formula. Explain what told you which method to use and what each number in your completed labels represents.

Get a cue

Look up the symbol Cr and check whether a group in either card is listed as an ion. For a word with a count prefix, read the prefix together with the element it begins.

Compare the completed labels

Chromium(III) sulfate. The metal Cr and the sulfate groups point to charge balance. Three sulfate ions give 3 × (−2) = −6. Divide the balancing +6 by two chromium ions to get +3 per chromium. The (III) records that charge.

Cl2O7. The name’s prefixes give two chlorine atoms and seven oxygen atoms in one molecule. In this spelling heptoxide uses the seven-count prefix hepta-.

Retry by explaining a bad edit

The printer changes (III) to (II) on the first label and replaces the 7 with a 1 on the second. Which edit breaks a charge check? Which edit changes an atom count?

Compare your explanation

Two chromium ions at +2 would give +4, short of the +6 required. On the second label the edit changes seven oxygen atoms to one.

Next time. Before opening the lookup, write what a Roman numeral, a prefix, and a subscript outside parentheses each tell you. On a new label, underline the clue that picks your method and check your answer by translating it back.

Look up only what you need

Match the exact symbol and internal subscripts. Notice how changing the oxygen count distinguishes sulfite and sulfate while leaving the group charge the same. For a variable-charge metal such as copper, tin, titanium or chromium, use the given charge or find it from charge balance.

Glossary

Subscript
A small lower number that counts atoms or groups. An omitted count is one.
Charge
An electrical property. An ion’s superscript, such as 2−, shows the sign and size of its net charge.
Ion
An atom or bonded group with a net electrical charge. A cation is positive and an anion is negative.
Ionic compound
A compound of positive and negative ions with zero total charge.
Formula unit
The simplest ratio of the ions in an ionic compound.
Molecular compound
A substance made of distinct molecules that each contain more than one element.
Roman numeral
In the ionic names here, the numeral in parentheses records the positive charge of one metal ion. I = 1, II = 2, III = 3, IV = 4, V = 5, VI = 6.
Polyatomic ion
A group of bonded atoms with a net charge. Parentheses show which group an outside subscript repeats.
Binary compound
A compound with two distinct elements, even if it has several atoms of either element.
Prefix
A word part attached at the beginning. In these molecular names it gives an atom count.
