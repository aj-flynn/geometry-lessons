Isotope mass and percent error

Use the same kind of data to answer two questions. How much mass does an atom in a mixture have on average? How far is a measured share from its reference?

Before you start

In a group of 100 atoms, 35 are of one kind. Write the share as a fraction and a decimal.

Compare the conversions

35% = 35/100 = 0.35. A percent gives a count per hundred. Use its decimal form when multiplying by an atom’s mass.

Open the glossary for words and units.

1. Give each mass its share

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons. They have different masses. The number after the element symbol, as in Mg-24, counts protons and neutrons. The measured mass is listed separately in unified atomic mass units (u).

The abundance is the share of atoms of one isotope. For average atomic mass, each isotope’s share tells you how much weight to give its mass.

Predict where the dot will go if the share of heavier atoms rises from 25% to 75%. Try your prediction.

25% heavier · 20.50 u average
Compare positions on the scale

At 75% heavier, the mean is 21.50 u, three quarters of the way toward the heavier mass. At 50%, the dot is halfway at 21.00 u. With a single isotope at either end, the average equals that isotope’s mass.

Worked example · A magnesium blend

Givens. This blend was prepared with the atom shares below. Its abundances are chosen for practice.

Magnesium blend
IsotopeMass (u)Share (%)
Mg-2423.98520
Mg-2524.98630
Mg-2625.98350

Goal. Write a numerical setup for the blend’s average atomic mass, then check it by calculating.

  1. Convert the shares to fractional abundances. The decimals 0.20, 0.30 and 0.50 sum to 1.00, so they account for the whole blend.
  2. Keep each mass paired with the share in its row. Multiply, then add the products. The decimals have no unit, so each product is in u.
    mavg =
    (23.985 u) × 0.20
    + (24.986 u) × 0.30
    + (25.983 u) × 0.50

    That expression is the setup. It shows every mass and the weight that goes with it.

  3. Evaluate from the given numbers, then round for reporting.
    mavg = 25.2843 u
    mavg ≈ 25.28 u

    The mean sits between the lightest and heaviest masses. Equal weighting gives about 24.98 u. Compared with equal shares, this blend gives more weight to the heaviest isotope and less to both lighter isotopes. The mean rises.

Check the pairing

Using the same magnesium masses, a second blend has 50% Mg-24, 10% Mg-25 and 40% Mg-26. Write the setup on paper, then enter the average in u, rounded to two decimal places.

Compare the setup
mavg =
(23.985 u) × 0.50
+ (24.986 u) × 0.10
+ (25.983 u) × 0.40
≈ 24.88 u

Equal weighting ignores the new shares. Copying the first setup gives the largest weight to the wrong isotope for this blend.

Retry with a new blend

Use 60% Mg-24, 30% Mg-25 and 10% Mg-26. Write the setup and predict whether the new mean lies above or below the first worked mean.

Compare the retry

Pair the masses with 0.60, 0.30 and 0.10, respectively. The result is about 24.49 u, below the first mean because some of the weight shifted from the heaviest isotope to the lightest.

2. Measure the gap against a reference

Percent error expresses the size of a measurement gap relative to an accepted value, the reference for that quantity. Match the isotope and the quantity before choosing the reference. An abundance needs an abundance for comparison.

Worked example · A sulfur calibration

Givens. A calibration mixture has a certified S-34 abundance of 18.0%. An instrument reports 16.2% S-34 for that mixture.

Goal. Find the percent error of the instrument’s abundance reading.

  1. Find the absolute value of the difference, so a low reading and a high reading can be compared by size.
    |16.2% − 18.0%| = 1.8 pp

    The gap is 1.8 percentage points (pp). That number describes the subtraction of two percentages.

  2. Divide the gap by the accepted value, measured on the same scale. As fractions of all atoms, the gap is 0.018 and the reference is 0.180.
    0.018 ÷ 0.180 = 0.10
  3. Express the resulting ratio as a percent.
    0.10 × 100% = 10%

    The gap is one tenth of the accepted abundance. This size comparison needs a positive accepted abundance. It cannot tell you what caused the gap.

Check the denominator

An argon standard has a certified Ar-38 share of 40.0%. An instrument reports 42.0%. Find the percent error. On paper, mark the value you divide by before you check.

Compare the calculation

The gap is 2.0 percentage points. Divide 0.020 by the reference fraction 0.400 to get 0.050, or 5.0% error. A division by 0.420 would put the reading in the denominator.

Retry with a different reference

A second argon standard has a certified Ar-38 share of 25.0%. The instrument reports 23.0%. Find the percent error and explain why the size of the error changed.

Compare the retry

The gap is again 2.0 percentage points. This time 0.020 ÷ 0.250 = 0.080, or 8.0% error. The same gap is a larger fraction of the smaller reference.

Compare after attempting both

The average uses all three certificate rows.

mavg =
(27.977 u) × 0.100
+ (28.976 u) × 0.700
+ (29.974 u) × 0.200

The Si-30 share on the certificate is 20.0%. The gap between 23.2% and 20.0% is 3.2 percentage points. Using fractions, 0.032 ÷ 0.200 = 0.16, so the error is 16%. Both values refer to the abundance of Si-30.

Back to independent work

  • For an average, check that you paired the entries row by row and that the fractions cover the whole sample. Keep units in the setup.
  • For an error, check that the reading and reference describe the same thing. Label the final result with %.

Next time, before reopening the lesson, sketch where the average would sit if nearly all atoms were the lighter isotope. Then explain how the same gap in percentage points can yield two different percent errors.

Glossary

Isotope
One form of an element’s atoms. Its proton count identifies the element, and its neutron count distinguishes it from other isotopes of that element.
Unified atomic mass unit (u)
A unit equal to one twelfth of the mass of a free, neutral carbon-12 atom at rest in its ground state. It is also called the dalton (Da).
Abundance
The proportion of atoms of a particular isotope. Natural abundance describes the share in naturally occurring material.
Fractional abundance
The isotope’s share as a number from 0 to 1. Divide the numerical percentage by 100 to obtain it.
Average atomic mass
The sum of each isotope mass multiplied by that isotope’s fractional abundance. The value depends on the composition of the material.
Accepted value
The value used as the reference for the same quantity as the measurement.
Absolute value | |
The size of a number without its sign. Both +3 and −3 have an absolute value of 3.
Percentage point (pp)
A unit for the difference between two percentages. A move from 11% to 12% is a gain of 1 percentage point.
Percent error
For a positive accepted value, take the absolute gap between the measured and accepted values, divide by the accepted value, and express the ratio as a percent.
