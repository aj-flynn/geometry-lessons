High school geometry · Compass and straightedge

Build a reflection with two arcs

Use two distances to locate a mirror image, then check what your construction marks prove.

Set a compass by placing its point at one dot and its pencil tip at a second dot. As the pencil sweeps an arc (part of a circle), what distance stays the same?

Check the compass move

The distance from the fixed point to the pencil tip is the radius. Keeping the opening fixed gives every point on that arc the same distance from the center. Use the compass opening to transfer a length.

What has to match?

In a reflection, a point on the mirror line stays in place. For a point off the mirror line, its image is on the other side. A prime mark names the image. For example, N′ is read “N prime.”

The mirror line is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining a point to its image. It crosses at a right angle and cuts the segment into two equal lengths. Every point on that mirror line is the same distance from the original point and its image.

1. Set an opening

A rail and a fitting pin

A fitting needs a second pin on the far side of the rail. The rail runs through U and V. The two pins must have matching reaches from U and matching reaches from V.

For the first reach, place the compass point at U and open the compass to N. The opening is the length UN. Keep it fixed while you draw from U.

2. Sweep the first arc

One distance copied

Keep the compass centered at U. Draw through N and sweep to the other side of the rail. You can draw one long arc or leave two short pieces with the same opening.

Every point on these solid arcs is the right distance from U. Before reading on, say what the compass still needs to match.

3. Mark the other crossing

Two distances copied

Move the compass point to V and set the opening to VN. Sweep through N and across the solid arc below the rail. Mark the second crossing N′. Keep both sets of arcs on your page.

Use a straightedge, a tool for drawing straight lines, to join N and N′ as a check. The arcs give UN = UN′ and VN = VN′. The two openings can be different. Each equality uses one center and its own opening.

By the perpendicular bisector theorem, both U and V lie on the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining N and N′. A unique line goes through U and V, so their rail is that bisector. This proves N′ is the mirror image of N.

Check a tempting shortcut

A candidate across a slanted line

A classmate claims that W is the reflection of J over line m because m crosses halfway between J and W. Is the claim supported? Name the condition you can check and describe a repair.

Return to the J and W diagram
Compare the direction

The midpoint condition is met. The line from J to W must also be perpendicular to m. The segment JW is horizontal, while m is slanted. A horizontal segment would meet a vertical mirror line at a right angle. To repair the construction, match the reaches from two distinct points on m to find the opposite crossing. That locates the point on the true perpendicular through J.

Try a different error

A second candidate

A new claim says Z is the reflection of H over line n. Which part of the test does it pass? Which part does it fail? Explain how the candidate must move to pass both parts.

Return to the H and Z diagram
Compare the two lengths

The right-angle mark confirms the direction. The two lengths from the crossing are 6 and 4, so the crossing is closer to Z than to H. Keep the direction and move Z farther from n until its distance from the crossing is 6 units. In a construction, transfer that length with a compass.

On paper · Finish the mirror path

The path E–K–T

Print this practice page or sketch the same arrangement on blank paper. Use a compass and a straightedge to reflect the whole path E–K–T over the line through E and F.

Label the images of the vertices. Use your straightedge to connect the image path. Keep all construction marks. Write a sentence identifying which marks locate one of your image points.

Return to the blank path
Compare your finished construction

Comparison for the mirror path

If you sketched a different path, compare the marks and relationships in your drawing. E stays fixed, so E′ = E. One way to find K′ is to use openings EK and FK, then mark their arcs’ second crossing. For T′ the openings are ET and FT. The compass opening changes when the center-to-vertex distance changes. Keep the original and image crossings in your work.

Join E to K′ and K′ to T′. If your crossing came out on the original side, follow the same two arcs to their other meeting. If they miss, return each compass point to its center and check the opening against the original vertex.

Check your own drawing

  • Does each new point lie across the mirror line from its original, with the mirror line cutting the joining segment at a right angle and in half?
  • Can you point to the construction marks that locate each image?
  • Did every point on the mirror line stay fixed?

Recall it later

A fresh triangle

Next time, start here before rereading the worked steps. Say how you would construct the missing triangle that reflects XRY across the line through X and Y. Name the compass centers, what sets each opening and which crossing you would keep. Which vertices stay fixed?

Return to the triangle
Compare the plan

Use X as the center with opening XR and Y as the center with opening YR. Mark the meeting across the rail from R as R′. Leave both arcs, then join X to R′ and R′ to Y. The points X and Y are already on the rail and stay fixed.

Glossary

Compass
A drawing tool with one point that stays at a center and one that marks an arc at a fixed distance.
Arc
A portion of a circle.
Radius
The distance from a circle’s center to any point on the circle.
Straightedge
A tool for drawing a straight line through two points. A ruler’s plain edge can serve as a straightedge.
Reflection
A transformation across a line. The line keeps its points fixed and is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining any other point and its image.
Image
The point or figure produced by a transformation. A prime mark, as in N′, labels the image of N.
Perpendicular bisector
A line that crosses a segment at its midpoint and makes a right angle with it. The theorem says a point is on this line exactly when it is the same distance from the segment’s two endpoints.
