Around the edge or across the inside?

Find the distance around a shape, the space inside it, or a side length that is missing.

Start with a sheet of paper. Run your finger around its edge. Now sweep your palm over its face. What did you touch each time? Look up a word if you get stuck.

1. What are you measuring?

The boundary is the edge all the way around a shape. Its length is the perimeter. The area is how much space is inside the boundary.

Given. A game board is a rectangle, a four-sided shape with four square corners. It is 7 centimeters (cm) across and 2 cm tall.

Goal. Find how much tape goes around its edge. Find how many little paper squares cover its face.

Predict. Point to what the tape touches in the picture. Where would the paper squares go? Say what you would count for each.

7 cm + 2 cm + 7 cm + 2 cm = 18 cm

Start at a corner and add the sides in order until you get back. You need 18 cm of tape if the ends meet without overlap.

Switch the job to covering the board

One square that is 1 cm on each side covers 1 square centimeter. The board fits 2 rows of 7 of these squares, with no gaps or overlaps.

2 × 7 = 14 paper squares

The board covers 14 square centimeters. You need 14 paper squares.

When the shape is a square

A square has four sides of the same length. A square name tag with a 3 cm side has four sides of 3 cm. Find the distance around it by adding four lengths of 3 cm.

3 cm + 3 cm + 3 cm + 3 cm = 12 cm

Try it with a display panel

A square display panel has a 6-meter (m) side. A crew puts a row of lights around the edge. How many meters of lights do they need? Sketch your plan. Explain why you chose what to count.

Compare your lights plan

Each of the four sides adds 6 meters. The lights run for 24 meters. If you got 36, you counted squares across the face. Trace the four edges and try again.

2. Follow a path that turns

Given. A tiny robot follows the thick line once. Each little grid edge is 1 step long. Goal. Find how many steps the robot travels.

Trace from the top left corner and go right. Say the length of each straight part when you turn. Stop when you reach that corner again. Count the grid edges along the path. The squares inside the path would measure area.

  • Count the first four parts in steps. 3 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 6
  • Count the last four parts in steps. 1 + 1 + 1 + 3 = 6
  • Put the parts together. 6 steps + 6 steps = 12 steps

Try a different route

A toy train rolls once along the dark track. Each little grid edge is 1 meter. How far does the train go? Write down how you kept track of the pieces.

Compare your track count

Count in meters. From the top left corner, going right, the sum is 4 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 3 + 3 = 14. If you got 11, you counted the squares inside. Mark each straight part of the track and retry.

3. Find a missing length

One side is missing

Given. The triangle has a perimeter of 30 cm. Two sides measure 9 cm and 10 cm. Goal. Find the third side.

  • Add the sides you know. 9 cm + 10 cm = 19 cm
  • Take that length away from the whole. 30 cm − 19 cm = 11 cm
  • Check by putting the side back. 19 cm + 11 cm = 30 cm

Two sides are missing and equal

Given. A stunt net is a rectangle with a perimeter of 42 m. Its top is 6 m wide. Goal. Find the bottom, left and right lengths.

  • Use both sides you know. 6 m + 6 m = 12 m
  • Find what is left for the two dashed sides. 42 m − 12 m = 30 m
  • Share that length equally between the two sides. 30 m ÷ 2 = 15 m
  • Check all four sides. 6 m + 15 m + 6 m + 15 m = 42 m

The bottom is 6 m. The left and right sides are 15 m each.

All sides are equal

Given. This badge has a perimeter of 28 cm. Each side has the same short mark. The matching marks tell us the four lengths are equal. Goal. Find one side length.

  • Share the whole length among the four equal sides. 28 cm ÷ 4 = 7 cm
  • Check four sides of that length. 4 × 7 cm = 28 cm

Each side is 7 cm. You can share a length equally only among sides you know are the same length.

Try choosing what to do

For each example, draw or label the sides and write one line that explains your choice. Then open the check. If a length seems off, add your sides and see whether they make the total you started with.

A. A rigid triangle is made from three strips joined at their ends. The strips total 31 cm. Two of the strips measure 12 cm and 13 cm. How long is the third strip?

Check A

The strips you know use 25 cm, so 31 cm − 25 cm = 6 cm. If you shared the total into three equal parts, look at the two given lengths. They differ. Try adding 6 cm, 12 cm and 13 cm to check the whole.

B. A rectangular sign has a strip of metal around it. The strip is 38 cm long. The left edge of the sign is 7 cm. How long are its top, right and bottom edges?

Check B

The right edge also measures 7 cm. Together the left and right edges use 14 cm. The two matching edges share the remaining 24 cm, so the top and bottom are 12 cm each. If you took away 7 cm only once, label the right side and try again.

C. Eight equal sticks join end to end to make a loop. Laid out in one straight line, the sticks reach 72 cm. How long is one stick?

Check C

Making a loop changes where the sticks point. The total length stays 72 cm. The sticks are equal, so 72 cm ÷ 8 = 9 cm. If you subtracted 8 from 72, notice that 8 counts sticks. Check whether eight sticks of your length use 72 cm.

Put the ideas together

A rectangular stage spot is 7 meters long and 4 meters wide. A crew lays one strip of tape to mark its outline. They also fill the spot with foam squares that are 1 meter on each side.

How many meters of tape do they need? How many foam squares? Make your own sketch. Show what you counted each time and why the units are different.

Compare your sketch and explanation

The tape goes along all four sides. In meters, 7 + 4 + 7 + 4 = 22. The foam covers four rows of seven squares, so 4 × 7 = 28 squares. Tape measures length in meters. The foam covers 28 square meters, one square meter per piece. If you used 7 × 4 for tape, trace your four edges and write a length beside each before adding.

Next time. Before opening the lesson, draw any rectangle. Show where you would count lengths and where you would count squares. Then imagine you know the distance around and just one side. Explain how you could find the other three.

Glossary

Boundary
The edge all the way around a shape.
Perimeter
The total length of the boundary.
Area
How much space a flat shape covers.
Rectangle
A four-sided shape with four square corners. Sides across from each other are equal.
Square
A rectangle whose four sides are equal.
Grid edge
One side of a small square on a grid.
Centimeter (cm) and meter (m)
Units of length. A meter is 100 centimeters.
Square centimeter and square meter
Units of area. A square centimeter is the space in a square 1 cm on each side. A square meter uses a square 1 m on each side.
÷
Divide. Here we use it to share a length equally among sides.
