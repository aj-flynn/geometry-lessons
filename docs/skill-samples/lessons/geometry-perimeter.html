Grade 3 · Shape workshop

Around the edge or across the middle?

Find how far it is around a shape, work out a missing edge, or count what fits inside.

Warm up. There are 18 clips on a desk. Put 5 in a cup and 4 in a tray. How many clips did you move? How many are still on the desk?

There are 4 equal piles with 3 clips in each. How many clips are there? If you share that total equally among 4 people, how many clips does each person get?

Check the warm-up

5 + 4 = 9 clips moved. Take those 9 away from 18. Since 18 − 9 = 9, there are 9 clips still on the desk.

4 × 3 = 12 clips. Split them back into 4 equal piles: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 clips in each pile.

Show your warm-up thinking on scrap paper. Compare at the end.

Pick a picture job. Start with the clue you see, or go in order. After a job, try a few mixed problems. Come back for the rest when you are ready.

A grid or a trip

A sample board

Perimeter is the distance all the way around a shape. Follow its boundary, or outside edge, back to where you started.

For two more examples of adding edges, see the rectangle and pentagon at the top of Math Is Fun’s perimeter guide.

Area is how much flat space a shape covers. One tile that is 1 cm by 1 cm covers one square centimeter. Here, cm is short for centimeter.

Predict first. How many centimeters would a bug travel along the outside of the board? How many 1 cm by 1 cm tiles would cover it? Point to one piece you would count for each job.

Compare after you try

The trip

One trip uses the top, right, bottom and left edges. Count the spaces between marks along the edge; a corner is a turning point, so it adds no length.

3 cm + 2 cm + 3 cm + 2 cm = 10 cm

The cover

To cover the board, count whole squares in rows. Two rows with three squares in each row use six tiles. Each tile covers one square centimeter.

2 × 3 = 6 square centimeters

Fix a mix-up. “I see six squares, so the trip is 6 cm.” Six counts tiles. To fix it, start at a corner. Mark one line piece at a time until you get back. Explain what six really counts in your answer.

Try a new board. Sketch a board with 4 columns and 3 rows of 1 cm squares. How far is it around? How many tiles cover it? What did you count each time?

Check the retry

The trip is 4 cm + 3 cm + 4 cm + 3 cm = 14 cm. Three rows of 4 tiles cover 12 square centimeters. One count uses pieces of a line; the other uses whole squares.

Ready to move on? Tell what gets counted in each job.

One blank side

A sign with a torn label

All four edges together are 22 cm. Find the edge marked with a question mark.

What has to be true? The known edges and the missing edge together must make 22 cm. Take away what you can already count.

  1. Add the three labeled lengths.4 cm + 6 cm + 5 cm = 15 cm
  2. Find the length that is left.22 cm − 15 cm = 7 cm
  3. Check that you have the whole edge again.15 cm + 7 cm = 22 cm

Try a ramp flag

The perimeter is 29 cm. Find the side marked with a question mark. Show what you took away and why.

Compare after you try

The labels add to 8 cm + 11 cm = 19 cm. The third side is 29 cm − 19 cm = 10 cm. If you used 29 cm − 11 cm, you left out the 8 cm edge. Mark both known sides and revise.

Try a fresh strip. A paper triangle has sides of 6 cm, 7 cm and one unknown length. It is 21 cm around. Show what is left for the unknown side.

Check the retry

6 cm + 7 cm = 13 cm is known. Then 21 cm − 13 cm = 8 cm for the third side.

Stop when you can tell what the leftover length stands for.

A rectangle with blanks

A drawer front

The rectangle has four square corners. The outside edge measures 30 cm in all. Find the three edges without labels.

What has to be true? In a rectangle, sides across from each other match. The right edge is 6 cm because it is across from the left edge. The top and bottom match each other.

Picture the four edges laid out in a line, one after the other:

  1. Top
    ?
  2. Right
    6 cm
  3. Bottom
    ?
  4. Left
    6 cm
  1. Add the two lengths you know.6 cm + 6 cm = 12 cm
  2. Take that away from the total. This leaves both blank pieces.30 cm − 12 cm = 18 cm
  3. Share 18 cm equally between the top and bottom.18 cm ÷ 2 = 9 cm

Answer: top 9 cm, right 6 cm, bottom 9 cm. All four sides give 9 cm + 6 cm + 9 cm + 6 cm = 30 cm.

Try a workbench

A rectangular workbench has 90 cm around its top. Its right edge is 18 cm. Find the other three edge lengths. Draw and label them or name them in words. Show why they fit.

Compare after you try

The left edge matches the right, so it is 18 cm. The two known edges total 36 cm. The other two total 90 cm − 36 cm = 54 cm, so the front and back are 27 cm each. If you found 72 cm left, you only took away one known piece. Label its match, take away both, and try again.

Try a different start. A rectangle measures 48 cm around. The top is 15 cm long. Show which side you can label next, then find both other edges.

Check the retry

The bottom is 15 cm. The two remaining edges share 48 cm − 30 cm = 18 cm. Each is 9 cm.

Stop when you can point to both equal pieces of the leftover length.

All sides equal

A triangle emblem

The edge is 27 cm around. All the sides are the same length. Find one side.

What has to be true? The 27 cm is made of three equal side lengths. Count the sides to decide how many equal shares to make.

  1. Split the total into three equal lengths.27 cm ÷ 3 = 9 cm
  2. Check with three copies of 9 cm.9 cm + 9 cm + 9 cm = 27 cm

Go the other way

A square has four equal sides and four square corners. A square stamp has a side length of 5 cm. Use four copies of 5 cm to find the trip around it.

4 × 5 cm = 20 cm

Try a metal badge

A badge has four sides. Each side is the same length. The whole edge is 44 mm long. Find one side length and write a check. (mm means millimeters.)

Compare after you try

44 mm ÷ 4 = 11 mm. Check: 11 mm + 11 mm + 11 mm + 11 mm = 44 mm. If you subtracted 4 from 44, you mixed up the number of sides with a length. Sketch four blanks that together make 44 mm, then share equally.

Try it in reverse. A three-sided tag has three equal edges. One is 12 mm long. How far around the tag is it? Show what you repeated.

Check the retry

12 mm + 12 mm + 12 mm = 36 mm. We know one edge, so we build the total with three copies.

Stop when you can tell when to share a total and when to build one.

Mixed practice

Pick your own method. For each problem, show your thinking with a sketch, numbers, or words. Put units in your answer.

Pause after problem 4 or 7 if you need a break. When you come back, start with the next number.

Use the space below each problem or a separate sheet. Finish the practice before turning to the comparisons.

1. Robot test

A robot follows the edges of a rectangular work mat. The mat is 125 centimeters long and 45 centimeters wide. How far does the robot travel in one complete trip along the edges? Show how you counted.

Compare after you try

The lengths across from each other match. One trip uses 125 cm + 45 cm + 125 cm + 45 cm = 340 cm. A total of 170 cm only gets the robot along two edges. Draw its trip through all four edges.

2. Equipment label

The perimeter of this label is 66 cm. Find the lengths of the left, right and bottom edges. Show how you know.

Compare after you try

The bottom matches the top, so it is 14 cm. Both of these edges total 28 cm. The left and right share 66 cm − 28 cm = 38 cm, so each is 19 cm. A 38 cm answer uses the leftover length twice. Check by adding all four edges.

3. Ink panel

A rectangle on a sign is 9 centimeters long and 8 centimeters wide. Each ink stamp makes a square 1 centimeter by 1 centimeter. How many stamps will fill the rectangle with no gaps or overlaps? Show how you counted.

Compare after you try

There are 8 rows with 9 stamps in a row. That is 8 × 9 = 72 stamps. The stamped area is 72 square centimeters. An answer of 34 counts the distance around the rectangle in centimeters. Sketch one row of stamps and show how the rows fill it.

4. Game token

All the edges of this token are equal. It measures 84 millimeters around. How long is each edge? Use the drawing to show the number of equal lengths.

Compare after you try

The edge from G back to A also counts. There are seven equal pieces, so 84 mm ÷ 7 = 12 mm per edge. Check: 7 × 12 mm = 84 mm. If you forgot the closing edge, trace all the way back to A.

5. Trolley test

Track map

Read the route as text

Post columns are A through I from left to right. Rows are 0 through 6 from top to bottom. Start at B1, the dot. Follow the line through D1, D3, F3, F1, H1, H5 and B5, then back to B1. Each pair of neighboring posts on a line is 1 meter apart.

A lab trolley makes one trip along the thick line. How far does it travel? Show on paper how you counted, or list the lengths of the straight runs. Count the return to the dot, too.

Compare after you try

The straight runs are 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 4, 6 and 4 meters. The total is 24 meters. A total of 20 meters skips two short vertical runs if you go straight across the top instead of following the dip. Touch each turn and tally the spaces until you get back to B1.

6. Bolt pattern

A square bolt pattern has a bolt at each corner. Two bolts along one edge are 17 centimeters apart. How long is a cord that runs bolt to bolt around all the edges? Show how you counted.

Compare after you try

The square has four equal sides. Each trip from bolt to bolt covers 17 cm. Four trips give 17 cm + 17 cm + 17 cm + 17 cm = 68 cm. Two trips cover only half the path.

7. Cutout

This cutout measures 64 cm around. Find the length marked with a question mark. Show why it makes the total fit.

Compare after you try

The five labels add to 18 cm + 7 cm + 11 cm + 6 cm + 9 cm = 51 cm. There is one unknown piece, so it is 64 cm − 51 cm = 13 cm. Add the missing length back: 51 cm + 13 cm = 64 cm. If your number does not pass that check, follow the edges and mark each label you used.

8. Table edges

A carpenter measures the edges around a rectangular tabletop. The total is 74 centimeters. The edge nearest you is 16 centimeters. Find the lengths of the other three edges. Label them in a sketch or name them in words.

Compare after you try

The far edge matches the near edge: 16 cm. The pair totals 32 cm. The left and right share the other 74 cm − 32 cm = 42 cm, so they are 21 cm each. Check: 16 cm + 21 cm + 16 cm + 21 cm = 74 cm.

9. Filter cover

The filter cover is 88 centimeters around. Its edges all have the same length. Find their lengths. Show why your answer fits the whole outline.

Compare after you try

The eight edges include H back to A. They share the total equally: 88 cm ÷ 8 = 11 cm each. Check: 8 × 11 cm = 88 cm.

Check your own work

  • Am I measuring a trip, a missing edge, or a flat space?
  • Did I use every edge or every row I needed, once each?
  • What shows which sides match? Can I build the total again?
  • Did I write a unit that fits?

Try tomorrow

Before you look back at the lesson, draw a rectangle with 3 rows and 8 columns of 1 cm squares. How long is a trip around it? How many squares fill it? Tell what you count for each.

Compare after you try

The outside trip is 8 cm + 3 cm + 8 cm + 3 cm = 22 cm. Filling it takes 3 × 8 = 24 squares, so the area is 24 square centimeters. The first answer counts line pieces. The second counts whole squares.

Glossary

Boundary
The edge that separates the inside of a shape from the outside.
Perimeter
The total length of the boundary. Count the length of every edge once.
Area
The amount of flat space inside a shape. We measure it in square units.
Square centimeter
The area of one square that is 1 centimeter long on each side.
Rectangle
A four-sided shape with four square corners. The top and bottom match, and the left and right match.
Square
A rectangle with four sides of the same length.
