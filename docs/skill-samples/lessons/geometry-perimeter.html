GRADE 3 · MEASURING SHAPES

Follow the edge

Find how far it is around a shape. Find the length of a side with a missing label.

Pick a job. Is the question about covering or going around, one missing length, a rectangle with one label, equal sides, or a path on a grid? Look up a word or unit.

Try it with a book. Put a finger at a corner. Slide along the edges until you get back to that corner. Now put your hand flat on the cover. Which move went around?

Check the two moves

Your finger went around the edge. Your hand covered part of the inside of that outline.

Around or inside?

The boundary is the edge all the way around. The perimeter is its total length. The area is how much space the shape covers.

Look at this mosaic, a picture made of tiles. Each small square has sides 1 inch long. How would you count for a strip along the edge? How would you count for tiles to fill the inside?

Compare the two counts

Strip around. Add the four side lengths, in inches.

4 + 2 + 4 + 2 = 12

The strip is 12 inches long. The opposite sides, the ones across from each other, match in a rectangle. In a square all four sides match, so one side length tells you all four.

Tiles inside. Count two rows of four tiles.

4 + 4 = 8

The area is 8 square inches. Each tile covers one square inch.

Try a tray

A tray is 7 inches long and 3 inches wide. A sheet of paper will cover the bottom. What will you count? Find the amount and tell why.

Check the tray

Cover the bottom with 3 rows of 7 one-inch squares. That is 21 square inches. The word cover tells you to measure the inside. Try a tray that is 9 inches by 2 inches. How much paper will cover the bottom? How long is a strip around the edge?

Find a missing side

A bike flag has a perimeter of 24 centimeters. Two sides are 6 and 8 centimeters long. Find the third side. We write centimeters as cm.

  1. Add the sides you know. 6 cm + 8 cm = 14 cm
  2. Take that length out of the whole edge. 24 cm − 14 cm = 10 cm
  3. The side is 10 cm long. Check that all three sides add to 24 cm.

Try a four-sided badge

The edge is 40 cm all the way around. Three sides measure 14 cm, 8 cm, and 8 cm. Find the last side. Show how you know it fits.

Check the badge

The three known sides add to 30 cm. That leaves 10 cm for the last side. Add the 10 cm back to check the full 40 cm.

Try a new badge. The whole edge is 39 cm. You know three sides are 12 cm, 9 cm, and 11 cm. What is the last length?

Find a pair of missing sides

A rectangular game board has a perimeter of 34 cm. The top is 11 cm long. Find all three unlabeled sides.

  1. The bottom also measures 11 cm. Add that pair. 11 cm + 11 cm = 22 cm
  2. Take the pair out of the whole. 34 cm − 22 cm = 12 cm
  3. The 12 cm is shared by the left and right sides. They are the same length. 12 cm ÷ 2 = 6 cm

The other sides are 11 cm, 6 cm, and 6 cm. All four lengths add to 34 cm.

Try a book cover

A rectangular book cover has a perimeter of 38 cm. Its left edge is 4 cm. Find the top, bottom, and right edge. Tell how you knew which edge was 4 cm.

Check the cover

Right and left match. They use 8 cm together. The top and bottom share the remaining 30 cm, so they are 15 cm each. Try the same idea with 42 cm around and a left edge of 5 cm.

Split the whole into equal sides

A triangle for a game has three sides of the same length. The perimeter is 21 inches. Find the length of one side.

Share the total length among three equal sides.

21 inches ÷ 3 = 7 inches

Each side is 7 inches long. Check by adding 7 inches three times.

Try a maker's sign

A sign has 8 sides. They all have the same length. The sign is 88 cm around. Find each side length. Tell how you chose how many shares to make.

Check the sign

Make one share for each side. Give 10 cm to each of the 8 sides. That uses 80 cm. Share the last 8 cm by giving each side 1 cm more. Each side is 11 cm long. Try again with 8 equal sides and a perimeter of 80 cm.

Count around a bend

A cart follows the dark line from the dot and back to it. Each small grid edge stands for 1 meter. Find the distance the cart rolls. Show how you kept track.

Compare the cart path

From the dot, go up first. Count the short edges on each part, in meters.

4 + 5 + 1 + 3 + 2 + 3 + 1 + 5 = 24

The cart rolls 24 meters. Try going the other way. Then try the robot path below and keep track of its turns.

Choose your own way

Work on paper or say your thinking. For each one, show your steps and say what clue told you what to do.

  1. A rectangular stage mat is 6 feet wide and 9 feet long. Each foam pad covers 1 square foot, the space in a 1-foot-by-1-foot square. How many pads will cover the mat?
  2. A hiker walks 7 meters along the first side of a square path. How far is one full loop?
  3. A toy truck drives once around a rectangular mat 15 cm long and 6 cm wide. How far does it drive?
  4. A triangle has a perimeter of 27 cm. Two sides are 8 cm and 10 cm. Find the third side.
  5. A rectangular stamp design is 46 cm around. Its bottom is 15 cm long. Find the other three side lengths.
  6. A game token has 8 sides of the same length. It is 112 mm (millimeters) around. Find the length of one side.
  7. A robot follows the dark line once. How far does it roll? Each small grid edge stands for 1 meter.
Compare after you try
  1. Cover tells you to fill the mat. Six rows of nine pads use 54 pads.
  2. Square tells you the four sides match. Add four 7-meter sides for 28 meters.
  3. Around tells you to count every edge once. Add 15 cm + 6 cm + 15 cm + 6 cm for 42 cm.
  4. Two known sides use 18 cm. The last side uses the 9 cm left over.
  5. The top and bottom use 30 cm. The other two sides share the 16 cm left over. The three unlabeled lengths are 15 cm, 8 cm, and 8 cm.
  6. Same length tells you to share the whole equally. Share 80 mm first. Each side gets 10 mm. Share the 32 mm left. Each side gets 4 mm more, for 14 mm in all.
  7. The robot moves along 10 parts that are 2 meters long and two parts that are 1 meter long. It rolls 22 meters. Count the last part that takes it back to the dot.

Before you go back

  • Point to the edge or the space inside.
  • Say what you know about each side you use.
  • Check your total and name your unit.

Next time. Draw a rectangle on grid paper. Trace the edge with a finger. Then explain to someone what you would count to cover it. Try it before looking back here.

Words and units

Boundary
The edge all the way around a shape.
Perimeter
The length of that whole edge.
Area
How much space a flat shape covers. Count the unit squares that fill it.
Square inch
The space covered by a square that is 1 inch along each side.
Square foot
The space covered by a square that is 1 foot along each side.
Opposite sides
Sides across from each other. In a rectangle, each side has the same length as the side across from it.
cm and mm
Short ways to write centimeters and millimeters. Both are units for length.
