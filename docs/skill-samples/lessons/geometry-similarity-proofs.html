Geometry · Similarity proofs

Prove it with similar triangles

Use the givens to prove that two triangles are similar (the same shape). Then turn their matching sides into an equation you can justify.

Glossary · Independent practice

Quick start. S lies between F and G. If FS = 9 and SG = 2, what is FG? If H is off the line FG, do ∠SFH and ∠GFH name the same angle?

Check the side and angle

FG = FS + SG = 11 by segment addition. Both names use vertex F and the same two rays. Ray FS is ray FG, and the other ray is FH in both names.

1. Start with side lengths

SAS similarity (side–angle–side) needs two matching side ratios and congruent (equal-measure) included angles, the angles between those sides.

Goal Prove the triangles similar and write the match in order.

  1. UV = UT + TV = 10 by segment addition. The side runs from U to V.
  2. PQ/UV = 5/10 = 1/2 and PR/UW = 7/14 = 1/2. Both ratios compare the first triangle to the second.
  3. ∠QPR and ∠VUW are the included angles, and they are congruent by the given arcs. So △PQR ∼ △UVW by SAS.

Read the correspondence (which parts match) from the order. P ↔ U, Q ↔ V, R ↔ W, so the closing pair is QR ↔ VW.

Suppose the only congruent angle marks were at R and W. Would SAS still apply to the same ratios?

Check the angle change

The compared sides meet at P and U. Marks at R and W would leave the included-angle condition unsupported.

2. Start with angles

AA similarity (angle–angle) needs two pairs of congruent angles. Parallel lines can give one pair. A crossing can give another.

Goal Prove JK · XL = LM · XJ for this figure, with no numerical lengths.

  1. ∠JXK ≅ ∠LXM because they are vertical angles (opposite angles at a crossing).
  2. ∠KJX ≅ ∠MLX because they are alternate interior angles on opposite sides of JL, between the parallel bases.
  3. △JXK ∼ △LXM by AA. The order matches J ↔ L, X ↔ X, and K ↔ M.
  4. JK/LM = XJ/XL because the lengths of corresponding sides of similar triangles are proportional. Each numerator belongs to △JXK.
  5. JK · XL = LM · XJ after multiplying both sides by LM · XL. Both side lengths are positive.

To plan an equal-products proof, pick a ratio of matching sides. Choose the second ratio so clearing denominators produces the requested products.

Two brackets along a rail

Prove the triangles are similar in matching order. Then justify a proportion that uses one vertical and one sloping side from each triangle.

Compare the bracket proof
  1. ∠HJK ≅ ∠LMN because both are right angles.
  2. ∠JHK ≅ ∠MLN because they are alternate interior angles where line HL crosses the parallel sides.
  3. △HJK ∼ △LMN by AA, so HJ/LM = HK/LN because corresponding side lengths are proportional.

3. Build the proof yourself

Match two cutouts

In △UVW, X is between U and V, and UX = 5, XV = 7, UW = 8. In △RST, Y is between R and T, and RY = 3, YT = 9, RS = 18. The marks give ∠VUW ≅ ∠SRT.

A classmate writes △UVW ∼ △RTS. Decide whether that order works. Write and justify the correct statement using only the givens.

Compare your proof

By segment addition, UV = 5 + 7 = 12 and RT = 3 + 9 = 12. The proposed order gives UV/RT = 1 and UW/RS = 4/9.

Pair UV with RS and UW with RT instead. UV/RS = UW/RT = 2/3. The given angles are between those sides, so △UVW ∼ △RST by SAS. The closing sides are VW and ST.

Crossed braces at one corner

Prove PQ · OR = SR · OQ. Justify each angle and side match you use.

Compare your proof
  1. ∠POQ and ∠SOR are the same angle. Ray OP is ray OR, and ray OQ is ray OS.
  2. The given ∠OPQ ≅ ∠OSR supplies the second pair, so △OPQ ∼ △OSR by AA. The order matches Q with R.
  3. Matching sides give PQ/SR = OQ/OR. Multiply by the positive product SR · OR to get PQ · OR = SR · OQ.

Retry. If the arc at S instead marked ∠QSR, could you use the marked pair for the same proof?

Check the new arc

Ray SQ goes away from O. The angle at S in △OSR is ∠OSR. You would need evidence about that angle to use AA here.

Take this back to your work

  • Identify whole side lengths by their endpoints.
  • Choose a theorem the givens support. Locate the included angle for SAS or justify two angle pairs for AA.
  • Write the vertices in matching order. Check both endpoints of each side.
  • For a product goal, justify your proportion and show how you clear its denominators.

Next time. Before looking back, write what SAS and AA each require.

Glossary

Similar triangles (∼)
Triangles with congruent corresponding angles and proportional corresponding side lengths.
Included angle
The angle whose rays contain the two sides being compared. Its vertex is the common endpoint of those sides.
SAS similarity
If two pairs of corresponding side lengths have equal ratios and their included angles are congruent, the triangles are similar.
AA similarity
If two angles of one triangle are congruent to their corresponding angles in another triangle, the triangles are similar.
Correspondence
Matching vertices, angles, or sides. In a similarity statement, corresponding vertices are in the same position in the names.
Vertical angles
Opposite angles formed by two intersecting lines. They are congruent.
Alternate interior angles
Angles between two lines, on opposite sides of a line that crosses them. If the two crossed lines are parallel, these angles are congruent.
Congruent angles (≅)
Angles with equal measures.
Ray
A straight path starting at one point and continuing in one direction. Ray OP starts at O and passes through P.
Equal products
For a/b = c/d with b and d nonzero, multiplying by bd gives a · d = b · c.
