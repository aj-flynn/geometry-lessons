Geometry · Triangle similarity

From a diagram to a proof

Use given lengths or angles to prove triangles are similar (the same shape). Then use that match to prove an equation about their sides.

Quick check · Endpoints, angles and ratios

Sketch these two lines if it helps. Answer before you compare.

  1. F, G and H lie on a line in that order. FG = 13 and GH = 9. Find FH. Do rays FG and FH point in the same direction or opposite directions?
  2. J, G and K lie in that order on a second line through G. Are ∠FGJ and ∠HGK congruent (≅, equal in measure)? Give a reason.
  3. Do 6/9 and 14/21 have the same value? Show how you checked.
Compare the quick check
  1. FH = FG + GH = 22. By segment addition, the two pieces add to the full span. Both rays start at F and point through G in the same direction.
  2. ∠FGJ ≅ ∠HGK. They are vertical angles, opposite angles formed by intersecting lines. Find G in the middle of both angle names, then follow their rays.
  3. 6/9 = 2/3 and 14/21 = 2/3. If the fractions are hard to simplify, multiply both by the product of their denominators. Here 6 · 21 and 14 · 9 both equal 126.

Carry this forward. When a triangle side spans several marked pieces, add the pieces between its endpoints. When you know the full span and one piece, subtract to find the other.

1. Prove a match from lengths

Two triangular frames

Goal. Explain why △NOP ∼ △QOR. The written order matches N with Q, O with O, and P with R. Matching parts are corresponding.

Two known sides in each triangle meet at O. To use side-angle-side (SAS) similarity, establish equal ratios for those two side pairs and equal measures for the angles between them.

  1. OQ = 21 − 7 = 14 because O is between N and Q.
  2. OR = 33 − 11 = 22 because O is between P and R.
  3. NO/OQ = 7/14 = 1/2.
  4. PO/OR = 11/22 = 1/2. Use △NOP on top in both ratios.
  5. ∠NOP ≅ ∠QOR because they are vertical angles, opposite angles made by intersecting lines. These are the angles between the sides in the ratios.
  6. △NOP ∼ △QOR by SAS similarity. The closing edge NP matches QR from the endpoints N ↔ Q and P ↔ R.

Check the side you divided by. NQ is a full span through O. The side of △QOR ends at O, so the denominator is OQ.

Fix a shortcut

Angle-angle (AA) similarity requires two pairs of matching angles with equal measures. Check which facts the following proof actually uses.

In this text-only case, △YJK and △YLM sit on the same two rays from Y. J is between Y and L, and K is between Y and M. YJ = 8, JL = 12, YK = 10 and KM = 17. A student writes, “The triangles share an angle at Y, so they are similar by AA.” Decide whether the claim follows and write a correction.

Compare the correction

The shared angle gives one pair of equal angles. AA similarity needs two. Comparing sides that meet at Y, YJ/YL = 8/20 and YK/YM = 10/27. These differ since 8 · 27 = 216 and 10 · 20 = 200. So the proposed side pairing fails the SAS test. Swapping the side pairing gives 8/27 and 10/20, which also differ.

Revise your explanation to show what the shared angle proves and where the second test fails.

Retry. Keep the same rays, YJ = 8, JL = 12 and YK = 10. Suppose KM = 15. Decide whether △YJK and △YLM are similar and justify your verdict.

Compare the revised lengths

Now YM = 10 + 15 = 25. Both YJ/YL = 8/20 and YK/YM = 10/25 equal 2/5. The angle at Y is between those side pairs, so △YJK ∼ △YLM by SAS.

2. Prove an equation from angles

Two rails on a fan

Goal. Prove JK · NP = JN · KL. Both products use one ray-side length and one rail length. A proportion comparing ray side to ray side and rail to rail will connect them.

  1. ∠KJL ≅ ∠NJP because both use the same two rays from J.
  2. ∠JKL ≅ ∠JNP because they are corresponding angles on parallel lines. They sit in the same position where line JN crosses each rail.
  3. △JKL ∼ △JNP by AA. J ↔ J, K ↔ N, and L ↔ P.
  4. JK/JN = KL/NP because corresponding side lengths of similar triangles are proportional. The first triangle is on top in both fractions.
  5. JK · NP = JN · KL. Multiply both sides by JN · NP, which is positive, and cancel the denominators.

Check the order. JK/JN = NP/KL flips just one fraction. Keep the same triangle in the numerator on both sides of the equation.

Your turn · Overlapping braces

Four pieces on two arms

Write an ordered similarity statement for △UMV and the triangle formed by brace WX and the two arms. Explain why your statement must hold. Name the facts that justify the theorem you choose.

Return to the brace diagram
Compare the brace explanation

MW = MU + UW = 30 and MV = MX + XV = 20. Then MU/MX = 10/15 = 2/3 and MV/MW = 20/30 = 2/3. ∠UMV ≅ ∠XMW because both use the same two rays from M, in reverse order. Thus △UMV ∼ △XMW by SAS. U ↔ X and V ↔ W, so UV ↔ XW.

If you paired U with W because both are on the same ray, your ratios would be 10/30 and 20/15. Test the pairings of sides that meet at M and revise the triangle order to reflect the common ratio.

Your turn · Crossed cables

Cables between parallel tracks

Prove TO · VU = OU · TS. Write a paragraph or a statement-and-reason proof. Justify any angle or length relation you use and show the step that gives the product equality.

Return to the cable diagram
Compare the cable proof
  1. ∠TOS ≅ ∠UOV because the cables intersect at O and these are vertical angles.
  2. ∠OTS ≅ ∠OUV because these are alternate interior angles. They lie between the parallel tracks on opposite sides of cable TU.
  3. △TOS ∼ △UOV by AA. In order, T ↔ U, O ↔ O, and S ↔ V.
  4. TO/OU = TS/VU because corresponding sides of similar triangles have proportional lengths.
  5. TO · VU = OU · TS. Multiply both sides by the positive product OU · VU.

Repair a flipped ratio. TO/OU = VU/TS puts the upper triangle on top in the first fraction and the lower triangle on top in the second. Put TS on top in the second fraction, then multiply both sides by OU · VU. In your own proof, underline the matching sides in the proportion and write out what cancels.

Retry the product step

In a fresh text-only case, △HBD ∼ △KWF. Prove HB · WF = KW · BD. Write a proportion from the triangle order and show what cancels.

Compare the product retry

H ↔ K, B ↔ W and D ↔ F, so HB/KW = BD/WF. Multiply both sides by KW · WF. KW cancels on the left, and WF cancels on the right, giving HB · WF = KW · BD.

Return checklist

  • Trace each side between the endpoints in its triangle name.
  • Give a reason for every equal angle. Check which side pairs meet there.
  • Read the vertex order before writing ratios. If the goal is a product, show how the denominators cancel.

Recall this later

On a return visit, try this text-only case from memory. In two separate triangles, HG = 9, HF = 14, ZY = 18 and ZX = 28. The angles ∠GHF and ∠YZX are congruent. Write an ordered similarity statement and one proportion involving GF and YX. Justify both.

Compare the recall case

HG/ZY = 9/18 = 1/2 and HF/ZX = 14/28 = 1/2. The given equal angles are between those side pairs, so △GHF ∼ △YZX by SAS. G ↔ Y and F ↔ X, so GF/YX = HG/ZY by corresponding sides.

Glossary

Similar triangles (∼)
Triangles with equal matching angles and one common ratio for their matching side lengths.
Congruent angles (≅)
Angles with equal measures, even if they face different directions.
Segment addition
If B is between A and C on a line, AB + BC = AC.
Corresponding parts
Matching vertices, angles or sides. In a similarity statement, match vertices by position in the names. Match sides by both endpoints.
SAS similarity
Side-angle-side. If two pairs of corresponding sides have equal length ratios and their included angles are congruent (≅, equal in measure), the triangles are similar. The included angle lies between the two sides used.
Vertical angles
Opposite angles formed by two intersecting lines. Their measures are equal.
AA similarity
Angle-angle. If two pairs of corresponding angles are congruent, the triangles are similar.
Corresponding angles on parallel lines
When a third line cuts two parallel lines (∥), angles in the same relative position at the two intersections are congruent.
Alternate interior angles
Angles between the two lines on opposite sides of the line that crosses them. They are congruent when the two lines are parallel.
