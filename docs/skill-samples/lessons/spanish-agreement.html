Beginner Spanish · Backstage workshop

Describe it in Spanish

A crew needs labels for its props and short introductions for its program. Choose a detail that fits, then make the Spanish words match what you describe.

Look up a grammar term

Before you start

El telón y las sillas means “the curtain and the chairs.” Which phrase names more than one thing? For just one chair, which “the” would you use, el or la?

Compare the warm-up

Las sillas names more than one chair. El telón names one curtain. The articles (words like “the”) el and las help you tell them apart. For one chair, las sillas changes to la silla. Both las and la are feminine. Look for the article if you’re unsure of a noun’s group.

Comparisons start after the lesson. Try each task before turning to them.

Pick a detail. Then match it.

A lamp on the stage

  1. Find the thing you’re describing. The noun (a word that names something) is lámpara. Del escenario tells you which lamp.
  2. Choose what you want to say about it. The lamp is yellow, so use a form of amarillo (yellow). This describing word is an adjective.
  3. Look at the word before the noun. The article la (“the”) marks feminine gender, the noun’s grammar group.
  4. Count, then pick the ending. There’s one lamp, so the number is singular. For this adjective, feminine singular takes -a.

La lámpara del escenario es amarilla.

This match is called agreement. The description can sit across from the noun, after es. It still matches la lámpara.

What changes when the noun changes?

For these descriptions, use a form of ser (to be). In these examples, you’re talking about the props. Use es for one prop and son for more than one.

  • Keep the color. Change just the number. La lámpara es amarilla → Las lámparas son amarillas.
  • Go back to one. Use a masculine noun with that same color. La lámpara es amarilla → El foco es amarillo. El foco means “the spotlight” here.
Endings for adjectives like amarillo
Noun groupOneMore than one
Masculineel → -olos → -os
Femininela → -alas → -as

Some adjectives use the same form for both gender groups. For example, verde (green) becomes verdes in the plural. Azul (blue) becomes azules. Learn each pattern with a sample word.

Try one ending

The workshop just printed a batch of new signs. Finish the line with the correct form of nuevo (new).

Las señales del teatro son ____.

Las señales del teatro means “the theater’s signs.”

Which form fits the sentence?

Compare the ending

Nuevas fits las señales, feminine and plural. Del teatro tells you where the signs belong. If you wrote nueva, keep the feminine -a and add -s for more than one. Point to the noun and revise your line before trying the maps.

Try a different noun

The museum’s new maps have just arrived. Finish Los mapas del museo son ____. Use the same word with the ending that fits the noun. Los mapas means “the maps.”

Compare the new case

Los mapas del museo son nuevos. Los tells you that mapas is masculine plural, even though mapa ends in -a.

Read the whole description

El foco no es redondo. Es cuadrado. The spotlight is square. No es (“isn’t”) rules out the first shape. The second es still refers to the spotlight.

La tela es suave y ligera. The fabric is soft and light. When y (“and”) joins two details about the same thing, check both against that noun. Suave stays the same for either gender. Ligera uses the feminine ending for la tela.

Try a new line. A prop suitcase is heavy and has a hard shell that doesn’t bend. Finish La maleta no es ligera. Es ____ y ____. Use forms of pesado (heavy) and rígido (rigid).

Compare the suitcase line

La maleta no es ligera. Es pesada y rígida. The second sentence describes the same suitcase. Both adjectives take -a for la maleta. You can swap their order.

Write the prop labels

Inventory for the puppet show

Tray 1 has a purple dragon mask on a yellow cape. Tray 2 has two drums of the same size, red on the left and white on the right.

Finish both Spanish labels on paper. Use the picture and the words in each label. For each blank, jot down which prop you described and what helped you choose.

Colors to choose from · amarillo yellow, azul blue, blanco white, morado purple, rojo red, verde green.

  1. La máscara del dragón es ____. The dragon mask
  2. El tambor del desfile no es rojo. Es ____. The parade drum
Back to the prop trays
Compare the prop labels
  1. La máscara del dragón es morada. The mask is purple. Match máscara, even with dragón closer to the blank.
  2. El tambor del desfile no es rojo. Es blanco. No es rojo rules out the red drum. The other drum is white. Use -o to match el tambor.

Find the noun when you see “my”

At a rehearsal, two aunts wrap every glass before packing it. Their care supports Mis tías son cuidadosas (“My aunts are careful”). Mis means “my” with more than one person or thing. To check gender, look at the noun. Try it with “the” first: las tías → cuidadosas. Keep that adjective ending with mis tías.

In son muy cuidadosas, muy means “very.” The adjective still matches the noun.

Caption the rehearsal

Three moments backstage

In scene 1, two crew mates make different puppets from scraps. In scene 2, two cousins hold a chair beside a stack of chairs and a row of stools. In scene 3, Omar stands about a head taller than the other adult and has red-orange hair.

Complete the Spanish captions on paper. For the two groups, choose a trait their actions support. Give two visible details for Omar.

Words to choose from · alto tall, bajo short, creativo creative, desordenado messy, moreno dark-haired here, ordenado organized, pelirrojo red-haired.

  1. Mis compañeras son muy ____. My crew mates
  2. Mis primos son ____. My cousins
  3. Omar es ____ y ____. Omar
Back to the rehearsal scenes
Compare the rehearsal captions
  1. Mis compañeras son muy creativas. The different puppets made from scraps support this idea. Use -as for compañeras. Muy tells how strongly the trait applies.
  2. Mis primos son ordenados. The chairs are stacked and the stools are lined up. Primos calls for -os. With mis, check the noun for gender.
  3. Omar es alto y pelirrojo. His height next to the other adult and his hair are the clues. Omar uses masculine forms here, so both adjectives take -o. Either order works.

Another description can work if you can point to a clue for it and the form agrees.

Describe a person

Find the subject (who the sentence is about). When Lía speaks about herself, she says Soy curiosa (“I’m curious”). Someone introducing Lía says Lía es curiosa (“Lía is curious”). The person being described stays the same, so curiosa stays the same. The verb changes because the sentence moves from “I am” to “Lía is.” Use the verb form that fits who the sentence is about.

Soy already signals “I,” so you can leave out yo when the context is clear. Soy curioso uses the masculine form. Soy paciente (“I’m patient”) uses an adjective with the same form for either gender.

Try it with a new speaker. Teo says Soy tranquilo (“I’m calm”). What would you say to tell a guest that Teo is calm?

Compare talking about Teo

Teo es tranquilo. Use es when you talk about Teo. He is still the person you’re describing, so tranquilo keeps the same form.

On your own · Write three lines for the program

You can pause here. On your next visit, start with this program.

You’re hosting the puppet show. The audience needs a quick introduction to the crew. Write three complete Spanish sentences on paper, one per person. Give at least one trait per sentence and use two traits in one of them.

  • Inés, your cousin. She builds flowers out of egg cartons for the set. She also takes time to show new helpers how to make them.
  • Marcos, your friend. He keeps the sound cables sorted and puts name tags on the cases. He always has a joke ready before the show.
  • The host, speaking in your own voice. Pick a trait that fits you or a host you make up.
Look up a trait if you need a word

Creativo creative, paciente patient, ordenado organized, gracioso funny, curioso curious.

Compare with one possible program
  • Inés es creativa y paciente. Both traits fit what Inés does. Creativa matches her. Paciente works for either gender.
  • Marcos es ordenado. The labels and sorted cables support this choice. The jokes also support gracioso.
  • Soy curioso. Here a fictional host uses a masculine adjective. Choose a word and a form that fit the person speaking in your own line.

In the first two lines you’re talking about one other person, so use es. In the last, the host says soy because the host is speaking about themself.

If you wrote Inés soy creativo, make two repairs. Use es because you are talking about Inés. Use creativa because she is the person you’re describing. Reread your three lines and fix any similar mismatch.

Check your own descriptions

  1. Point to the word that names who or what you’re describing.
  2. Check that your detail fits the clue. If you see no, work out which idea it rules out.
  3. If you’re writing the whole sentence, choose the form of ser. Check the gender and number of each adjective.

Tomorrow, before you look back. Say “The doors are blue” in Spanish using las puertas and a form of azul. Then choose someone you know and give a line about them. Give a second line about yourself using a different adjective.

Compare the recall task

Las puertas son azules. Son goes with the plural subject. The plural of azul is azules for both gender groups. Use es for the person you chose and soy for your own line. Check each adjective against the person it describes.

Glossary

Noun
A word that names a person, place, thing or idea.
Article
A short word before a noun. Here, el, la, los, and las all mean “the” and help show the noun’s gender and number.
Adjective
A word that describes someone or something.
Grammatical gender
A Spanish noun’s grammar group, masculine or feminine.
Grammatical number
Singular for one, plural for more than one in these examples.
Agreement
Matching features across words. Spanish adjectives match the gender and number of what they describe. Some use the same form for both genders.
Ser
A Spanish verb that means “to be.” This lesson uses ser for traits and uses the present forms soy, es, and son.
Subject
The person or thing the verb is about. Sometimes the verb lets you tell who the subject is without naming them.
