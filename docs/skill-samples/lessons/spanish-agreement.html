First-year Spanish · Field notes 01

What’s it like?

Help a theater crew label its props and introduce its members. You’ll use clues to choose a description, then fit the Spanish words to what you’re describing.

Glossary · Crew challenge

01 / Read the clue

Choose the meaning first

An adjective is a describing word. For la ficha (the token), “round” describes its shape. The noun is the word that names the thing.

La ficha es redonda y gris. The token is round and gray.

Y means “and.” Both adjectives describe la ficha. Check both meanings, then check both forms. Here, redondo changes to redonda to fit la ficha. The color word gris has the same singular form with el or la.

Read a correction

Someone put the wrong color on a sign. The crew sees that the sign is black and writes this.

El cartel no es blanco. Es negro. The sign isn’t white. It’s black.

No before es rules out blanco (white). The next sentence gives the supported color, negro (black). A rejected color alone can’t tell you the right color.

Make a stock note

The inventory says La cuerda no es corta. (The rope isn’t short.) A tape measure shows it’s long, and a tag says it’s made of a flexible material. Write a new label that includes both supported traits.

Word help

La cuerda means “the rope,” largo means “long,” and flexible means “flexible.”

Compare your stock note

La cuerda es larga y flexible. The measurement supports larga. The material tag supports flexible. Corta is the trait the note rules out. Both describing words go with la cuerda.

Retry with a tag for a different rope. It rules out “long,” and your measurement shows “short.” Give only the trait you can support.

Compare the retry

La cuerda es corta. The measurement supports “short.” This tag gives you one supported trait.

02 / Fit the words

Match the thing you describe

Gender is a grammar category. In these examples el or los marks masculine, and la or las marks feminine. Number tells you one (singular) or more than one (plural). Agreement means the describing word fits both.

Use the noun for the thing being described. In las ruedas del carro (the wheels of the cart), las ruedas tells you which things you mean. Del carro just tells you which wheels.

Switch one label at a time

Picture a stage curtain. Use either el telón or la cortina (both mean “the curtain” here). The cloth is always liso (smooth) and resistente (sturdy).

Start with one telón. Predict what changes if you call it cortina. Then predict what changes if there are two curtains.

Start: El telón es liso y resistente.

El telón es liso y resistente.

One curtain. The word for it is masculine.

Compare all four labels
One · masculine

El telón es liso y resistente.

One · feminine

La cortina es lisa y resistente.

Two · masculine

Los telones son lisos y resistentes.

Two · feminine

Las cortinas son lisas y resistentes.

The noun sets the gender. Liso changes with gender, but resistente uses the same gender form. Plural changes both adjectives and switches es to son.

The ending depends on the word. Try these patterns when you check a label.

  • Liso, lisa, lisos, lisas. This -o adjective has four forms.
  • Resistente, resistentes. This -e adjective takes -s in the plural. Idealista (idealistic) also keeps one form for both genders and adds -s in the plural.
  • Gris, grises. Many adjectives ending in a consonant, like gris, keep the same form for both genders and add -es in the plural. Some have other patterns. Check a dictionary when you meet a new one.

Pick the matching label

The cart has two wheels, both very narrow. Say what the wheels are like in Spanish before comparing. Las ruedas del carro means “the wheels of the cart,” muy means “very,” and estrecho means “narrow.”

Which label fits your description?

Compare your label

Las ruedas del carro son muy estrechas. The adjective describes las ruedas (feminine plural), so it takes -as. Muy adds intensity and stays the same.

Try a new label after checking

The openings in a new set piece are gray. Describe las aberturas (the openings), using the color word gris.

Compare the openings

Las aberturas son grises. There’s more than one opening. Gris adds -es in the plural and has no separate feminine form.

03 / Change who’s speaking

Use ser for a trait

Ser is a Spanish verb that can mean “to be.” Here it links the subject (who or what the sentence is about) to a characteristic. Think “what this person or thing is like.” Choose the verb from the subject and the adjective from the person or thing described.

On mic · Nora

«Soy meticulosa».

“I’m meticulous.” Nora takes great care with details. She’s talking about herself, so she uses soy. She can leave out yo (I) when it’s clear that she’s speaking about herself.

Crew announcement

Nora es meticulosa.

“Nora is meticulous.” Now the announcer is talking about Nora, so the verb is es. The adjective still describes Nora and keeps its feminine singular form.

For “I am,” use soy. When talking about one other person or thing, use es. When describing two or more others together, use son. Compare Nora y Eva son meticulosas. The ending -as describes both women.

Give the line to the right speaker

Mateo double-checks the props before every show. The stage manager calls him cuidadoso (careful). Say the line Mateo could use to tell a new crew member the same thing about himself.

Which line can Mateo say?

Compare Mateo’s line

Soy cuidadoso. Mateo is speaking about himself, so the verb is soy. The adjective describes Mateo, so it ends in -o. Yo soy cuidadoso. also works.

Switch from one voice to the crew announcement

New fact. Rosa and Alba both double-check the props. Say what they are like as a pair.

Compare the announcement

Rosa y Alba son cuidadosas. Two people called for son. The description fits both women, so cuidadoso ends in -as.

04 / Take the cue

Send the next crew a handoff

Write or say these lines privately. Start with the message the new crew needs. Open the help only if you need a word.

  1. Correct the prop list

    The old list calls the cases striped. Write a correction that rules out the old pattern and describes both the shape and the actual pattern. Explain which word in your sentence controls the adjective endings.

    Word help for the prop list

    Las cajas means “the cases,” de rayas means “striped,” cuadrado means “square,” and de lunares means “with polka dots.” The phrases with de stay the same here.

    Compare one correction

    Las cajas no son de rayas. Son cuadradas y de lunares. The dots rule out stripes. Cuadradas fits las cajas, feminine plural. The phrase de lunares names the pattern. You could also split the two supported traits into separate sentences.

  2. Introduce the crew

    Luz and Tomás are fictional crew members. Luz calmly waits while new people learn the cues. Tomás sorts every prop into labeled bins so anyone can find it. The lighting technician keeps trying new setups until a tricky light works. Write Luz’s own line about herself, then write two lines for the announcer, one about Tomás and one about the technician. Refer to the technician by her job. Choose a trait supported by each fact.

    Word help for the crew lines

    Paciente means “patient,” organizado means “organized,” and ordenado means “tidy.” La técnica de luces means “the lighting technician.” Perseverante means “persistent.”

    Compare a possible introduction

    Luz says Soy paciente. She uses soy because she’s speaking about herself. The announcer says Tomás es organizado. The verb fits a statement about one other person. The adjective describes Tomás and takes the -o form. The announcer also says La técnica de luces es perseverante. The subject names her by her role, so the verb is still es. The adjective perseverante has the same form for both genders here. Yo soy paciente. and Tomás es ordenado. also fit. Check your own words for meaning, then verb and ending.

Keep the three questions

  1. What detail supports my description? If there’s a no, what does it rule out?
  2. Who or what is the sentence about? Which form of ser fits?
  3. What do my describing words describe? How does each word show agreement?

Next time, before you look back, imagine a fictional sound technician. Give them a line about themself, then say the same thing as the announcer. Explain what had to change and what stayed the same. If your first try feels uncertain, revisit the two voices above and try a new trait.

Glossary

Noun
A word that names a person, place, thing, or idea.
Adjective
A word that describes a noun or someone or something already understood from context.
Subject
Who or what the sentence is about. Spanish can leave the subject word out when context and the verb make it clear.
Gender
In this lesson, a noun’s masculine or feminine grammar category.
Number
Singular means one. Plural means more than one.
Agreement
Matching an adjective to what it describes in gender and number. Some forms do double duty.
Ser
“To be,” as used here to describe a characteristic. The verb forms we practiced are soy (I am), es (he, she, or it is), and son (they are).
No · muy · y
“Not,” “very,” and “and.” These words serve different jobs from adjectives.
