Spanish · Descriptions

Describe it in Spanish

Pick a word that fits what you know, then make its ending fit who or what you’re describing.

Before you start

Which means more than one, el foco (the spotlight) or los focos (the spotlights)?

Check the clue

Los focos means more than one. Here el points to one, and los points to more than one. The noun changes too.

Go to the glossary

1. Find the thing you describe

An adjective is a word that tells what someone or something is like. At a theater, a volunteer says that the curtain is red. The adjective for “red” has to fit the subject, the person or thing the sentence is about.

La cortina del teatro es roja.

  1. What is red? The curtain, la cortina. The words del teatro (of the theater) tell you which curtain.
  2. La signals feminine and singular (one). This adjective has four forms. Pick roja.
  3. Es means “is” here. It goes between the subject and the description.

Agreement means matching the adjective to the described noun’s gender (masculine or feminine) and number (one or more than one). The article, a word like el or las, often tells you both.

Red · four forms
el foco rojo · los focos rojos
la cortina roja · las cortinas rojas
Green · same for either gender
el foco verde · los focos verdes
la cortina verde · las cortinas verdes

Keep the -e in verde. Add -s for plural. Other adjectives have their own patterns. For example, “punctual” is puntual for either gender and puntuales in the plural.

Try one change

The theater has two red curtains now. Predict what goes in Las cortinas del teatro son ___.

Choose the ending

Compare your prediction

Rojas matches feminine plural. Roja describes one curtain. Rojos is masculine plural. Try again starting from the article las.

Retry with a new color. If both curtains are green, what changes?

Compare the new color

Las cortinas del teatro son verdes. Only the color word changes. Verde keeps the -e and takes -s for two.

2. Use the clue before choosing the word

Two rehearsal rooms have a different size. The east room fits four people and the west room fits twenty. The director points to the west room.

La sala no es pequeña. Es grande.

No es pequeña rules out “small.” The number of seats, compared with the other room, supports grande (large). The word sala (room) is still the subject of the second sentence, even though it is left out.

When two words fit the same subject, join them with y (“and”). If the west room also has large windows, you could say La sala es grande y luminosa. Grande keeps its -e and luminosa (bright) takes -a. Check each adjective on its own.

Check the evidence

A map key says a hall with six windows is bright, and a hall with none is dark. The west hall has six windows. Which fits El pasillo oeste es ___. (pasillo means hall)?

Choose a word that fits

Compare your prediction

Luminoso means bright here and matches el pasillo. Oscuro misses the window clue and luminosa misses the gender clue. Check both clues and try again.

Try the other hall. The east hall has no windows. Replace the adjective with one that fits this hall.

Compare the other hall

El pasillo este es oscuro. You used a different meaning and kept the masculine singular ending.

3. Choose the form of ser

Use ser (“to be”) here to describe a trait, such as being calm or kind. The verb is the word that connects the subject to the description. Pick its form by asking who is being described.

I am
yo soy
He, she, or one named person is
él es · ella es · Inés es
They or several named people are
ellos son · ellas son · Inés y Joel son

When Inés describes herself, she says Soy tranquila. When Joel describes Inés, he says Inés es tranquila. The verb changes with who is speaking about whom. The adjective still describes Inés. If both are calm, Inés y Joel son tranquilos uses the usual masculine plural form for a mixed group.

Switch speakers

Joel says Soy puntual. Say it as a description of Joel, then of Inés and Joel together.

Compare both versions

Joel es puntual. Then Inés y Joel son puntuales. With two people, the verb is son and the adjective adds -es.

Retry. Try the two versions again with amable (kind). Its plural is amables.

Your turn · Write for the stage crew

Help a crew member find the right props, the objects used on stage. Look at this drawing of a storage rack and write the missing adjectives. You can write here or on paper.

Objects
el marco = frame · la cinta = ribbon · la caja = box
Shape and size
ancho, ancha, anchos, anchas = wide · estrecho, estrecha, estrechos, estrechas = narrow · sencillo, sencilla, sencillos, sencillas = plain or simple
largo, larga, largos, largas = long · corto, corta, cortos, cortas = short
Color
azul, azules = blue · verde, verdes = green · rojo, roja, rojos, rojas = red
Compare the labels after writing
  • A. Es ancho. The front frame is wide and marco is masculine singular.
  • B. Las cintas de la caja son azules. Blue describes the ribbons. Green describes the box. Both genders use the same plural form of azul.
  • C. Las dos cintas son cortas y verdes. Short and green both describe the two on the left. Either order works. A different adjective works if the drawing supports it and its form fits las cintas.
  • Extra label. Los marcos son sencillos. Both frames have plain edges. Son and sencillos fit los marcos.

For a retry, choose the long ribbon in C and write a new line giving its length and color.

Compare the single ribbon

La cinta es larga y roja. The verb and both adjectives fit one ribbon.

Write a rehearsal note

Imagine you’re Remi, a stage manager. You arrive on time and explain things kindly. Vera, the lighting lead, rarely gets flustered. Nico, the sound lead, invents unusual sound effects. Write three short sentences in Spanish introducing the crew to a visiting director. Describe Remi in your own voice as that character, and describe Vera and Nico by name. Choose one fitting trait for each.

Trait bank. puntual (punctual), amable (kind), tranquilo, tranquila (calm), creativo, creativa (creative). For the two named roles, use ella for Vera and él for Nico if you use a pronoun. For Remi, both adjectives that fit the clues have the same form for either gender.

Explain a choice. Point to one clue for a trait. Then explain why the verb in that sentence fits. Would you check the clue, the verb, or the ending first if your description didn’t match the scene?

Compare your note after writing

Soy puntual. Vera es tranquila. Nico es creativo.

  • Meaning. The note matches arriving on time, staying calm, and inventing sounds. Soy amable also fits Remi.
  • Form. Use soy when Remi speaks about themself. Use es for each named person. Check tranquila for Vera and creativo for Nico.
  • Variation. Yo soy puntual also works. A name makes the first mention clear. You can use a pronoun later once the reader knows whom you mean.

Retry. Write just Vera’s line as if she were introducing herself. Keep the same trait.

Compare Vera’s line

Soy tranquila. The speaker is now Vera. The adjective still describes Vera.

Glossary

Adjective
A word that describes a person or thing.
Agreement
Matching the form of a word to another word in the sentence. In this lesson an adjective matches the gender and number of what it describes.
Article
A word such as el, la, los or las (the).
Gender
A grammar category. Spanish nouns have a grammatical gender, such as feminine la cortina or masculine el marco.
Number
Singular means one. Plural means more than one.
Subject
Who or what the sentence is about in these descriptions. You can often tell who the subject is even if the sentence leaves it out.
Verb
A word that expresses an action or a state. Here the verb ser connects the subject to a description.
