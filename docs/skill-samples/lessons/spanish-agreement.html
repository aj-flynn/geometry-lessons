Describing things in Spanish

Pick a word that fits the clue. Then make the description fit the Spanish sentence.

Glossary

1. Pick a description you can support

You’re making labels for a repair exhibit. A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea. An adjective describes a noun. In “thick rope,” rope names the thing and thick tells you something about it.

Quick start · Which word names the sample?

Say the noun and adjective in “thick rope” before checking.

Check

Rope names the sample. Thick describes it.

The width across the cut end supports grueso (thick). A price tag would help you decide whether caro (expensive) fits. In a scene with several clues, more than one description may fit. Ask yourself what you saw or learned that supports each one. A picture can show a visible detail. To describe someone's usual behavior, use a fact you know about what they do.

Use a different clue

A repair note says a strip bends easily. Which meaning does that clue support?

Choose the adjective by its meaning.

Compare your reasoning

Flexible fits because the strip bends easily. The note gives neither a price nor information about bumps.

Try a different case

A second strip has an even surface with no bumps. Which word does that clue support?

Check the new clue

Liso fits the surface clue. You would still need to try bending the strip to judge flexibility.

2. Trace the adjective back to its noun

Spanish nouns have grammatical gender. Here, masculine and feminine are categories of words, even for objects. Number means singular (one) or plural (more than one) in these examples. Agreement means matching those features. Learn a noun with its article, the word for “the” in these examples.

With many adjectives like grueso, the endings are -o, -a, -os, -as. Other adjectives share a form across genders. One cable and one rope can both be flexible. More than one of either takes flexibles.

Switch the exhibit label

All of these imaginary ropes and cables are thick and flexible. Predict what changes if you switch from one rope to several ropes. Now predict what changes if you switch from one rope to one cable. Say your prediction before comparing each label.

Pick the subject of the label
One cable · compare the sentence

El cable del puente es grueso y flexible.

One cable is masculine singular. Use es. The first adjective takes -o. The second keeps its singular form.

One rope · compare the sentence

La cuerda del puente es gruesa y flexible.

One rope is feminine singular. The verb and the second adjective stay the same as for one cable. The first adjective takes -a.

Several cables · compare the sentence

Los cables del puente son gruesos y flexibles.

Several cables are masculine plural. Use son. Both adjectives take a plural form.

Several ropes · compare the sentence

Las cuerdas del puente son gruesas y flexibles.

Several ropes are feminine plural. The first adjective shows feminine and plural. The second shows plural with -s.

After both switches, explain why changing the number affected both adjectives, while changing the gender affected only one.

Keep track of the thing you describe

Las cajas del taller son … means “The workshop’s boxes are …” You want to say they are square (cuadrado). Which form fits?

Choose the form of the adjective.

Compare your reasoning

Las cajas points to feminine plural, so use cuadradas. Del taller tells you which boxes.

Try a different case

In Los marcos de la ventana son … (The window frames are …), what form of cuadrado fits?

Check the frames

Cuadrados matches los marcos, the masculine plural noun being described.

More endings you might meet
  • Many adjectives ending in -e or -ista share a gender form and add -s in the plural, as in amable → amables (kind) and ecologista → ecologistas (environmentalist).
  • Many adjectives that end in a consonant add -es in the plural. Some share a gender form, such as especial → especiales (special). Others have a feminine form ending in -a, such as encantador, encantadora, encantadores, encantadoras (charming). Some words for nationality also change for gender, such as alemán, alemana, alemanes, alemanas (German).

If a word is new, check its forms in a dictionary. These are common patterns with exceptions.

3. Choose the form of ser

Ser means “to be.” Use it in these examples to describe a characteristic. The subject is who or what the sentence is about. It determines the verb form. Check the adjective separately, even when it comes after the verb.

soy · I am
A fictional probe introduces itself. Soy sumergible y hermética. “I’m submersible and watertight.” You can leave out yo because soy already tells you who is speaking.
es · is
A guide introduces one probe. La sonda es sumergible y hermética. The subject is one probe. Both adjectives describe la sonda. Only hermética shows feminine gender with its ending.
son · are
A guide introduces several probes. Las sondas son sumergibles y herméticas. The verb and both adjectives now match a plural subject.

Y means “and.” When it joins two adjectives describing the same thing, check each one against that thing. Muy means “very” and keeps the same form before the adjective.

Change the speaker

A guide says El robot es diminuto. “The robot is tiny.” In the exhibit, that same robot gets its own microphone. Which line should it say?

Choose the line from the robot’s point of view.

Compare your reasoning

Soy diminuto. The subject is now “I,” the robot. The adjective still describes the same robot.

Try a different case

A guide points to two tiny robots. What would the guide say?

Check the guide’s line

Los robots son diminutos. Talking about more than one robot changes the verb and the adjective.

Put the checks together

What needs fixing?

A product test shows that the fabric of two sleeves keeps water from passing through. Nobody weighed the sleeves. The draft reads Las fundas del equipo son ligeras. “The equipment sleeves are lightweight.” Choose the edit the test supports.

Choose the line that fits the evidence and the subject.

Compare your reasoning

Impermeable means waterproof. Las fundas means “the sleeves.” Keep son and add -s to the adjective because the sleeves are plural.

Try a different case

Now the label is for just one waterproof sleeve. Change the words that need a singular form.

Compare the edit

La funda del equipo es impermeable. The article, noun, verb, and adjective now fit one sleeve.

Draft two lines for the exhibit

A fictional glove introduces itself. Use el guante (the glove), térmico (thermal, designed to keep heat in), and flexible (flexible). Say or write its line privately. Then rewrite the line for a guide describing two of those gloves. Explain which changes came from changing the speaker and which came from changing how many gloves there were.

Compare your two lines

Soy térmico y flexible. “I’m thermal and flexible.” Los guantes son térmicos y flexibles. “The gloves are thermal and flexible.” The subject changes from “I” to “the gloves,” so soy becomes son. Both adjectives change to plural. Only térmico has a different masculine and feminine form.

Check your own descriptions

  1. For each adjective, point to a clue or a fact that supports its meaning.
  2. Underline who or what the sentence is about. Check the form of ser against that subject.
  3. Trace each adjective back to what it describes. Check both gender and number and read the whole line once.

Before opening this lesson next time, recall what controls the verb and what controls the adjective. Try changing a description of one bicycle to a description of two, then explain each change aloud.

If you ask for help in private chat, say which check is giving you trouble.

Glossary

Noun
A word that names a person, place, thing, or idea.
Adjective
A word that describes something, such as its shape or a characteristic.
Grammatical gender
A category of a Spanish noun, such as masculine or feminine. It affects the words that match the noun.
Number
In these examples, whether the word refers to one thing (singular) or more than one (plural).
Agreement
Matching features between related words. A Spanish adjective matches the gender and number of what it describes, even if its spelling happens to be shared across genders.
Article
A word used with a noun. In these examples el, la, los, las all mean “the.”
Subject
Who or what the sentence is about here. It tells you which form of the verb to use.
