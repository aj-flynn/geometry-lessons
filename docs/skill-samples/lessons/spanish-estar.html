Feelings and places with estar

Tell a friend how you feel. Help a partner find things in a picture. Use estar (“to be”) for both.

Before you start

One friend asks ¿Cómo estás? (“How are you?”). Another asks ¿Dónde están las llaves? (“Where are the keys?”). Which friend needs a feeling word? Which question asks about you?

Check the two requests

The first friend is asking about you. Cómo means “how,” and estás means “you are” when speaking informally to one person. The second friend needs a place. Dónde means “where,” and están goes with the keys.

Comparisons start after the lesson. Try each task before turning to them.

Say how you feel

Imagine Ana is about to get on a Ferris wheel. Her friend asks ¿Cómo estás? (“How are you?”). Ana feels excited, so she answers Estoy emocionada. (“I’m excited.”)

  1. Estás points to “you.” Ana is answering about herself, so she switches to estoy (“I am”). In Spanish, that verb can stand without yo (“I”).
  2. Ana picks the feeling. Emocionado and emocionada both mean “excited.” Ana uses the feminine form, emocionada, for herself. This match is called agreement.

A different rider might say Estoy tranquilo or Estoy tranquila (“I’m calm”). Both use estoy. A situation tells you when the question applies, and you choose what feeling to express.

  • tranquilo / tranquila · calm
  • sorprendido / sorprendida · surprised
  • preocupado / preocupada · worried
  • emocionado / emocionada · excited
  • feliz · happy, same form for any one person
  • bien / mal · well / poorly, words that keep their form

Try a quick reply

Your train is late. A friend asks ¿Cómo estás? Answer aloud in Spanish with the feeling you would have in that moment.

Compare a possible reply

Estoy bien. This speaker feels fine. A worried speaker could say Estoy preocupado or Estoy preocupada.

If you answered Estás bien, you told your friend how they are doing. Change estás to estoy to report how you feel. Revise your line aloud before trying Luis’s reply.

Answer as Luis

The next day the train arrives early. Luis is surprised. Say his answer to ¿Cómo estás? as if you were Luis.

Compare Luis’s reply

Estoy sorprendido. Luis says “I am” and uses the masculine form of “surprised.” Yo estoy sorprendido also works.

Give a location someone can use

At the bike rack

El casco ____ ____ de la mochila.

  1. Find what you’re locating. The subject is the thing or person the verb describes. Here it’s one helmet, so use está. Two things together use están.
  2. Find the reference point. A landmark is the thing you look from. Here it’s the backpack. Starting at the backpack, look left to find the helmet. Use the place phrase a la izquierda de (“to the left of”). It tells where to look from the backpack.
  3. Say the whole sentence. El casco está a la izquierda de la mochila. Only a la izquierda goes in the second blank because de la mochila is already there.

Está and están both have an accent on the á. When de comes before el, the two usually join into del, as in del casco (“of the helmet”).

Change just one clue

Imagine you set a bike pump (la bomba) next to the helmet on the same side of the backpack. Tell your friend where the helmet and the pump are together. Say the whole sentence in Spanish. Which part changes?

Compare after you say it

El casco y la bomba están a la izquierda de la mochila.

The helmet and pump make two things to find, even though there’s one backpack. The side stays the same. If you used está, point to the helmet and the pump, count them together, and say the line with están.

Switch what you’re locating

Use the drawing. Say where the backpack is relative to the helmet.

Compare the reversed location

La mochila está a la derecha del casco.

One backpack takes está. Starting at the helmet instead gives the opposite direction. De joins with el in del casco.

Words for finding things

A place phrase tells where one thing is in relation to another. Read these once, then try to name the position yourself before looking them up.

  • a la izquierda de · to the left of
  • a la derecha de · to the right of
  • encima de · above or on top of
  • debajo de · below or under
  • delante de · in front of
  • detrás de · behind
  • dentro de · inside
  • al lado de · next to

Your turn · Check in with a friend

You can pause here. When you come back, try these questions without looking back at the examples. Imagine each situation and give a full Spanish reply aloud or on paper. Use a feeling that fits you or the character you’re playing.

  1. Eres Leo. Tu robot se cae de la mesa. ¿Cómo estás? eres · you are. se cae de la mesa · falls off the table.
  2. ¿Cómo estás cuando por fin encuentras tus llaves? por fin encuentras · you finally find. llaves · keys.
  3. ¿Cómo estás cuando se apagan las luces y empieza la música? se apagan · go out. empieza · starts.
  4. ¿Cómo estás cuando estás en un ascensor lleno? ascensor · elevator. lleno · full.
  5. ¿Cómo estás al final del ensayo? ensayo · rehearsal
  6. ¿Cómo estás antes de probar un plato nuevo? antes de probar · before trying. un plato · a dish.
Compare your replies

Answer as the speaker in each situation, including when you play Leo. You’re saying how you feel in that role, so look for estoy even if you left out yo. Compare any changing adjective with the person it describes. The feeling can differ from one speaker to another.

For example, one speaker might say Estoy sorprendida when the lights go out and the music starts, Estoy preocupada in the crowded elevator, and Estoy feliz after rehearsal. As Leo, you might say Estoy sorprendido when your robot falls. A speaker who finds their keys or tries a new dish might feel calm or excited and could use Estoy tranquilo or Estoy emocionado, for example.

Read the picture in words

A hangs high in the center. B sits on shelf F at mid-height. C and D hang at mid-height near the right edge. At floor level near the left edge, E is visible through the clear front panel of G, between its sides. In the center, the feet of shelf F meet the farther floor line. H stands on the closer floor line and hides the bottoms of both shelf legs. A coil of hose sits at the right edge of the floor.

A new greenhouse volunteer has the same picture. Work with a partner or play both parts. Take turns saying a complete line in Spanish. Fill both gaps from the picture. Your partner starts at the landmark and follows the direction you give. Explain which clue supports your choice, or give a more precise location if your partner hesitates.

  1. El guante y las tijeras ____ ____ de las macetas.
  2. La lámpara ____ ____ de las macetas.
  3. La regadera ____ ____ del cajón.
  4. Las botellas ____ ____ del estante.
Compare the greenhouse lines
  1. El guante y las tijeras están a la derecha de las macetas. Both tools hang on the right.
  2. La lámpara está encima de las macetas. One lamp hangs above the pots, so the verb is singular even though there are two pots.
  3. La regadera está dentro del cajón. The clear bin shows the can inside. The supplied del already contains de.
  4. Las botellas están delante del estante. The two bottles stand on the closer floor line and hide the feet of the shelf. That shows they are in front of it.

If you said están for the lamp, mark what you were asked to locate. One lamp takes está. If your partner found the right area but hesitated, make the clue more precise. For instance, al lado de las macetas gives a nearby position, and a la derecha de las macetas tells which side. Say any revised line again.

Take it back to your work

  1. For a feeling, read the situation and answer as the speaker. Reread the verb and the feeling word together.
  2. For a location, point first to what you are locating. Count those things, then find the named landmark.
  3. Read the completed sentence aloud. Check the direction, the written accent and whether de is already part of the line.

Glossary

estar
Spanish for “to be” in uses that include reporting a feeling or locating an object. The forms in this lesson are estoy (I am), estás (you are, speaking to one person informally), está (he, she, or it is) and están (they are).
Agreement
A match in form. The verb matches who or what it is about. A feeling word such as tranquilo changes form to match the person it describes. In these replies, feliz has the same form for any one speaker.
Subject
The person or thing the verb is about. Two things joined with y (“and”) usually make a plural subject.
Landmark
The reference object. In “the boots are under the chair,” the chair is the landmark.
del
The short form of de + el. Keep de la, de los and de las separate.
Place phrase
Words that describe location. In “under the chair,” the place phrase says where to look.
