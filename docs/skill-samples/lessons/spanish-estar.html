Estar for feelings and places

Imagine you’re helping at a planetarium. Tell a friend how you feel, then help a visitor find things in a display. Both messages use estar, a verb that can mean “to be.”

Which question asks for a feeling? ¿Cómo? means “How?” and ¿Dónde? means “Where?”

Glossary and word bank

Say how you feel

The subject is who or what the sentence is about. Listen for the subject in the question, then find the subject of your reply. The verb changes when the speaker changes.

A friend asks Mara how she feels when she sees Saturn.

Friend ¿Cómo estás cuando ves Saturno?
How do you feel when you see Saturn?

Mara Estoy asombrada.
I’m amazed.

  1. Spot cómo. The friend wants a feeling. The words after cuando (“when”) tell Mara which situation to imagine.
  2. The friend uses estás for tú (“you”). Mara uses estoy for yo (“I”). Both subjects are understood, so the speakers can leave the pronouns out.
  3. Asombrada is an adjective, a word that describes someone or something. Mara uses the -a form for herself. If Nico gives the same reply about himself, he says Estoy asombrado. Both forms mean “amazed.”

Matching an adjective to the person it describes is called agreement. Many feeling words use -o or -a for one person. A word like feliz (“happy”) has the same singular form for Mara and Nico. People can feel differently in the same situation.

Catch the change of speaker

A friend asks ¿Cómo estás cuando se apaga la luz? (“How do you feel when the light goes out?”) In this scene, Nico feels uneasy. Say his full reply before checking. Inquieto and inquieta both mean “uneasy.”

Compare your reply

Estoy inquieto. Nico speaks about himself, so he switches to estoy. He uses inquieto for himself.

If Mara gives the same reply, predict the one change before opening the retry.

Compare the retry

Estoy inquieta. Mara is still saying “I,” so only the adjective changes.

Say where something is

Find two things before speaking. The target is what you want the listener to find. The landmark is what you use to explain where the target is. Read the display as you face it.

Use está for one target and están for two or more. Keep the accent. Count only the targets, whether you name one landmark or several.

Build the sentence from the picture

Give a side. Start at the Sun and look toward the Moon. The Moon is one target, on the viewer’s left of the Sun. La Luna está a la izquierda del Sol.

Give a distance. Start at the Moon and look toward the comet. There’s a long gap. El cometa está lejos de la Luna.

Give a height. The stars hang lower than the Sun. The plural target is the stars. Las estrellas están debajo del Sol.

Count two names joined by y (“and”). From the Moon, both targets are on the right. El Sol y el cometa están a la derecha de la Luna.

For a short gap use cerca de (“near”). For something right beside a landmark use al lado de (“next to”). Both can fit the same scene. For the planet right beside the Moon, you could say El planeta está al lado de la Luna. When you name the landmark, join de + el as del. Keep de la, de los and de las separate.

Swap what you’re looking for

A visitor wants to find the comet using the planet as the landmark. Look again at the same wall. Give a sentence with a side. Then predict what happens to the side and verb if the planet becomes the target and the comet becomes the landmark.

Compare both views

El cometa está a la derecha del planeta. Reverse the trip and you look left instead. El planeta está a la izquierda del cometa. Each sentence has one target, so both use está.

Retry with a distance. The Moon is now the target and the stars are the landmark. Choose cerca de or lejos de by comparing the gap to the long gap across the display.

Compare the distance

La Luna está cerca de las estrellas. Their gap is small within the display. Use está for the one Moon, even with a plural landmark.

Make two new messages

Say or write each reply. Decide what the listener needs before choosing any Spanish words.

  1. A friend asks ¿Cómo estás cuando aparece el cometa? (“How do you feel when the comet appears?”) Give a full reply about yourself. You choose the feeling.
  2. For a guest by the wall, describe what side the planet and the Moon are on compared with the Sun. Make two choices, the form of estar and a phrase that gives the side. Put them together in one sentence.
Compare after trying both

For the friend, Estoy feliz is one possible reply. It has a feeling that fits for some people, estoy for the speaker and feliz, which works for any one speaker. A different feeling can fit your experience.

For the guest, El planeta y la Luna están a la izquierda del Sol. Start at the Sun and look left to see both. Use están because there are two targets. Cerca del Sol tells a distance and leaves the guest without the side they asked for.

Retry by locating the planet and Moon relative to the comet. Give the distance this time and explain how you choose the verb.

Compare the retry

El planeta y la Luna están lejos del cometa. Count two targets to choose the verb. Use the long gap to the comet to choose the distance phrase.

Explain it. In your first reply, who is the subject? In your second, which objects did you count to choose the verb?

Recall it later. Before you reopen the lesson, imagine a bus stop and two bicycles. Say where the bicycles are. Then answer a friend who asks how you feel when you see a rainbow. Which verb form fits each subject?

Glossary and word bank

Estar
“To be.” Use it here for feelings and for the location of people or things.
Subject
The person, people, thing or things the sentence is about.
Adjective and agreement
An adjective describes. Agreement means matching its form to the person or thing it describes.
Target and landmark
The target is what you locate. The landmark is what you describe its position from.
Asombrado / asombrada, inquieto / inquieta, feliz
Amazed, uneasy, happy.
A la izquierda de, a la derecha de
To the left of, to the right of.
Cerca de, lejos de, al lado de, debajo de
Near, far from, next to, underneath.
El planeta, la Luna, el Sol, el cometa, las estrellas
The planet, the Moon, the Sun, the comet, the stars.
