Follow the electrons

Check a Lewis structure by tracking every valence electron once.

Use these original models to learn a method, then try your own work independently. Open the glossary or use the quick reference.

An electron budget

Electrons carry negative charge. The outer electrons used in bonding are valence electrons. A Lewis drawing is an electron-accounting model: dots track electrons and lines can stand for pairs. Two dots are one pair. A pair used between atoms is counted once in the whole drawing’s budget.

Build a budget before drawing: add the valence electrons supplied by all atoms, add electrons for negative charge, and subtract electrons for positive charge. Record how many electrons have been placed and how many remain. A line costs two electrons in this accounting, but the names for different bond patterns are a separate vocabulary step for you to make using your course reference.

A lone pair is a pair that is not shared between atoms. Many simple main-group atom models use an eight-electron outer-shell goal (an octet); a hydrogen atom uses two. These are limited guidelines, not universal rules. When checking a local shell, a shared pair contributes around both atoms; when checking the overall budget, count it only once.

A flat Lewis drawing records electron accounting and connections. It does not by itself establish the actual shape in space.

Predict → place electrons → audit

Imagine an abstract budget of eighteen electrons. No atoms or connections are specified: this is a practice ledger, not a proposed molecule. Predict the remainder after two pairs have been allocated.

Reveal the model stages after predicting
  1. Start with 18 electrons. Zero have been placed.
  2. Allocate one pair: 2 placed and 16 left.
  3. Allocate a second pair: 4 placed and 14 left. The budget is still 18.

Make a prediction, then reveal the stages or use Next step.

To continue a real Lewis drawing, you would still need the identities of the atoms, connections, shell goals, and charge. A correct subtraction alone cannot validate a structure.

Two different bonding models

Compare two pictures before attaching a scientific label. In a shared-electron picture, negative electrons are attracted to more than one positive nucleus. In a charged-particle picture, particles already have opposite net charges and attract one another. Forming charged particles and attracting charged particles are different steps.

Imagine one initially neutral token particle losing a negative electron and a second initially neutral particle gaining it. The first becomes more positive; the second becomes more negative. The total charge of the two-particle system stays unchanged. A real crystal involves many neighbors; a two-particle cartoon leaves much out.

Keep two questions separate: how many pairs are represented between two atoms, and how evenly those electrons are shared. Changing the pair count does not by itself tell you how unevenly the electrons are distributed. Use these pictures to make your own connection to the course’s bonding terms.

Your turn: audit before drawing

A different fictional model has a total budget of 14 valence electrons. Its ledger allocates three pairs. How many electrons remain to place as lone pairs? This is an accounting exercise, not a proposed molecular structure.

Hint 1: Notice

Count the pairs allocated in the ledger.

Hint 2: Recall

Each allocated pair uses two electrons.

Hint 3: Choose a strategy

Subtract allocated electrons from the total budget. Check that used + remaining = total.

An initially neutral model particle gains two electrons without changing its nucleus. Which change follows?

Exit check: explain a new case

A fictional Lewis drawing has a 12-electron budget but displays two bond lines and five lone pairs. Describe how to audit it and what kind of correction is needed. Do not infer a real molecule or shape.

Check your method after making an attempt

Count each line and each lone pair as two electrons, once in the total. The drawing uses more than its budget; remove or rearrange electrons only after checking the intended connections and shell goals. A tidy drawing is not proof of a valid budget.

If this is difficult, return to the model, make one change, and describe what stays the same. Then try the exit check again without looking.

Glossary: tools for thinking

Electron / valence
An electron carries negative charge. Valence electrons are outer electrons used in bonding.
Budget / pair
A budget is the total available. A pair contains two items; count two electrons for each represented pair.
Nucleus / net charge
The nucleus is the positive central region of an atom. Net charge is the balance after combining positive and negative contributions.
Lone pair / octet
A lone pair is a pair not shared between atoms. An octet is an eight-electron outer-shell goal in some simple models.
Ledger / model
A ledger tracks what has been allocated and what remains. A model represents selected features and leaves others out.
