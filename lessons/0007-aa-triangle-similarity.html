Geometry · AA similarity

Two angles unlock a length

Prove the shape match. Pair the corners. Then scale the sides.

Your goal: justify triangle similarity and use its corner (vertex) order to find a missing length. Take the short route through the examples; open the hints when you need them.

Quick recall. A triangle has angles of 48° and 72°. What is its third angle? If a length grows from 4 cm to 10 cm, what multiplier takes the first length to the second?

Check the starting tools

Triangle angles add to 180°, so the third is 180° − 48° − 72° = 60°. The multiplier is target ÷ original: 10 cm ÷ 4 cm = 2.5. Use the angle sum to find missing angles; use multiplication, rather than adding a fixed amount, to scale lengths.

1. Find two angle matches

Similar triangles have the same shape: one can fit the other after turning, flipping, sliding, and uniformly resizing it. AA similarity is a shortcut: two pairs of congruent angles (equal angle measures) prove two triangles similar.

Why two? The third angles are forced to match because each triangle’s angles total 180°. To see why the sides scale too, resize one triangle until the side between the matched angles fits the other. Those two angles and the included side fix the triangle’s shape. Illustrative Mathematics states the AA criterion.

Small worked example. The given segment DE is parallel to BC: their lines never meet (the arrow marks). D lies on AB and E lies on AC.

  1. ∠DAE ≅ ∠BACThese are the same angle at A: their rays overlap.
  2. ∠ADE ≅ ∠ABCThese angles occupy the same relative position where AB crosses the parallel lines DE and BC.
  3. △ADE ∼ △ABC by AA.The symbol ∼ means “is similar to.” We have two angle pairs with reasons, so no side calculation is needed to establish similarity.
Need another parallel-line angle rule?

When a line crosses two parallel lines, alternate interior angles also match. These angles lie inside the parallels and on opposite sides of the crossing line. Use the placement of the angles to choose this rule or the corresponding-angle rule.

What if only one pair matches?

One equal angle leaves the other two free to change. For example, triangles with angles 40°, 60°, 80° and 40°, 50°, 90° share one measure but are not similar. A sketch that looks like a resized copy is not evidence. Use stated measures, marks, or a theorem.

Check the angle evidence

Triangle KLM has ∠K = 31° and ∠L = 84°. Triangle RST has ∠R = 65° and ∠S = 31°. Decide whether they are similar. Give the two matching angle pairs before checking.

Use the angle sum in each triangle. The given angles need not be the matching pair.

Compare your reasoning

∠M = 65° and ∠T = 84°. Match K ↔ S, L ↔ T, M ↔ R. Thus △KLM ∼ △STR by AA. Retry: change ∠R to 64°, keeping ∠S = 31°. Are the triangles still similar?

No. The second triangle now has measures 64°, 31°, 85°. Only the 31° angles match; its angle set differs from 31°, 84°, 65°.

2. Let the order choose the sides

A correspondence pairs matching corners. In △ADE ∼ △ABC, the positions say A ↔ A, D ↔ B, E ↔ C. Match both endpoints of a side, including the closing pair:

AD ↔ AB, DE ↔ BC, EA ↔ CA.

New worked example. Two detached triangular panels have the marked equal angle pairs. Point H divides the straight side LM. All side expressions (formulas for lengths) below are in centimeters; t is a number to find. Goal: find the full length VW.

The arcs give L ↔ U and M ↔ V, so the third corners pair N ↔ W. Thus △LMN ∼ △UVW by AA. Match LM ↔ UV and MN ↔ VW. The side LM is the whole side: LM = LH + HM = 3 cm + 5 cm = 8 cm.

Corresponding lengths have one scale factor (common multiplier). Keep large/small in both ratios and keep each expression in parentheses:

VW/MN = UV/LM(2t + 10)/(3t + 1) = 16/(3 + 5) = 2

Finish the algebra and check the actual length

The numerical equation uses centimeter values on both sides; the units cancel in each ratio.

2t + 10 = 2(3t + 1)2t + 10 = 6t + 28 = 4t, so t = 2VW = (2 × 2 + 10) cm = 14 cm

Check every expression: MN = (3 × 2 + 1) cm = 7 cm, so VW/MN = 14/7 = 2, matching UV/LM = 16/8. Both lengths are positive. The variable value t = 2 is not the requested side length. Using only HM = 5 cm would compare a piece with a whole side and give the wrong ratio.

The shared angle at A and the corresponding angles at D and B still match, so △ADE ∼ △ABC at both positions. Resizing changes lengths, while the angles stay fixed. 3. Use similarity to measure indirectly

An ideal pinhole camera uses a tiny opening to project an inverted image. A flat panel and the image screen are parallel. The ray from the panel’s top passes through the hole to the image’s bottom.

∠PBO and ∠ISO are right angles. ∠POB ≅ ∠IOS because they are vertical angles, the opposite angles where two straight lines cross. So △PBO ∼ △ISO by AA. PB ↔ IS and BO ↔ SO.

Work the units and proportion one step at a time

Use millimeters (mm) throughout the calculation. Convert each part before adding: 1 m = 1000 mm and 1 cm = 10 mm.

3 m × 1000 mm/m = 3000 mm60 cm × 10 mm/cm = 600 mmOB = 3000 mm + 600 mm = 3600 mmPB/IS = BO/SOPB / 18 mm = 3600 mm / 45 mm = 80PB = 80 × 18 mm = 1440 mmPB = 1440 mm × 1 m / 1000 mm = 1.44 mPB ≈ 1.4 m, to the nearest tenth.

The panel is 80 times farther from O, so its height is 80 times the image height. Keep extra digits until the final rounding. This ideal model assumes straight rays, a flat screen parallel to the panel, and negligible hole size. A real camera with a lens needs a different optical model.

Try a fresh camera setup

The same ideal geometry has OB = 2 m 70 cm, OS = 36 mm, and image height IS = 15 mm. Find panel height PB in meters, to the nearest hundredth. Write an ordered similarity statement and your unit conversion before checking.

Hint 1 · Pair the roles

Hint 2 · Choose consistent units

Compare your calculation

△PBO ∼ △ISO. The compound distance is 2000 mm + 700 mm = 2700 mm. In millimeters, h/15 = 2700/36 = 75, so h = 1125 mm = 1.125 m ≈ 1.13 m. Multiplying meters by the millimeter value without converting first loses a factor of 1000. Retry: if only the panel distance doubles, what happens to its calculated height for the same image height?

Compare the retry

The distance ratio doubles, so the calculated panel height doubles. The corresponding angles remain equal under the stated model.

Glossary

Similar · ∼
Figures that fit after rigid moves (turns, flips, slides) and a uniform resize. Corresponding angles match and corresponding lengths have one common ratio. △ABC ∼ △DEF pairs A ↔ D, B ↔ E, C ↔ F.
AA similarity
For two triangles, two pairs of corresponding congruent angles are enough to establish similarity. AA means Angle–Angle.
Congruent angles · ≅
Angles with the same measure. ∠ABC names the angle with vertex B; ° means degrees, the unit of angle measure.
Correspondence · ↔
A pairing of matching vertices and their sides. Match both endpoints to identify a side pair.
Parallel · ∥
Lines in the same plane that never meet. A crossing line produces equal corresponding angles (same relative position), or equal alternate interior angles (inside the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the crossing line).
Vertical angles
Opposite angles formed by two intersecting straight lines. They have equal measures; “vertical” here does not mean upright.
Side expression
A formula for a side’s length, such as (3t + 1) cm. The variable stands for a number. Solve for that number, substitute it into the formula, and check that each resulting length is positive.
Scale factor
Target length divided by its matching original length, in consistent units. Multiply every original side length by that same factor.
Pinhole camera
An ideal model where rays pass through one tiny opening onto a screen. The model here assumes a screen parallel to the object.
Units and symbols
m = meters, cm = centimeters, mm = millimeters. 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm. = means exactly equal; ≈ means approximately equal, such as after rounding.
