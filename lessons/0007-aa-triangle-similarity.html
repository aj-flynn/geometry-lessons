Geometry · Triangle similarity

Two angles, one shape

Use two angle matches to line up triangles. Then use that order to find lengths you can’t measure directly.

Opening checks

Check the two prerequisites

The angle measures in a triangle add to 180°, so 48° + 77° + a = 180° and a = 55°. The middle letter X names the vertex, the corner where the two rays meet. Say ∠ZXY and point to X before tracing the rays XZ and XY.

Read the arc marks

∠K and ∠R have the same measure because they have matching single arcs. ∠M and ∠N have matching double arcs. You can pair angles by the arc counts without knowing their measures in degrees.

1. Match the angles

One corner divided in two

Similar triangles have equal matching angles and one common multiplier between matching side lengths. Corresponding means matching in that pairing.

Goal Decide if the pieces △PHQ and △QHR are similar. Identify which edge of the right piece matches the cut QH of the left piece.

  1. The perpendicular cut makes both angles at H right angles, so ∠PHQ ≅ ∠QHR. The symbol ≅ means congruent, or equal in measure.
  2. In the left piece, m∠PQH = 180° − 90° − 33° = 57°. The cut splits the 90° corner at Q, so m∠HQR = 90° − 57° = 33°. This matches ∠QPH on the left.
  3. Two distinct corresponding angle pairs prove similarity by Angle–Angle (AA). Once two angles match, subtracting them from 180° gives the same third angle in both triangles. The triangle’s shape is fixed even though its size can vary.
  4. Pair P ↔ Q, H ↔ H and Q ↔ R. Write △PHQ ∼ △QHR. The side pairs are PH ↔ QH, HQ ↔ HR and QP ↔ RQ. The cut HQ of the left piece matches HR on the right. A side can belong to both triangles and still have a different corresponding partner.

Trace the rays when triangles overlap

Two outlines from A

Goal Decide if △AEI and △ABT are similar. Picture yourself tracing A → E → I → A, then A → B → T → A.

At A, both angles use the same two rays. So ∠EAI ≅ ∠TAB. This is a shared angle.

The 108° labels give the second match, ∠AEI ≅ ∠ATB. Pair A with A and E with T, leaving I with B. By AA, △AEI ∼ △ATB.

Writing △AEI ∼ △ABT would pair E with B just because both are on the upper ray. Check the 108° labels. They pair E with T, on the other ray. So even AE and AT are corresponding sides across the rays.

Find the angle evidence at a crossing

Two arms on one upright

Goal Decide whether the right-angle boxes and crossed braces prove △PKS is similar to the triangle across S.

∠PSK ≅ ∠DSE. Each uses the opposite ray of the other at the crossing. Such a pair is called vertical angles.

The right angles on the upright are outside the triangles. They establish that both arms are perpendicular to the same line, so PK ∥ ED. The symbol ∥ means the lines are parallel. One box by itself would give no such guarantee.

Trace P–S–D across those parallel arms. ∠KPS and ∠EDS lie between the arms and on opposite sides of the brace. They are alternate interior angles and are congruent because the arms are parallel. Arrow marks on the arms would also establish parallelism directly.

Now P ↔ D and S ↔ S, leaving K ↔ E. Therefore △PKS ∼ △DES by AA.

2. Use whole corresponding sides

A split edge on a frame

Goal Find x, the length FD in centimeters.

The two angle pairs give △XBU ∼ △FDZ by AA. XB ↔ FD and BU ↔ DZ. W marks a point along XB, so the full corresponding side is XB.

  1. XB = 9 cm + 19 cm = 28 cm.
  2. x28 = 1221 Match frame FDZ over frame XBU, using lengths in centimeters.
  3. x = 28 × 1221 Multiply both sides by 28.
  4. x = 16, so FD = 16 cm. Both corresponding side lengths shrink by the scale factor 12/21.

Keep the order when both sides have x

Two similar pieces

△CGJ ∼ △LQS

Goal Find x. The statement pairs GJ with QS and CG with LQ. GJ includes both pieces of the split edge. Write a proportion, an equation that sets two ratios equal.

GJ = GM + MJ = (2x − 3) + 5 = 2x + 2.

  1. 2x + 2x + 6 = 2416 = 32 Use △CGJ lengths over △LQS lengths in both ratios.
  2. 2(2x + 2) = 3(x + 6). Clear the denominators. Since QS is a positive length, x + 6 > 0.
  3. 4x + 4 = 3x + 18. Distribute.
  4. x = 14. Subtract 3x and 4 from both sides.

Check the lengths. GM = 25 and MJ = 5, so GJ = 30. QS = 20. The lengths are positive and 30/20 = 24/16.

Use one unit before measuring indirectly

A brace design

Two straight braces meet their vertical walls at the same angle. Each wall meets its level overhead beam at a right angle.

Goal Find the long brace’s reach along its beam to the nearest centimeter.

The right angles pair T ↔ L. The brace-to-wall angles pair B ↔ G. By AA, △HTB ∼ △JLG. So the beam reaches correspond, and the wall reaches correspond.

  1. 1 m 5 cm = 100 cm + 5 cm = 105 cm. Convert before comparing.
  2. x70 = 190105 Compare long to short in both ratios. All lengths are in centimeters.
  3. x = 70 × 190105 = 3803 Multiply both sides by 70.
  4. The reach is about 127 cm. Round the result, keeping the exact ratio until this step.

The model assumes straight braces, vertical walls, level beams, and the same brace-to-wall angle.

D · Whole sides of a frame

∠GHW and ∠PHR share the same rays. ∠HGW ≅ ∠HPR because the arrows mark GW ∥ PR and the angles are in matching positions along HP. So △HGW ∼ △HPR by AA.

Glossary

Vertex
A corner of a figure. In ∠PQR the vertex is Q, the middle letter.
Similar triangles (∼)
Triangles with matching angle measures and a common scale factor between corresponding lengths.
Corresponding
In the same position in the pairing. In △UVW ∼ △GHJ, U pairs with G and W pairs with J, so WU corresponds to JG.
Congruent angles (≅)
Angles with the same measure.
Angle–Angle similarity (AA)
Two triangles are similar if two distinct angles of one triangle are congruent to two distinct corresponding angles of the other.
Shared angle
One angle used by both triangles. Its vertex and both rays agree.
Vertical angles
Opposite angles formed when two straight lines cross. The two angles in a vertical pair are congruent.
Alternate interior angles
Angles inside two lines and on opposite sides of a line crossing them. They are congruent when the two lines are parallel.
Scale factor
The multiplier that takes a corresponding length in one figure to the length in the other. It depends on the direction of comparison.
Proportion
An equation showing that two ratios are equal.
