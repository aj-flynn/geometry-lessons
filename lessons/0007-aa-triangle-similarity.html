Before you start

A triangle has angles of 48° and 77°. Write an equation for the third angle. In ∠ZXY, which letter names the corner?

Check the two prerequisites

The angle measures in a triangle add to 180°, so 48° + 77° + a = 180° and a = 55°. The middle letter X names the vertex, the corner where the two rays meet. Say ∠ZXY and point to X before tracing the rays XZ and XY.

Which angles do the arc marks pair? What do the marks tell you about their measures?

Read the arc marks

∠K and ∠R have the same measure because they have matching single arcs. ∠M and ∠N have matching double arcs. You can pair angles by the arc counts without knowing their measures in degrees.

How the Try-it tasks work

Each problem type ends with a task. Try one task at a time on paper. Use the facts and marks on its diagram, then open the comparison to check your reasoning. Stop after any task. For a short first session, try A and B.

When you compare, find the first step where your reasoning differs. Repair that line on paper, then attempt the retry.

Words to know

Reading a similarity statement

Similar triangles have equal matching angles and one common multiplier between matching side lengths. Corresponding means matching in that pairing. The statement’s letter order is the pairing, not where the triangles sit on the page.

Vertex
A corner of a figure. In ∠PQR the vertex is Q, the middle letter.In the diagram: U, V and W are the vertices of the small triangle; the leader points to U.
Corresponding vertices
Letters in the same position of the statement. Their angles are congruent.In the diagram: the red curves join U ↔ G, V ↔ H, W ↔ J, and the arc counts match.
Corresponding sides
Sides whose endpoints are corresponding vertices. Map each endpoint on its own.In the diagram: W ↔ J and U ↔ G, so WU corresponds to JG (blue).
Similar (∼)
Same shape, possibly a different size.In the diagram: the ∼ in △UVW ∼ △GHJ, read “is similar to”.
Scale factor
The multiplier from one figure’s length to its partner. It depends on the direction of comparison.In the diagram: JG ÷ WU goes from UVW to GHJ.

Angle–Angle similarity (AA) Two triangles are similar if two distinct angles of one triangle are congruent to two distinct corresponding angles of the other.

Why are two angle pairs enough?

Once two angles match, subtracting them from 180° gives the same third angle in both triangles. The triangle’s shape is fixed even though its size can vary.

Words to know

Where angle matches come from

AA needs two angle pairs, and each pair needs a reason. The symbol ≅ means congruent, or equal in measure. Besides measures written on the figure, these are the reasons this lesson uses.

Congruent angles (≅)
Angles with the same measure. Matching arc counts show it.In the diagram: the two red single arcs, and the two yellow double arcs.
Vertical angles
Opposite angles formed when two straight lines cross. Each uses the opposite rays of the other. They are always congruent.In the diagram: ∠PSK and ∠DSE at S, red.
Alternate interior angles
Inside two lines and on opposite sides of a line crossing them. Congruent when the two lines are parallel.In the diagram: ∠KPS and ∠EDS, yellow, between the blue arms on opposite sides of PD.
Parallel (∥)
Lines that never meet. Matching arrow marks show it. Two lines perpendicular to the same line are also parallel.In the diagram: arms PK and ED, blue with arrows.
Shared angle
One angle used by both triangles. Its vertex and both rays agree.In the diagram: the red arc at A, used by △AEI and △ABT.

Problem type 1

Match the angles from their measures

Decide if the pieces △PHQ and △QHR are similar. Identify which edge of the right piece matches the cut QH of the left piece.

Given H lies between P and R. QH is a cut perpendicular to PR. The outer angle ∠PQR measures 90°, and ∠QPH measures 33°.

  1. Read the givens

    Mark what you know

    m∠PQR = 90°, m∠QPH = 33°

    H lies between P and R. QH is a cut perpendicular to PR, so it makes right angles at H.

  2. First pair

    Right angles at H

    ∠PHQ ≅ ∠QHR

    The perpendicular cut makes both angles at H right angles, so they are congruent.

  3. Left piece

    Find the third angle

    m∠PQH = 180° − 90° − 33° = 57°

    The angles of the left piece add to 180°.

  4. Right piece

    Split the corner at Q

    m∠HQR = 90° − 57° = 33°

    The cut splits the 90° corner at Q. The part in the right piece is 33°, which matches ∠QPH.

  5. Second pair

    Two pairs is AA

    ∠QPH ≅ ∠HQR

    Two distinct angle pairs prove similarity by AA. The third angles must match too.

  6. Write it in order

    Pair the vertices

    △PHQ ∼ △QHR

    P ↔ Q (the 33° angles), H ↔ H (the right angles), and Q ↔ R is left over.

  7. Read the sides

    Find the cut’s partner

    HQ ↔ HR

    The side pairs are PH ↔ QH, HQ ↔ HR and QP ↔ RQ. A side can belong to both triangles and still have a different partner.

Tip: the arrow keys also move through the steps.

The steps

  1. Match the right angles made by the cut.

    ∠PHQ ≅ ∠QHR

    Why?

    The cut is perpendicular to PR, so both angles at H are right angles. Any two right angles are congruent.

  2. Find the missing angle in the left piece.

    m∠PQH = 180° − 90° − 33° = 57°

    Why?

    The angle measures in a triangle add to 180°.

  3. Subtract to find the angle on the other side of the cut.

    m∠HQR = 90° − 57° = 33°

    Why?

    The cut splits the 90° corner at Q into two parts that add to 90°. The 33° part matches ∠QPH in the left piece.

  4. Conclude similarity by AA.

    △PHQ ∼ △QHR

    Why?

    Two distinct corresponding angle pairs are enough. Once two angles match, subtracting them from 180° gives the same third angle in both triangles.

  5. Pair the vertices by their angles, then read off the side pairs.

    P ↔ Q, H ↔ H, Q ↔ R; HQ ↔ HR

    Why?

    The order of the statement is the pairing. The cut HQ is a side of both pieces, but in the right piece its partner is HR.

Try it

A. Choose the match

Compare △VGF with each of △QYD and △UZE. For each comparison, decide whether the triangles are similar. Support the decision with the angle measures. For any similar pair, write an ordered similarity statement beginning with △VGF.

A · Right triangles

In △VGF, m∠V = 180° − 90° − 53° = 37°. So V ↔ Q and G ↔ Y. That leaves F ↔ D, giving △VGF ∼ △QYD by AA. △UZE has angles 40°, 90°, and 50°. Neither acute angle matches either acute angle of △VGF, so these triangles are not similar.

If you paired F with Q because they both had numbers, compare the numbers. The marked 53° and 37° are different. Subtract from 180° before deciding what matches.

Problem type 2

Trace the rays when triangles overlap

Decide if △AEI and △ABT are similar. Picture yourself tracing A → E → I → A, then A → B → T → A.

Given A, E, B lie on the upper ray in that order. A, T, I lie on the lower ray in that order. m∠AEI = m∠ATB = 108°. Solid and dashed edges distinguish the outlines.

  1. Trace

    Trace the first triangle

    A → E → I → A

    Follow the solid outline. A, E, B lie on the upper ray in that order.

  2. Trace

    Trace the second triangle

    A → B → T → A

    Follow the dashed outline. A, T, I lie on the lower ray in that order.

  3. First pair

    The shared angle at A

    ∠EAI ≅ ∠TAB

    At A, both angles use the same two rays. This is a shared angle.

  4. Second pair

    The labeled angles

    ∠AEI ≅ ∠ATB

    Both are marked 108°.

  5. Pair and write

    Follow the marked angles

    △AEI ∼ △ATB

    Pair A with A and E with T, leaving I with B. By AA, the triangles are similar.

  6. Avoid the trap

    Not by which ray

    not △AEI ∼ △ABT

    E and B are both on the upper ray, but the 108° labels pair E with T. So AE and AT are corresponding sides across the rays.

Tip: the arrow keys also move through the steps.

The steps

  1. Trace each triangle separately.

    A → E → I → A, A → B → T → A

    Why?

    Overlapping outlines share parts. Tracing shows which segments and corners belong to which triangle.

  2. Use the shared angle at A.

    ∠EAI ≅ ∠TAB

    Why?

    The two angles have the same vertex and the same two rays, so they are the same angle. An angle is congruent to itself.

  3. Use the labeled angles.

    ∠AEI ≅ ∠ATB

    Why?

    Both measure 108°. Angles with the same measure are congruent.

  4. Pair vertices by the marked angles, not by which ray they sit on.

    A ↔ A, E ↔ T, I ↔ B

    Why?

    Corresponding vertices are the ones with congruent angles. Writing △AEI ∼ △ABT would pair E with B just because both are on the upper ray.

  5. Write the statement in that order.

    △AEI ∼ △ATB

    Why?

    Two angle pairs prove similarity by AA. The order then gives the side pairs, such as AE ↔ AT.

Try it

B. Compare two outlines

Decide whether △CKJ and △CLF are similar. Give angle names and a reason for your decision. If they are similar, write an ordered similarity statement beginning with △CKJ.

B · Overlapping outlines

∠KCJ ≅ ∠FCL because their sides are the same rays. ∠CKJ ≅ ∠CFL because both measure 100°. That gives C ↔ C and K ↔ F, leaving J ↔ L. Thus △CKJ ∼ △CFL by AA. If you matched K with L, follow the marked angles instead of matching the two letters on the same ray.

Problem type 3

Find the angle evidence at a crossing

Decide whether the right-angle boxes and crossed braces prove △PKS is similar to the triangle across S.

Given P–S–D and K–S–E are straight. The dashed continuations of PK and ED meet the same upright at right angles.

  1. Look

    Two triangles at the crossing

    △PKS and △DES

    P–S–D and K–S–E are straight. The triangle across S from △PKS has corners D, E and S.

  2. First pair

    Vertical angles at S

    ∠PSK ≅ ∠DSE

    Each uses the opposite ray of the other at the crossing. They are vertical angles.

  3. Read the boxes

    Right angles outside the triangles

    PK ⊥ upright, ED ⊥ upright

    The dashed continuations of PK and ED meet the same upright at right angles.

  4. Parallel arms

    Both perpendicular to one line

    PK ∥ ED

    Both arms are perpendicular to the same line, so they are parallel. One box by itself would give no such guarantee.

  5. Second pair

    Alternate interior angles

    ∠KPS ≅ ∠EDS

    Trace P–S–D across the parallel arms. These angles lie between the arms on opposite sides of the brace. They are alternate interior angles.

  6. Write it in order

    Pair the vertices

    △PKS ∼ △DES

    P ↔ D and S ↔ S, leaving K ↔ E. By AA, the triangles are similar.

Tip: the arrow keys also move through the steps.

The steps

  1. Find the angle pair at the crossing.

    ∠PSK ≅ ∠DSE

    Why?

    They are vertical angles: opposite angles where two straight lines cross. Vertical angles are always congruent.

  2. Use the right-angle boxes to show the arms are parallel.

    PK ∥ ED

    Why?

    The boxes are outside the triangles. They show both arms are perpendicular to the same upright, and two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel. Arrow marks on the arms would also establish parallelism directly.

  3. Find the alternate interior angles along the brace P–S–D.

    ∠KPS ≅ ∠EDS

    Why?

    The angles lie between the parallel arms and on opposite sides of the brace. Alternate interior angles are congruent when the lines are parallel.

  4. Pair the vertices and write the statement.

    P ↔ D, S ↔ S, K ↔ E: △PKS ∼ △DES

    Why?

    Two angle pairs prove similarity by AA. The leftover vertices K and E must pair with each other.

Try it

C. Choose a triangle at the junction

Which of △KRN or △KUO can you prove similar to △ATK from the marked facts? Identify the two angle pairs and their reasons, then write the similarity statement in matching order starting with △ATK. Explain what the other triangle is missing from the givens.

C · Bars at a junction

∠AKT ≅ ∠NKR as vertical angles. ∠TAK ≅ ∠RNK as alternate interior angles along A–K–N across the two marked parallel bars. A ↔ N, T ↔ R, K ↔ K, so △ATK ∼ △NRK by AA.

The angle at K in △KUO is also vertical to ∠AKT, but there is no marked parallel relationship or second matching angle pair for that triangle. If you chose it because it shares K, write out which rays form its angle at K and then look for separate evidence for a second match.

Problem type 4

Use whole corresponding sides

Find x, the length FD in centimeters.

Given The frames have ∠X ≅ ∠F and right angles at B and D. W is between X and B. XW = 9, WB = 19, BU = 21, and DZ = 12. All lengths are in centimeters.

  1. AA first

    Prove the frames similar

    △XBU ∼ △FDZ

    ∠X ≅ ∠F, and the right angles at B and D match. Two angle pairs give AA.

  2. Pair the sides

    Read the side pairs from the order

    XB ↔ FD, BU ↔ DZ

    X ↔ F and B ↔ D, so XB pairs with FD. B ↔ D and U ↔ Z, so BU pairs with DZ.

  3. Whole side

    Add the pieces

    XB = 9 cm + 19 cm = 28 cm

    W marks a point along XB, so the full corresponding side is XB.

  4. Proportion

    Same triangle on top

    x28 = 1221

    Match frame FDZ over frame XBU in both ratios, using lengths in centimeters.

  5. Solve

    Multiply both sides by 28

    x = 28 × 1221 = 16

    FD = 16 cm. Both corresponding side lengths shrink by the scale factor 12/21.

Tip: the arrow keys also move through the steps.

The steps

  1. Prove the triangles similar and pair the sides.

    △XBU ∼ △FDZ: XB ↔ FD, BU ↔ DZ

    Why?

    ∠X ≅ ∠F and the right angles at B and D give two angle pairs, so AA applies. The statement’s order tells you which sides correspond.

  2. Add the pieces to get the whole side.

    XB = 9 cm + 19 cm = 28 cm

    Why?

    W only marks a point along XB. The pieces XW and WB are not sides of the triangle; the whole segment XB is.

  3. Write a proportion with the same triangle on top in both ratios.

    x28 = 1221

    Why?

    A proportion sets two ratios equal. Keeping frame FDZ on top both times keeps each ratio a comparison of partners.

  4. Multiply both sides by 28.

    x = 28 × 1221

    Why?

    Multiplying by 28 undoes the division by 28 on the left side.

  5. Finish and give the unit.

    x = 16, so FD = 16 cm

    Why?

    Both corresponding side lengths shrink by the same scale factor, 12/21, so the answer stays in centimeters.

Try it

D. Find x along the frame

Find x. Identify the triangles you use and justify their similarity. Write a proportion using corresponding sides, then solve it.

D · Whole sides of a frame

∠GHW and ∠PHR share the same rays. ∠HGW ≅ ∠HPR because the arrows mark GW ∥ PR and the angles are in matching positions along HP. So △HGW ∼ △HPR by AA.

  1. HP = 9 cm + 15 cm = 24 cm.
  2. x24 = 17.530 HG ↔ HP and GW ↔ PR. Each ratio is inner over outer, using centimeters.
  3. x = 24 × 17.530 = 14. HG = 14 cm. It lies between HQ = 9 cm and HP = 24 cm.

If you used QP alone, trace H → P. The outer triangle starts at H, and that full side includes HQ and QP.

Problem type 5

Keep the order when both sides have x

Find x. The statement pairs GJ with QS and CG with LQ. GJ includes both pieces of the split edge.

△CGJ ∼ △LQS

Given CG = 24, LQ = 16, and QS = x + 6. M lies between G and J, with GM = 2x − 3 and MJ = 5. The boxes mark right angles at G and Q. Lengths are in the same unit.

  1. Read the statement

    The order is the pairing

    △CGJ ∼ △LQS

    C ↔ L, G ↔ Q, J ↔ S. The boxes mark right angles at G and Q.

  2. Pair the sides

    Partners from the order

    CG ↔ LQ, GJ ↔ QS

    Map each endpoint on its own.

  3. Whole side

    Combine the split edge

    GJ = (2x − 3) + 5 = 2x + 2

    M lies between G and J, so GJ includes both pieces.

  4. Proportion

    △CGJ over △LQS both times

    2x + 2x + 6 = 2416 = 32

    Write a proportion, an equation that sets two ratios equal.

  5. Solve

    Clear the denominators

    2(2x + 2) = 3(x + 6) → 4x + 4 = 3x + 18 → x = 14

    Since QS is a positive length, x + 6 > 0.

  6. Check

    Lengths must be positive

    GJ = 30, QS = 20, 30/20 = 24/16

    GM = 25 and MJ = 5, so GJ = 30. QS = 20. The ratio matches.

Tip: the arrow keys also move through the steps.

The steps

  1. Use the statement’s order to pair the sides.

    CG ↔ LQ, GJ ↔ QS

    Why?

    Corresponding sides have endpoints in the same positions of the statement: C, G ↔ L, Q and G, J ↔ Q, S.

  2. Write the whole side as one expression.

    GJ = (2x − 3) + 5 = 2x + 2

    Why?

    GJ includes both pieces of the split edge. GM alone is not a side of the triangle.

  3. Write a proportion with △CGJ lengths over △LQS lengths in both ratios.

    2x + 2x + 6 = 2416 = 32

    Why?

    Both ratios must point the same direction, or the equation compares the wrong things.

  4. Clear the denominators, distribute, and solve.

    2(2x + 2) = 3(x + 6); 4x + 4 = 3x + 18; x = 14

    Why?

    Since QS is a positive length, x + 6 > 0, so multiplying by it is safe. Then distribute, and subtract 3x and 4 from both sides.

  5. Check the lengths.

    GM = 25, MJ = 5, GJ = 30, QS = 20

    Why?

    The lengths are positive and 30/20 = 24/16, so the value of x fits the figure.

Try it

E. Find x on the cutouts

△JNF ∼ △KWR

Find x. Use the given order to name the side pairs, write and solve an equation, then check whether both expressions give positive lengths in the correct ratio.

E · Two expressions

The given order pairs NF ↔ WR and JN ↔ KW. Use the second triangle over the first throughout.

  1. 3x + 4x + 8 = 3520 = 74.
  2. 4(3x + 4) = 7(x + 8). Multiply to clear the denominators.
  3. 12x + 16 = 7x + 56. Distribute.
  4. 5x = 40. Subtract 7x and 16 from both sides.
  5. x = 8. Divide by 5.

Then NF = 16 and WR = 28. Both are positive, and 28/16 = 35/20. If your ratios point in different directions, label each numerator with its triangle and use the same triangle on top both times.

Problem type 6

Use one unit before measuring indirectly

Find the long brace’s reach along its beam to the nearest centimeter.

Two straight braces meet their vertical walls at the same angle. Each wall meets its level overhead beam at a right angle.

Given The short brace reaches 70 cm along its beam and 1 m 5 cm down its wall. The long brace reaches 190 cm down its wall. Its reach along its beam is x cm. End-on sketches show the angles and dimensions.

  1. AA first

    Why the sketches are similar

    △HTB ∼ △JLG

    The right angles pair T ↔ L. The brace-to-wall angles pair B ↔ G.

  2. Pair the sides

    Beam with beam, wall with wall

    HT ↔ JL, TB ↔ LG

    So the beam reaches correspond, and the wall reaches correspond.

  3. One unit

    Convert before comparing

    1 m 5 cm = 100 cm + 5 cm = 105 cm

    Every length must be in centimeters before you write a ratio.

  4. Proportion

    Long over short, both times

    x70 = 190105

    All lengths are in centimeters.

  5. Solve

    Round only at the end

    x = 70 × 190105 = 3803 ≈ 127

    The reach is about 127 cm.

  6. Check the model

    What the answer assumes

    The model assumes straight braces, vertical walls, level beams, and the same brace-to-wall angle.

Tip: the arrow keys also move through the steps.

The steps

  1. Show the triangles are similar.

    △HTB ∼ △JLG

    Why?

    The right angles pair T ↔ L and the equal brace-to-wall angles pair B ↔ G. Two angle pairs give AA.

  2. Convert every length to one unit.

    1 m 5 cm = 100 cm + 5 cm = 105 cm

    Why?

    A ratio of 70 to “1 m 5 cm” mixes units, so the numbers would not compare like with like.

  3. Write the proportion, long over short in both ratios.

    x70 = 190105

    Why?

    The beam reaches correspond and the wall reaches correspond. Comparing long to short both times keeps the ratios in the same direction.

  4. Multiply both sides by 70, then round the final result.

    x = 70 × 190105 = 3803; about 127 cm

    Why?

    Keep the exact ratio until this step. Rounding early would carry an error into the answer.

  5. State what the model assumes.

    Why?

    Straight braces, vertical walls, level beams, and the same brace-to-wall angle. If any of these fails, the triangles might not be similar.

Try it

F. Estimate the rise of a conveyor

The belts are straight and sit above the same level slab. An installer slid one rigid template along the slab without turning it and used its sloped edge to align both belts. Each rise is measured vertically from the slab.

Find the rise of the production belt to the nearest inch. Use the setup to explain why the triangles are similar. Show the unit conversions, corresponding side ratios, and rounded rise.

F · Conveyor model

Sliding the template keeps its sloped edge at the same inclination to the slab, so ∠CNP ≅ ∠DUF. Each rise is vertical and each run is level, so ∠NCP and ∠UDF are both 90°. Therefore △NCP ∼ △UDF by AA.

  1. 1 ft 5 in = 12 in + 5 in = 17 in. The production run is 3 ft = 36 in.
  2. x17 = 3624 Compare production over test for both rises and runs, in inches.
  3. x = 17 × 3624 = 25.5.
  4. The rise is about 26 in to the nearest inch. At 25.5 in the result is halfway between 25 and 26, so use 26 under the usual school rounding rule.

If you used 3 directly above 24, the runs are in different units. Multiply the run in feet by 12 to express it in inches before comparing.

Glossary

Vertex
A corner of a figure. In ∠PQR the vertex is Q, the middle letter.
Similar triangles (∼)
Triangles with matching angle measures and a common scale factor between corresponding lengths.
Corresponding
In the same position in the pairing. In △UVW ∼ △GHJ, U pairs with G and W pairs with J, so WU corresponds to JG.
Congruent angles (≅)
Angles with the same measure.
Angle–Angle similarity (AA)
Two triangles are similar if two distinct angles of one triangle are congruent to two distinct corresponding angles of the other.
Shared angle
One angle used by both triangles. Its vertex and both rays agree.
Vertical angles
Opposite angles formed when two straight lines cross. The two angles in a vertical pair are congruent.
Alternate interior angles
Angles inside two lines and on opposite sides of a line crossing them. They are congruent when the two lines are parallel.
Scale factor
The multiplier that takes a corresponding length in one figure to the length in the other. It depends on the direction of comparison.
Proportion
An equation showing that two ratios are equal.
