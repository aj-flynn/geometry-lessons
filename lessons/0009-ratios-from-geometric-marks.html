Words to know

Read the marks first

Every sample starts the same way: read the marks, then decide which ratio they allow. A ratio is a comparison by division, so keep the same order on both sides of a proportion.

Angle bisector
A line or ray dividing an angle into two equal angles. Equal-angle arcs identify it; a right-angle square would identify a perpendicular line instead.In the diagram: ray VM, with one arc on each side of it at V.
Parallel
Lines in the same plane that never meet. The symbol ∥ means parallel; matching arrow marks show it in a drawing.In the diagram: PQ ∥ BC, each with one arrow.
Transversal
A straight line crossing two or more other lines.In the diagram: line AB, crossing PQ at P and BC at B.
Similar triangles
Triangles with matching equal angles and proportional sides. AA means two corresponding angles are equal.In the diagram: shaded △APQ and the whole △ABC share angle A, and the parallel marks make the angles at P and B equal.

Problem type 1 · Equal-angle marks

Split a side with an angle bisector

Use this when equal arcs show that a segment bisects an angle of a triangle and you need the two pieces of the opposite side.

  1. The givens

    Start with the givens

    DE = 9, DF = 15, EF = 16 units. DM bisects angle D. Find EM and MF.

    Read the given lengths and marks. Decide what you need to find before choosing a ratio.

  2. The mark

    Read the mark

    DM splits angle D equally.

    Equal-angle arcs identify an angle bisector. A right-angle square would identify a perpendicular line instead.

  3. The ratio

    Match the endpoints

    EM / MF = DE / DF = 9 / 15 = 3 / 5

    The angle bisector theorem matches each piece of EF with the adjacent side touching the same endpoint: E with E, F with F.

  4. The shares

    Count the shares

    3 + 5 = 8 shares; 16 ÷ 8 = 2 units per share

    The pieces together make the whole side EF. A ratio of 3 to 5 means eight equal-sized shares in total, not two equal pieces.

  5. The pieces

    Find both pieces

    EM = 3 × 2 = 6; MF = 5 × 2 = 10 units

    Give EM three shares and MF five shares. Multiplying the common share size by each count preserves the ratio.

  6. The check

    Check the answer

    6 + 10 = 16 and 6 / 10 = 9 / 15

    Both conditions must work: the pieces add to the given whole and their ratio agrees with the two adjacent sides.

Problem type 2 · Parallel marks

Find a missing piece using parallel lines

Use this when arrow marks show a crossbar parallel to one side of a triangle and one piece of a side is missing.

  1. The givens

    Start with the givens

    PQ ∥ BC. AP = 10, PB = 15, AQ = 14 units. Find QC.

    Read the given lengths and marks. Decide what you need to find before choosing a ratio.

  2. The mark

    Read the mark

    PQ is parallel to BC.

    Parallel lines give equal corresponding angles. Together with the shared angle A, this makes triangles APQ and ABC similar by AA.

  3. Near parts

    Match near parts with near parts

    AQ / AP = 14 / 10 = 1.4

    Compare the right edge to the left edge consistently. These two segments both start at A and end at the first crossbar.

  4. Far parts

    Match far parts with far parts

    QC / PB = 1.4

    Similar triangles give the same multiplier for the whole edges and near pieces. Subtracting the near pieces leaves far pieces with that same multiplier.

  5. The missing piece

    Find the missing piece

    QC = 1.4 × 15 = 21 units

    PB is the far piece on the left. Its matching far piece on the right is QC, so multiply 15 by 1.4.

  6. The check

    Check using the whole edges

    AB = 25, AC = 35; 35 / 25 = 1.4

    Add near and far pieces on each edge. The whole-edge ratio agrees with the near-piece and far-piece ratios.

Problem type 3 · Similar triangles

Find a wider crossbar

Use this when two parallel crossbars make a small and a large similar triangle that share a corner.

  1. The givens

    Start with the givens

    PQ ∥ RS. OP = 8 m, PR = 4 m, PQ = 6 m. How much wider is RS?

    Read the given lengths and marks. Decide what you need to find before choosing a ratio.

  2. Whole side

    Build the whole side

    OR = OP + PR = 8 + 4 = 12 m

    Both triangles start at O. The larger side is OR, not the gap PR. Add the two consecutive pieces to reach R.

  3. Scale factor

    Choose the scale direction

    Large / small = OR / OP = 12 / 8 = 1.5

    The parallel crossbars make triangles OPQ and ORS similar. To grow the small triangle into the large one, use large divided by small.

  4. The width

    Scale the width

    RS = 1.5 × PQ = 1.5 × 6 = 9 m

    Matching widths scale by the same factor as matching sides. A factor larger than one should give a larger width.

  5. The question

    Answer the actual question

    RS − PQ = 9 − 6 = 3 m wider

    Nine meters is the full larger width. The question asks how much wider, so subtract the original width.

  6. The check

    Check the matching ratios

    9 / 6 = 12 / 8 = 1.5

    The crossbar ratio matches the ratio of full distances from O. Using 4 / 8 would incorrectly compare a gap with a whole side.

Problem type 4 · Parallel guides

Scale a whole path from one piece

Use this when parallel guides cut two straight paths (transversals) and you know a piece of one and the pieces of the other.

  1. The givens

    Start with the givens

    JA, KB and LC are parallel. AB = 8 m, BC = 12 m, JK = 10 m. Find JL.

    Read the given lengths and marks. Decide what you need to find before choosing a ratio.

  2. Matching intervals

    Match the intervals

    JK matches AB; JL matches AC.

    Each matching pair starts and ends at the same two parallel guides. JK is one interval; JL is the whole span.

  3. Known whole

    Find the known whole

    AC = AB + BC = 8 + 12 = 20 m

    The whole span from A to C includes both consecutive intervals. Do not omit a gap that lies between the end guides.

  4. Multiplier

    Find the multiplier

    Upper / lower = JK / AB = 10 / 8 = 1.25

    Parallel guides cut proportional intervals on two straight transversals. Keep the upper path above the lower path in every ratio.

  5. Whole span

    Scale the whole span

    JL = 1.25 × AC = 1.25 × 20 = 25 m

    The same multiplier applies to each piece, and therefore to their sum. We can scale the whole known path at once.

  6. The check

    Check the remaining piece

    KL = 1.25 × 12 = 15; 10 + 15 = 25 m

    Scaling the second interval separately gives the same whole. This checks that the matching endpoints and addition were consistent.

Keep the idea

Before you go back to your own work

  • Did you read the mark first: equal arcs, or parallel arrows?
  • Does each ratio match endpoints, near parts with near parts and far parts with far parts?
  • Did you use whole sides from the shared corner, not a gap between two marks?
  • Did you keep the same order (upper over lower, large over small) in every ratio?
  • Did you check both ways: the pieces add to the whole, and the ratios agree?
