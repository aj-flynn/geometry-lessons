Ratios from geometric marks

Use a diagram’s marks to decide which lengths belong in a ratio.

Angle marksParallel marksCrossbar widthsShared pathsMixed practiceGlossary

Before you start

A strip’s marks are W, X, Y in order. WX = 4 cm and WY = 13 cm. Find XY, then write WX/XY. A ratio compares two quantities by division.

Compare your reasoning

XY = 13 cm − 4 cm = 9 cm. WX/XY = 4/9. The denominator is the other piece. Keep WX first when you write the ratio.

Use equal angles to split a side

Worked example · Split a frame edge

Goal. Find both pieces of EF. The paired arcs show DM is an angle bisector, which divides the angle at D into equal angles.

The triangle angle bisector theorem says that the split pieces have the same ratio as the sides meeting at D. Pair EM with DE because both touch E. Pair MF with DF because both touch F.

  1. EM/MF = DE/DF = 9/15 = 3/5

    Pair E with E and F with F.

  2. 16 units ÷ (3 + 5) = 2 units

    The two pieces account for eight equal shares.

  3. EM = 3 × 2 units = 6 units

    EM belongs to the three-share side.

  4. MF = 5 × 2 units = 10 units

    Together the pieces use all 16 units.

Read the same ratio in reverse. With EM = 6 units, MF = 10 units and DF = 15 units, DE/15 = 6/10, so DE = (6/10) × 15 units = 9 units.

Why does the ratio hold?

The right triangles DGM and DJM share hypotenuse DM. They have matching acute angles at D and matching right angles at G and J. Two equal angles and the matching side DM make the triangles congruent, so MG = MJ.

Using DE and DF as the bases, triangles DEM and DFM have equal heights MG and MJ. Their areas are in the ratio DE/DF.

Using EM and MF as the bases, the same two triangles share the perpendicular height from D to line EF. Their areas are also in the ratio EM/MF. Therefore EM/MF = DE/DF.

Try it · Read two marks on one frame

Which mark lets you split UV in a known ratio? Predict which piece made by that line is shorter. Find its length and name the two lengths you compared.

After your prediction, trace either mark to check what it tells you.

Hint 1 · Find the evidence

Which mark is drawn at vertex N? Which is drawn at H?

Hint 2 · Set up a ratio

Subtract the two known sides from the perimeter. Share the remaining side in the order U, V.

Compare your reasoning

Use NS: the arcs mark equal angles at N. The square at H tells you NH is perpendicular to UV. It gives a height.

  1. UV = 52 − 13 − 19 = 20

    All calculations here are in cm. Recover the whole opposite side from the perimeter.

  2. US/SV = NU/NV = 13/19

    Pair the U lengths and the V lengths. US is the shorter piece.

  3. US = (13/32) × 20 = 8.125

    There are 32 shares in all.

If you split 20 into two tens, you used equal lengths where only angles were marked equal. Revise the ratio before using the total.

Use parallel guides to match parts

Worked example · Match parts on two edges

Goal. Find QC. Matching arrows show PQ and BC are parallel. The symbol ∥ means the lines containing those segments are parallel.

Triangle APQ and triangle ABC are similar: they share the angle at A, and parallel lines give equal angles at P and B. In ∆APQ ∼ ∆ABC, the order pairs P with B and Q with C.

  1. AQ/AP = AC/AB

    Both ratios compare the AC edge to the AB edge.

  2. (AC − AQ)/(AB − AP) = AQ/AP

    If two wholes and their near parts grow by the same multiplier, so do their leftover parts.

  3. QC/PB = 14/10

    Match the two far parts, QC and PB.

  4. QC = (14/10) × 15 units = 21 units

    Multiply the matching far part by the common multiplier.

Try it · Read a three-guide layout

Find t and the actual length of MN. Write a proportion, which is an equality between two ratios. Name the diagram marks that make it valid. After checking, try the next layout.

A transversal is a straight line crossing other lines. Several parallel lines make one common multiplier between matching intervals on two transversals. The parallel guides here continue straight beyond the drawing.

Hint 1 · Find the evidence

Which guides share the same arrow mark? Which lower interval begins and ends at the same guides as MN?

Hint 2 · Set up a ratio

Use the first strip over the second strip on both edges. Simplify the ratio of the known lengths before removing the denominator.

Compare your reasoning

The parallel guides are marked. The two straight edges crossing them are the transversals. The successive triangles formed by extending the edges back to their meeting point are similar by equal angles. Subtract matching sides of successive triangles to match each strip, or use the matching whole spans.

  1. (3t − 2)/(4t + 6) = 12/24

    Compare first strip to second strip along each edge.

  2. 2(3t − 2) = 4t + 6

    The right side of the proportion is 1/2.

  3. 6t − 4 = 4t + 6

    Distribute the 2.

  4. 2t = 10

    Collect the variable terms on the left and the numbers on the right.

  5. t = 5

    Divide by 2.

  6. MN = 3(5) − 2 = 13

    The length MN is 13 design units. NO = 26 design units, so 13/26 = 12/24.

If you used 24/12 on the right, you switched the order on only one edge. Start both ratios in the first strip, then check that both resulting lengths are positive.

Retry · Match a different span

Find u and the actual length of VW. Write an equation that links the whole spans to a pair of matching pieces. Check the length you get by substitution.

Hint 1 · Find the evidence

Which lower segment meets the same pair of guides as VW?

Hint 2 · Set up a ratio

Use the lower whole to get BC, then compare upper over lower for both matching spans.

Compare your reasoning

The matching arrows establish parallel guides. The whole spans go from the first guide to the last. First calculate the lower remainder, then match it to the slanted remainder.

  1. BC = 30 − 10 = 20

    All lengths here are in design units.

  2. VW/BC = UW/AC = 37.5/30 = 1.25

    In both ratios the upper span is over its matching lower span.

  3. VW = 1.25 × 20 = 25

    Scale the last strip, which lies between the second and third guides.

  4. 3u + 4 = 25

    Use the marked expression for VW.

  5. u = 7

    Subtract 4, then divide by 3. Substituting gives the positive length 25.

If you multiplied by 10, you scaled the first strip. Trace VW back through both guides to the last lower strip before you revise.

Measure both reaches from the vertex

Worked example · Compare crossbars inside one triangle

Goal. How much wider is RS than PQ? The parallel marks give ∆OPQ ∼ ∆ORS. A scale factor is the multiplier from one length to its matching length.

  1. OR = 8 m + 4 m = 12 m

    For the larger triangle, measure from shared vertex O all the way to R.

  2. RS/PQ = OR/OP = 12/8

    The crossbar widths match each other. The two distances from O match each other.

  3. RS = (12/8) × 6 m = 9 m

    Apply the same multiplier to the known width.

  4. RS − PQ = 9 m − 6 m = 3 m

    The question asks for the increase.

PR is the gap along the edge. It is useful for building OR. It is not a full side of ∆ORS.

Match the full spans before scaling

Worked example · Find a full path from part of it

Goal. Find JL. Each pair of guides cuts off one matching interval on each conduit. The same multiplier applies to the whole span because its pieces all scale by that multiplier.

  1. AC = 8 m + 12 m = 20 m

    Find the whole span of the known conduit.

  2. JL/AC = JK/AB = 10/8

    Both ratios run slanted over lower; each pair has the same end guides.

  3. JL = (10/8) × 20 m = 25 m

    Scale the full lower span.

When the full slanted span is given instead, multiply it by each matching share of the lower span. Include all intervals inside the two end guides when you find the whole.

Mixed practice

For each sketch, decide what the marks guarantee. Write the matching lengths before you calculate. Try the three sketches with hints and comparisons closed.

Sketch A · Three crossbars

Find PU and RW exactly. Name the diagram cue and write a ratio for each width.

Hint 1 · Find the evidence

What does each square tell you about its crossbar and the dashed line? Which given spans overlap?

Hint 2 · Set up a ratio

Turn PR into the sum PQ + QR. Compare the full reach of each target triangle to the full reach of the triangle with width QV.

Compare your reasoning

The three squares mark 90° where each crossbar meets the same line. The crossbars are parallel, so the triangles from G are similar by AA. Trace from vertex G to each crossbar. GP = 6 mi. PR contains PQ and QR, so PQ = 11 mi − 5 mi = 6 mi. GQ = 12 mi and GR = 17 mi.

  1. PU/QV = GP/GQ = 6/12

    Use one parallel crossbar for each triangle.

  2. PU = (6/12) × 8 mi = 4 mi

    Scale from the middle to the inner width.

  3. RW/QV = GR/GQ = 17/12

    Use the full reach to the far crossbar.

  4. RW = (17/12) × 8 mi = 34/3 mi

    The last width is larger than the middle width.

If you added both PR and QR, you counted QR twice. Mark the endpoints of PR before you rewrite it as pieces.

Sketch B · A display with an access gap

Find the upper display lengths JK, LM, and MN to the nearest tenth of a meter. Explain how the access gap affects the whole you use.

Hint 1 · Find the evidence

Which two guides bound the whole on each side? Which guides bound the last display?

Hint 2 · Set up a ratio

Find DE by subtracting the known intervals from AE. Use the exact whole-to-whole multiplier before rounding.

Compare your reasoning

The matched arrows make the corresponding intervals scale together. The lower full span is 66 m. Its DE interval is 66 m − (12 m + 11 m + 17 m) = 26 m. Use the exact multiplier 71/66.

  1. JK = (71/66) × 12 m ≈ 12.9 m

    Match AB with JK.

  2. LM = (71/66) × 17 m ≈ 18.3 m

    Match CD with LM.

  3. MN = (71/66) × 26 m ≈ 28.0 m

    Match DE with MN.

If you used all 71 m for the three display sections, you left out the part across the access gap. Its exact length is (71/66) × 11 m. Check the exact spans together before rounding each display section.

Mixed practice continued

Sketch C · Off center

Find FL. Explain how you recover the two pieces of LR and how the diagram lets you use those pieces to find FL.

Hint 1 · Find the evidence

Which marked point gives equal distances to L and R? Where is W relative to that point?

Hint 2 · Set up a ratio

Find LW and WR first. Match each one to the adjacent side that touches the same endpoint.

Compare your reasoning

The paired arcs on either side of FW mark equal angles at F. First reconstruct the two pieces of LR from its midpoint X.

  1. LX = XR = 25.5 cm ÷ 2 = 12.75 cm

    Each half of LR has the same length.

  2. LW = 12.75 cm − 2.25 cm = 10.5 cm

    W lies on the L side of X.

  3. WR = 12.75 cm + 2.25 cm = 15 cm

    Go from W to X, then X to R.

  4. FL/FR = LW/WR = 10.5/15 = 7/10

    Use the angle bisector theorem and match L with L and R with R.

  5. FL = (7/10) × 20 cm = 14 cm

    The shorter piece of LR pairs with the shorter side meeting at F.

If you treated W as the middle, you would conclude that FL and FR are equal. Mark X first, then follow the two distances from W to the endpoints.

When you come back

Return to another diagram by naming the mark, pairing matching endpoints, and checking whether each number is a part, a gap, or a whole. Then check the size and units of your result.

On a later day. A and E lie on the same ray from V, in the order V, A, E. B and F lie on a different ray from V. Crossbars AB and EF are both perpendicular to a third line GH. VA = 7 units and AE = 5 units. Decide whether AB/EF is determined. If it is, write the ratio and explain the cue you used.

Compare your reasoning

Yes. AB and EF are perpendicular to the same line GH, so AB ∥ EF. Triangle VAB is similar to triangle VEF by the shared apex angle and the angles made by the parallel crossbars. The width ratio AB/EF equals VA/VE = 7/12, because VE = 7 + 5 = 12 units.

If you used 7/5, you compared a full side of one triangle to the gap between the bars. Start each side at V.

If you want a fresh hint, describe the step that stopped you in your private chat.

Glossary

Ratio
A comparison by division, written such as 3/5. Order matters.
Angle bisector
A ray that divides an angle into two equal angles. Here it meets the opposite side of the triangle.
Parallel
Lines in the same plane that keep a constant distance apart and never meet. Symbol ∥.
Similar triangles
Matching angles are equal and matching sides share a multiplier. Two pairs of equal angles are sufficient (AA). The symbol ∼ means “is similar to.”
Proportion
A statement that two ratios are equal.
Transversal
A line crossing two or more other lines at distinct points.
Scale factor
The new length divided by the matching original length. Multiply other corresponding lengths by this factor.
