Four checks, in order

1. What particles and how many?
Separate the recipe within one particle from the number of copies. Multiply each per-particle count by the coefficient. An ionic formula describes an ion ratio in a crystal. Formula lesson and glossary.
2. Where are the valence electrons?
Find neutral-atom valence counts from a suitable periodic-table convention, add the budget, and adjust for charge. Propose or obtain the connection skeleton, place pairs, and check local shells. Count each bond line or lone pair as two electrons. Check the total once; check each atom’s shell separately. If a local shell is short, inspect whether existing pairs can be rearranged without changing the total. A flat Lewis drawing does not establish shape. The linked lesson shows a full new two-carbon construction. Electron lesson and glossary.
3. Do the polarity effects combine?
Use the absolute electronegativity difference (including zero) for a bond, with your course’s approximate convention. Obtain geometry independently, then combine bond effects in space. Equal opposite effects cancel; unequal effects do not. Polarity lesson and glossary.
4. What separates during a change?
Distinguish internal bonds from attractions between molecules. Compare total attractions under stated conditions; consider particle size, shape, and pressure. Do not rank substances from a single force label. Attraction lesson and glossary.

Check your reasoning

What evidence did I use? Which relationship connects it to my claim? What did I assume? Would changing one assumption change my conclusion?
