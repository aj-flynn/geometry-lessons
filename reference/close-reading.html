Read dialogue closely

A quick reference. Print using your browser's Print command.

The reading routine

  1. Context: who speaks, to whom, and in what situation? Read the surrounding lines.
  2. Plain meaning: restate what the speaker means without adding a motive or judgment.
  3. Language choice: identify exact words, name a device when useful, and explain its effect.
  4. Supported inference: make a claim, cite a detail, and explain why it supports the claim.
  5. Check: consider another possible reading. Keep uncertainty and limits visible.

Essential terms

Literal meaning
The ordinary, nonfigurative sense of words.
Paraphrase
A restatement of the intended meaning in your own words. A figurative statement needs its image unpacked.
Interpretation
An explanation of what words or actions might suggest, supported by evidence.
Evidence → reasoning
A specific detail → an explanation of how it supports your claim.
Trait / feeling
A behavioral tendency / a temporary emotion. One moment cannot establish “always.”
Metaphor
Describing one thing as another to create a comparison.
Simile
An explicit comparison, often using like or as. Not every use of those words is a comparison.
Personification
Giving a nonhuman thing human behavior or qualities.
Hyperbole
Deliberate exaggeration for emphasis.
Tone
An attitude conveyed by wording and delivery.

Untangle unfamiliar wording

Follow the complete sentence, not just a printed line. Find the subject and main verb. Reconnect separated phrases. Preserve negatives and conditions. Check an edition's gloss or a historical dictionary, then test the meaning in context. Separate what the sentence says from what you infer.

Before you finish

Does my paraphrase add anything? Have I explained the effect of the wording? Does my evidence support this exact claim? What does the passage leave uncertain?
