Matching lengths from geometric marks

Use this page to match the endpoints before writing a proportion.

Equal angles at a vertex

When an interior angle bisector meets the opposite side of a triangle, the two pieces of that side are in the same ratio as the two sides of the angle. Pair lengths by the endpoint they share. For a total, add the ratio shares. For a difference, subtract them.

Parallel cuts across a triangle

A crossbar parallel to a side of the triangle forms a smaller similar triangle. The two triangles share the vertex angle and have equal corresponding angles along the parallel cuts. Compare lengths measured from the shared vertex when you compare whole triangle sides.

Two cuts perpendicular to the same line in a plane are parallel. A gap between two cuts can help you build a complete side by addition or subtraction.

Parallel guides across two straight paths

When two straight paths cross the same set of parallel guides, intervals between the same pair of guides are proportional. For an allocation, match both ends of each whole span, including any gaps between those ends. Keep the common multiplier exact until the final rounding step.
